Virginia Question 2: Marriage Amendment

State of Virginia
RESOLUTE Citizen recommendation

Vote NO on Question 2

This is not merely housekeeping. Question 2 would remove Virginia's one-man, one-woman marriage provision and write a new constitutional rule requiring the Commonwealth to recognize marriage regardless of sex or gender.

What the ballot actually asks

“Should the Constitution of Virginia be amended to (i) remove the ban on same-sex marriage; (ii) affirm that two adults may marry regardless of sex, gender, or race; and (iii) require all legally valid marriages to be treated equally under the law?”

The existing 2006 provision is currently unenforceable under federal court decisions, including Obergefell v. Hodges. Question 2 goes further than deleting dormant language: it would replace that provision with an affirmative constitutional right and a state-recognition command.

What would change

  • The one-man, one-woman definition approved by Virginia voters in 2006 would be deleted.
  • Virginia would be required to issue marriage licenses to two adults regardless of sex, gender, or race.
  • Virginia and its political subdivisions would be required to recognize every lawful marriage between two adults and treat those marriages equally under law.
  • The amendment itself contains no express religious-liberty, church, or conscience exemption.

What the “Yes” mailer says — and what it leaves out

Claim: “Voting Yes protects Virginia families.”

The mailer uses the language and imagery of family while omitting that the amendment removes the sexed definition of marriage and separately constitutionalizes “gender” as a basis on which the state may not deny a license.

Claim: “Keep government out of personal decisions.”

The text does not remove government from marriage. It directs the Commonwealth and every political subdivision to issue licenses, recognize qualifying unions, and treat them equally under state law.

Claim: “It doesn't change what marriage means to your church.”

That is the Yes campaign's assurance, not language found in the amendment. Existing federal and state protections remain separate legal questions; Question 2 adds no explicit protection for churches, religious organizations, clergy, or conscience.

Who is paying for the “Yes” campaign?

The mailer identifies Virginians for Marriage Equality as its sponsor. VPM reported that the committee's early support was led by in-kind contributions from the ACLU of Virginia. VPAP's current $10,000-disclosure page lists the following major contributions:

  • $100,000 — Reginald James Brown, cash contribution (September 4, 2026)
  • $92,590 — New Virginia Majority, in-kind contribution (October 1, 2026)
  • $90,000 — Centerline Liberties Inc., cash contribution (September 25, 2026)
  • $25,000 — GLBTQ Legal Advocates & Defenders, cash contribution (October 6, 2026)
  • $10,000 — Human Rights Campaign, cash contribution (October 2, 2026)

Campaign-finance figures are a dated snapshot of public filings and may change. Verified October 15, 2026 through VPAP's committee disclosures.

RESOLUTE Citizen Analysis

Vote NO. Marriage is not merely a private contract between interchangeable adults. It is the public institution that joins man and woman and gives children a mother-and-father norm. Question 2 would replace that truth in Virginia's foundational law with a definition detached from sexual difference.

The mailer's central tactic is a moral inversion: it borrows the words family, freedom, and responsibility while concealing the amendment's treatment of sex and gender. Citizens should answer the argument firmly without degrading the people pictured in the advertisement. People bear God's image; false claims still deserve direct exposure.
