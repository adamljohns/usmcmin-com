Module 6 · The Husband Course

Module 6 of 7 · 45–60 minutes, plus one no-screen connection block

Guard Friendship and Intimacy

What deliberate attention does our friendship and intimacy need?

Opening

Why this matters now

Marital intimacy is larger than sex. It includes friendship, safety, affection, delight, knowledge, privacy, and embodied closeness. Sexual intimacy matters, but it cannot be isolated from the rest of married life.

Within the one-flesh covenant, sexual self-giving is mutual and never coerced. First Corinthians 7:3–5 speaks of mutual duty and temporary abstinence by shared agreement — not permission to force, threaten, punish, or demand what harms.

This week, invite one no-screen connection block of at least thirty minutes. Offer two realistic options, make refusal safe, and state clearly that there is no expectation of sex or forced disclosure.

Scripture anchor

Ground your work in Scripture

Read each passage in its wider context before applying it. Check each one as you read it.

This week

Your tasks

Work through these in order. Each task has an observable finish line.

Task 1

Protect unclaimed attention

Friendship requires presence, not performance.

Actions

  1. Schedule one no-screen connection block of at least thirty minutes.
  2. Offer two realistic options: walk, coffee, shared music, porch conversation, or quiet affection.
  3. State plainly that there is no expectation of sex or forced disclosure.
  4. Put devices away and protect the time you agreed on.

Task 2

Honor consent within covenant

Actions

  1. Accept that either spouse may decline any sexual or affectionate activity at any time.
  2. Offer kind words, practical partnership, and nonsexual touch as goods in themselves — not deposits toward access.
  3. Never criticize or compare your wife's body. Protect her privacy.
  4. After she states a boundary, keep ordinary warmth intact. Sulking and coldness punish a "no" without speaking a threat.

Task 3

Address structural pressure

Actions

  1. Ask about rest, pain, medication, privacy, unresolved hurt, body image, and caregiving load.
  2. Share household and caregiving responsibility as justice and love, not seduction strategy.
  3. Treat different desire patterns as shared information — not moral rank.
  4. Seek medical evaluation for pain; seek trauma-informed care where trauma is present.

Task 4

Guard fidelity and friendship

Actions

  1. Notice when secrecy, fantasy, messaging, pornography, or outside emotional disclosure draws energy from covenant truthfulness.
  2. Remember what makes your wife laugh, what drains her, and what she hopes for beyond household function.
  3. Speak about intimacy away from the bedroom and outside the immediate sting of rejection.
  4. Ask one question at a time and make opting out ordinary.

Private self-check

Reflect alone, then check each one off. Do not use these to diagnose or score your wife.

Required field action

Complete one pressure-free no-screen connection block

Observable finish line: You shared at least thirty minutes of mutually chosen, no-screen connection while respecting every physical and conversational boundary.

Optional conversation guide

Ask away from the bedroom. Your wife may decline any question or stop at any point.

Safety for this module

Do not use Scripture, marriage vows, headship, disappointment, money, affection, or course participation to demand sexual access or disclosure. Trauma, sexual pain, medical concerns, coercion, addiction, affairs, and major betrayal require specialized individual care; joint intimacy exercises may be unsafe or premature.
