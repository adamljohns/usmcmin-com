Future amphibious ship Harrisburg finishes builder's trials; acceptance trials still ahead

Ingalls Shipbuilding said Tuesday that the future USS Harrisburg finished multi-day builder's trials on Saturday, USNI News reports. The yard tested propulsion, steering, communications, and the ship-control, mission, and combat systems. Delivery to the Navy is planned later this year, after acceptance trials. Harrisburg is the first Flight II San Antonio-class amphibious transport dock. Pittsburgh and Philadelphia are also at the Pascagoula yard.

Royal Navy shadowed Russian warships in three operations over two weeks

Destroyer HMS Dauntless and patrol vessel HMS Tyne tracked frigate Neustrashimy from Oct. 1 to 14 as it escorted sanctioned cargo ships through the English Channel, USNI News reports. Tyne also watched amphibious ship Aleksandr Shabalin escort a cargo ship and a tug toward the Baltic from Oct. 11 to 14. Merlin helicopters tracked surveillance ship Viktor Leonov in the Outer Hebrides from Sept. 29 to Oct. 8. The Royal Air Force said its Protector drone flew a first operational sortie in U.K. airspace, about 10 hours watching Russian naval activity. The announcement did not say which day that flight took place.

Jimmy Lai has spent more than 2,200 days in solitary and still calls the cell a sanctuary, an essay says

George Weigel writes in First Things that Lai, 78 and diabetic, was falsely convicted of threatening national security and gets 50 minutes outside the cell each day. Weigel quotes Lai calling prison a holy sanctuary and a joy for this life and the next. Lai stayed in Hong Kong, where he holds British citizenship, rather than leave. Weigel says efforts by the Trump administration to secure his release have not freed him, and that he could die in prison. The essay also asks prayer for his wife, Teresa, and their children.

A review says Britain's unwritten constitution is a habit of flexibility

Daniel Johnson writes in Law & Liberty that Americans often treat the United Kingdom as a country without a constitution, or with only an unwritten one. He notes that Bagehot and Dicey last tried to describe those rules in the Victorian era, and that the Cabinet Manual has no statutory force. England's 1653 Instrument of Government lasted four years. A written frame, he adds, does not by itself guarantee stability. The review is of Max Skjönsberg's book on parliamentary reform.
