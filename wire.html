Four dozen U.S. Marines with Force Reconnaissance Company, 2d Reconnaissance Battalion, deployed to Finland this week, conducting boat operations off the country's southern coast as part of NATO's Baltic Sentry mission to protect undersea infrastructure in the Baltic Sea

USNI News reports the Marines, forward-deployed with Marine Rotational Force-Europe, were re-tasked to provide maritime domain awareness alongside the Finnish Coastal Brigade during the Finnish Navy's Narrow Waters 26-2 exercise, which runs this week in the coastal areas of southern Finland and the Gulf of Finland. Baltic Sentry is a European-led NATO activity stood up to protect critical undersea cables and pipelines after a string of suspected sabotage incidents, with the Marine Corps serving in a specialized, enabling role alongside the alliance's own naval and coast guard assets.

Four years after the Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade, Heritage Foundation researchers find pro-life policymakers used the first session of the new Congress and the Trump administration to keep advancing life in law, policy and culture rather than treating the ruling as a finish line

The report, published as Congress opens its first session following last year's midterms, catalogs pro-life policy actions taken since the 2022 ruling removed abortion from constitutional protection, framing continued legislative and administrative work — not the Dobbs decision itself — as the real measure of the movement's progress. Heritage credits state and federal lawmakers with sustaining momentum on life policy even as cultural and legal fights continue four years on from the reversal.

A wave of Democratic Senate and House wins this month featuring ordained ministers and a seminarian — including Texas's first Democratic senator since the 1980s and Kansas's first in more than 85 years — has commentators declaring the arrival of a 'religious left' to counter the religious right, but First Things argues the label obscures what actually happened

The piece notes that Senator-elect James Talarico, a Presbyterian seminarian, Senator-elect Adam Hamilton, a Methodist megachurch pastor, and House flips by ELCA pastor Sarah Trone Garriott and PCUSA minister Lindsay James in Iowa all cited Christ's call to care for the poor as their motivation — but argues what reporters are calling a 'religious left' is really the long-declining white mainline Protestant left reasserting itself, the same tradition behind the Social Gospel movement a century ago. Despite once being the country's largest, wealthiest and most culturally powerful religious institutions, First Things notes mainline denominations have been shedding members for decades, making this month's wins less a durable new coalition than a last visible flare from a shrinking tradition.

First Things

Even where the church holds no political power and is a tiny minority, it still has the duty to address the governing authorities with the word of God, a new American Reformer essay argues, rejecting the idea that Christian political engagement is optional for a faithful church

Drawing on missionary theologian Lesslie Newbigin's observation that secularism's claim to protect belief and unbelief alike actually fosters only a false neutrality, Pittsburgh pastor Nathanael Devlin argues the modern church has too often accepted that same secularist premise — treating silence toward 'the nations' as prudent restraint rather than a failure of duty. The essay, part of American Reformer's ongoing case for confessional political engagement, frames speaking to civil authorities as a biblical obligation that does not depend on the church having cultural power to back it up.

Tech / AI

China's fourth aircraft carrier, a still-unnamed vessel known as Type 004, is taking shape at a Dalian shipyard and will be bigger than any of Beijing's three existing carriers and nuclear-powered, with a 2027 launch and a five-to-ten-year path to deployment, defense analysts tell Defense News

As of October the hull measured about 322 meters bow to stern and 46 meters wide, which the Center for Strategic and International Studies says makes it 'far larger than any other class of surface combatant' in China's navy and slightly bigger than the Fujian, currently Beijing's largest carrier, according to Nanyang Technological University's Collin Koh. Nuclear propulsion would give the ship 'essentially unlimited endurance' and faster, no-compromise aircraft launches without the tanker support a conventional carrier needs, Koh said, while Taipei-based analyst Alexander Huang called the specs those of a 'supercarrier' in electronics, tonnage and aircraft capacity. Koh said the capability 'seems to signal a certain strategic objective' beyond coastal defense — 'it's not just going to move along your coast ... it seems to signal a certain global ambition.'

The United States pledged a $2 billion foreign military financing loan package to help Manila modernize its armed forces and develop an airport in the Luzon Strait, one of several defense deals signed last week during President Trump's visit to the Philippines

USNI News reports the targeted defense-assistance package was inked alongside several other agreements during the president's trip, aimed at strengthening the Philippine military's modernization push and expanding infrastructure in the Luzon Strait — the contested waterway between the Philippines and Taiwan that has grown in strategic weight as Beijing presses its own claims in the South China Sea.

Culture

November is a 'memento mori month' threaded with memory, grief and gratitude — All Saints and All Souls Day, Veterans Day, and Thanksgiving itself — and a new Public Discourse column finds that same thread running through the books left behind by the writer's own dead

The columnist, rearranging bookshelves to make room for new cases, describes finding his late mother's first editions of novelist Muriel Spark alongside other volumes that belonged to his father and late wife, treating each inherited book as both a physical object and a 'repository' of its departed owner's own reading life — their pleasures in a tale well told, the things they saw and marked and remembered. The essay frames November's cluster of commemorations — death, purgation, remembrance and grateful prayer — as a fitting backdrop for Thanksgiving itself, arguing that gratitude and memory of the dead belong together rather than being treated as separate, unrelated observances.

“The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping,” the second prequel to the dystopian franchise, fills in young Haymitch Abernathy's backstory 24 years before the original films with a new cast, and WORLD's review finds it a competent but familiar addition that mostly recycles the series' well-worn beats

WORLD's critic says newcomer Joseph Zada carries the role of a teenage Haymitch convincingly despite an uneven Southern accent, with returning franchise veterans Ralph Fiennes taking over as the venomous President Snow and Jesse Plemons stepping into the Capitol-insider role originated by the late Philip Seymour Hoffman. Set on Haymitch's 16th birthday — reaping day, when the authoritarian Capitol selects teenagers from the districts for its annual televised bloodsport — the film follows him from reluctant tribute to recruit in a conspiracy to end the games, a story most Hunger Games fans will likely find satisfying even if it breaks little new ground.
