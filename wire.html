Cole's crew marked 26 years since the Aden bombing at the ship's memorial in Norfolk

The crew of USS Cole held a remembrance today at the USS Cole Memorial on Naval Station Norfolk. The Navy said the ceremony included a wreath-laying, a reading of the names of the 17 sailors killed, a ceremonial bell, a three-volley rifle salute, and Taps. On Oct. 12, 2000, while the destroyer was refueling in Aden, Yemen, a small boat detonated against the port side at 11:18 a.m. local time, ripping a 40-by-60-foot hole near the waterline. Seventeen sailors were killed and nearly 40 were injured. Commanding officer Cmdr. Chris Gostel said the ship's legacy is part of everything the crew does. Cole remains an active warship.

Law & Liberty says Madison's limit on power starts with human nature, not procedure

Monday's lecture, revised from an April talk at Hillsdale's Washington campus, argues that James Madison treated government as a reflection on human nature. It quotes Federalist 51 and Madison's 1816 annual message, which tied the public good to the written Constitution and to the moral principles allied to it. The writer pushes back on readings that treat Madison as a pluralist whose order is only checks, balances, and bargaining. It is an argument about founding principles, not a bill or a floor vote.

Public Discourse looks at a 539 decree that regulated who could enter Constantinople

Monday's essay asks how far a country may regulate migration and return people already inside its borders. The writer uses Novel 80, a decree Justinian issued in 539 that regulated migration to Constantinople and set up an office to enforce it. He argues the Catholic tradition has a basis both for enforcement and for protecting the most vulnerable. It is a historical argument, not a statute or an agency order.

Tech / AI

The Army inducted its first robotics warrant officers for drone work

Defense News reported Monday that the Army formally introduced 10 warrant officers as 390A robotics technicians at the John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School. They are meant to program, build, repair, and integrate robotic and autonomous systems, and to advise commanders at brigade and group level. Candidates complete a 19-week course in which they build drones that do not need GPS or radio to navigate. Chief Warrant Officer 5 Jerry Brown said the skill cannot be one more hat for someone already on an operational detachment. Vice Chief of Staff Gen. Christopher LaNeve said the Army is scaling the specialty. This is an occupational specialty standing up, not a fielded drone fleet.

Defense News

Lockheed showed a longer PAC-3 interceptor at the Army's annual meeting

Defense News reported Monday that Lockheed Martin debuted the PAC-3 Edge at the Association of the United States Army meeting in Washington. Jason Reynolds, who runs the company's integrated air and missile defense business, said the missile can launch from the M903, from containerized launchers, and from a HIMARS configured for offense and defense. It is longer and wider than the PAC-3 MSE, adds attitude-control motors, and uses a three-pulse motor from Northrop Grumman. Lockheed said testing is planned for 2027. The Army timeline Reynolds cited is 2031, and the company wants it sooner. This is a debut, not a fielded round.

A Ranger squad took the Army's Best Squad title, and two of its own took the individual awards

The Army announced the winners today at the Association of the United States Army meeting in Washington. Sgt. Maj. of the Army Michael R. Weimer gave all three honors to a five-soldier squad from the 75th Ranger Regiment, representing U.S. Army Special Operations Command. Cpl. Michael Shield was named Soldier of the Year and Staff Sgt. Colin Doyle was named NCO of the Year. The events ran at Fort Benning from Sept. 11 to 19 and included a board, fitness tests, weapons lanes, an 18.6-mile night march, and a water-survival course. Doyle said the regiment's daily work is marksmanship, small-unit tactics, medical training, and mobility.

The Big Red One took eastern Kosovo from the Florida Guard today

Kosovo Force Regional Command-East transferred authority today at Camp Bondsteel from the 53rd Infantry Brigade Combat Team, Florida Army National Guard, to the 1st Infantry Division. Col. Sam Sargeant handed the command to Col. Jabari Miller. The release says the division helped stand up the NATO mission in 1999 and is back more than two decades later. During nine months, the outgoing task force ran more than 4,500 multinational battle-group patrols, 9,000 patrols in northern Kosovo, and about 1,900 flight hours. KFOR commander Maj. Gen. Özkan Ulutaş said the outgoing rotation led about 1,400 people from 12 nations.
