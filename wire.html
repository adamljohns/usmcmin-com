Defense News

Destroyer USS Spruance passes 342 days at sea, the longest Navy destroyer deployment since World War II

Defense News, Oct. 29: the Arleigh Burke-class destroyer left Naval Base San Diego on Nov. 21, 2025, with the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier strike group and stayed at sea after the carrier went home. As of Oct. 29 it had been deployed 342 days, far past the seven-month benchmark for U.S.-based ships. A crew spouse told Military Times families keep hearing the ship is due home "next month." The destroyers John Finn, Mustin, and Rafael Peralta have also had return dates pushed into 2027. Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Daryl Caudle said Oct. 26 that bringing these destroyers back into maintenance will take hundreds of millions of dollars and called it "a years-long recovery." GAO has reported private yards ran nearly 10,000 days late, cumulatively, on destroyer repairs from fiscal 2015 to 2022.

Political

RAND warns a rushed U.S. pullout from Europe could push allies off American weapons

Defense News, Oct. 29: a RAND study released Wednesday, "Anticipating Allies' Responses to U.S. Retrenchment from Europe," looks at how France, Germany, Poland, and the U.K. would react to deeper U.S. cuts short of leaving NATO. It drew on 51 interviews with officials and experts. The four allies told RAND they could absorb a coordinated, orderly drawdown but see a rapid one as opening capability gaps and possibly emboldening Russia. Over the long run, the authors wrote, broken trust would steer allies toward European or homegrown arms, hurting the U.S. defense industrial base. RAND recommends larger cuts on a long timeline worked out with allies. The Pentagon announced in May it would pull about 5,000 troops from Germany.

Tech / AI

General Dynamics unveils Vion, a suitcase-sized AI radio hub meant to link allied networks

Defense News, Oct. 29: General Dynamics Information Technology revealed Vion, a portable communications kit that lets ground troops pass messages and files and set up secure links to a command center and coalition forces from remote sites. GDIT's Jason Myers said it uses open architecture and AI to connect outside networks and translate text, voice memos, and video under zero-trust protections. A Defense Information Systems Agency acquisition official pointed to the March incident in which Kuwaiti air defenses shot down three U.S. F-15Es as the reason networks must stop working in silos. Myers said GDIT is in the acquisition process but would not say with whom.

blocking data centers at home could send Americans' data offshore

Heritage Foundation commentary, Oct. 28: if AI companies are boxed out of every U.S. jurisdiction, they may have to build their infrastructure overseas. The piece grants that would settle not-in-my-backyard fights and keep data centers from pushing up utility rates, then argues it creates other risks for Americans' data abroad.

Culture

CNO tosses the coin as Navy and Notre Dame meet for the 99th time, first at Gillette Stadium

Navy release, Oct. 31: Adm. Daryl Caudle took part in the ceremonial coin toss at Gillette Stadium in Foxborough, Mass., for the 99th Navy-Notre Dame game. The schools have met nearly every year since Oct. 15, 1927, and played 93 straight seasons before COVID-19 broke the run in 2020. During World War II the Navy ran a V-12 officer program at Notre Dame that kept the university going when enrollment fell. "The midshipmen on this field are preparing to lead America's Sailors and Marines," Caudle said.

DVIDS

Law & Liberty: Washington Irving gave a young republic its ghosts

Law & Liberty, Friday: the essay calls Irving the founder of American literature and reads "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" (1820) as an early statement of American identity. Set in 1790 in the Hudson Valley, where Tory and Patriot neighbors fought some of the war's bitterest battles, the story is haunted by the Revolution. Federalist Fisher Ames doubted in 1804 that the republic would ever produce a great literature. Irving shared some of that worry about American vulgarity but answered it by looking to the country's rivers, mountains, and history for material.

American Reformer takes up the PCA report on Christian nationalism and ethnicity

American Reformer, Oct. 28: Danny Bieber writes that the meaning of ethnicity has become a flashpoint in Reformed denominations. He opens on the 2026 Partial Report of the PCA's Ad-Interim Committee on Christian Nationalism, sent to the 53rd General Assembly and PCA churches, which treats ethnicity as a significant issue in the revival of Protestant political theology and cites Galatians 3:28.
