Tahoma's crew offloaded 3,622 pounds of cocaine at Port Everglades on Wednesday

A Coast Guard still posted Thursday shows cutter Tahoma's crew handling mooring lines at Port Everglades, Florida, on Nov. 4. The caption says the crew offloaded about 3,622 pounds of cocaine after two interdictions in the Caribbean Sea. A DVIDS video of the same offload values the narcotics at about $27 million and identifies Tahoma as a 270-foot medium endurance cutter homeported at Naval Station Newport, Rhode Island. The frame is the ship and the pier, not a press photo.

Marines named Lance Cpl. Devin Hernandez, 25, killed in fast-rope training at Camp Schwab

USNI News, Thursday: the 3rd Marine Division said Hernandez died this week after he was injured during fast-rope training at Camp Schwab in Okinawa. He was taken for medical treatment and later died of those injuries. He was 25. A later USNI line says he was a Maryland native and served as a 1371 combat engineer with 2nd Battalion, 6th Marines, 2nd Marine Division.

USS Stout lost power in Augusta Bay on Oct. 25 after an engineering casualty

USNI News, Thursday: destroyer USS Stout (DDG-55) was in port in Augusta Bay, Italy, for a scheduled visit on Oct. 25 when an engineering casualty knocked out power, Cmdr. Lauren Spaziano of U.S. 6th Fleet told the paper. The ship is on a scheduled 6th Fleet deployment. USNI said it understands the casualty happened during that port visit.

Germany named Army Chief Freuding as chief of defense in February 2027

Defense News, Thursday, from the German defense ministry: Lt. Gen. Christian Freuding will succeed Carsten Breuer, who was chosen to head NATO's military committee. Freuding became army chief in October 2025 after years overseeing Berlin's arms supplies to Kyiv, and he started a drone regiment. Defense Minister Boris Pistorius said Freuding keeps a cool head and acts quickly when the job changes.

Tech / AI

IWI says Thailand is fielding a rifle trigger built to shoot down small drones

Defense News, Thursday: Israel Weapon Industries said Thailand's Ministry of Defence began fielding the Arbel Anti-Drone System, a computerized trigger for a standard AR-15. Marketing director Semion Dukhan said the first shot is mechanical and the computer then sets the rate of fire. He called lasers, jammers, interceptors, and specialized ammunition too costly or too hard to move for the long run. The company treats Thailand's fielding as a real-world endorsement.

A Nov. 3 CRS primer restates how the secretary of defense designates special operations forces

USNI News, Thursday, posted the Nov. 3 Congressional Research Service paper Defense Primer: U.S. Special Operations Forces. The report says SOF are active-duty and reserve components of the services that the secretary of defense designates as special operations forces. USNI ran the primer as a document, not as a new force change.

TruthScript says Tuesday's election should send Christians back to Scripture, not to panic

A Law & Liberty review says Hamilton opposed the French Revolution without dropping republicanism

Law & Liberty, Thursday: Michael Lucchese writes that Hamilton is remembered as a war hero, an economic architect, and a framer, and that his opposition to French revolutionary radicalism is the neglected part. Lucchese says Hamilton saw that fire as different from the American founding, and that he did not waver on republican government or natural rights.
