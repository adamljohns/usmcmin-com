George H.W. Bush anchors off Phuket after five months in Central Command

USNI News reported Monday that USS George H.W. Bush (CVN-77) pulled into Phuket, Thailand, on Sunday with USS Delbert D. Black (DDG-119). The Navy said the port call follows five months on station in U.S. Central Command during the war with Iran. The U.S. Embassy in Thailand dated the arrival Oct. 4. A Navy still from the flight deck shows Super Hornets and helicopters with green islands on the horizon.

Lithuania says it will pay if the United States plants a permanent base

Defense News reported Tuesday that Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevicius is ready to cover the cost of a permanent U.S. base if Washington agrees. President Trump said Monday he would consider the request. The United States has kept an armored battalion of almost 1,000 troops in Lithuania since 2019, in overlapping rotations. Sinkevicius said he raised a permanent presence last month with Trump envoy John Coale in Vilnius.

Heritage marks 50 years of the Hyde Amendment outlasting Roe

Monday’s commentary notes that a federal policy barring taxpayer-funded abortion turns 50 this year. It contrasts that record with Roe v. Wade, which the piece says collapsed under its own reasoning just short of a 50th year. The argument is that Hyde protected women, girls, and unborn children for half a century while the decision that legalized abortion did not survive that long.

Virginia-class modules are taking shape on the Mobile River, 1,200 miles from the boat

USNI News reported Monday from Mobile, Alabama, that spaces for a pair of Virginia-class attack submarines are being built three decks high on the Mobile River, inside a plastic, air-conditioned barrier, already painted in the Navy’s shade. The rest of each submarine is 1,200 miles away. The piece frames the work as a new federation of American shipyards rather than a single-yard program.

Law & Liberty reviews a case for patriotism that has to be taught

Tuesday’s review of Paul Carrese’s Teaching America starts from Tocqueville’s split between instinctive love of a birthplace and a patriotism that grows from knowing the laws and using civil rights. Justin Buckley Dyer writes that a republic without ancient custom needs the second kind, and that Carrese treats reflective patriotism as the remedy for what ails civic life.

Tech / AI

Boeing signs a seven-year deal to triple PAC-3 seeker output

Defense News reported that Boeing formalized an approximately $14.7 billion undefinitized agreement to triple production of the seeker for the Patriot Advanced Capability-3 Missile Segment Enhancement. The work runs seven years at Boeing’s Huntsville plant, matching Lockheed Martin’s push to raise annual PAC-3 interceptor output from about 600 to 2,000. The seeker identifies and tracks threats from ballistic missiles and hypersonics to hostile aircraft. The story notes the scrutiny that followed eight months of firing costly interceptors at cheap munitions in Operation Epic Fury.

Defense News

Pentagon says the polygraph revamp is not using generative AI

Defense News reported that declassified slides had the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency preparing tools to read text, voices, and faces for trustworthiness. On Wednesday, DCSA said active research for the Modernizing Polygraph effort does not include generative AI or large language models. The agency vets security-clearance holders and applicants. Critics have argued that scanning speech, vocal stress, or facial expression to read a mind lacks scientific backing.

TruthScript says the year since Kirk’s killing is a lesson, not a fight

Monday’s essay says the year since Charlie Kirk was assassinated produced a kind of conservative unity that had not been felt before. People went back to the place he was killed and picked up the microphone, engaging students on the issues of the day. The piece argues the useful response is to learn from that habit, not to spend the anniversary fighting over him.
