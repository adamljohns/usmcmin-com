Marines flew a live Neros Archer drone at Camp Fuji for the first time

The Marine Corps said Battalion Landing Team 1/1 of the 31st Marine Expeditionary Unit flew the Neros Archer, the service's one-way attack drone, in a live kinetic test on the Combined Arms Training Center, Camp Fuji maneuver area, on Oct. 20. Chief Warrant Officer 2 Stephen Bilbo, the camp gunner and range control officer, said it was the first time the drone had been deployed there in a live kinetic variant. Junior enlisted Marines, lance corporal through sergeant, flew two scenarios, including an anti-armor ambush. Both targets detonated, and explosive-ordnance technicians found no unexploded ordnance.

Defense News says Pentagon data puts the Iran war above $47 billion

Defense News reported Wednesday that Pentagon data obtained by Reuters put U.S. military spending on the Iran war above $47 billion, more than 25 percent higher than the Defense Department's July estimate. The story says the latest figure includes operational costs, with more than $29 billion projected to replace expended munitions and more than $6 billion for equipment and platform losses, including aircraft and drones. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's July estimate was $37.5 billion. The Pentagon had not commented on the figure when the story was filed. The estimate does not clearly include repairs to U.S. bases hit by Iranian missiles and drones.

Defense News

Britain's finance minister said other departments will cover a £4.7 billion defense-plan gap

Defense News reported that Finance Minister John Healey told Parliament on Wednesday that capital reprioritization across Whitehall would fill a £4.7 billion ($6.3 billion) gap in Britain's Defence Investment Plan. He said the budget fully funds the plan and that a spending review next year will set the path to 3 percent of GDP on defense in 2030 and the NATO 3.5 percent mark by 2035. Healey, Prime Minister Andy Burnham, and Defense Minister Wes Streeting are under pressure to move faster. Conservatives have said they want a resignation if the budget did not set a credible path.

Heritage says China leads world shipbuilding and the United States ranks 14th by tonnage

Brent Sadler's Tuesday commentary at the Heritage Foundation says China dominates global shipbuilding and the United States ranks 14th by tonnage. He ties the piece to the Navy's 251st birthday on Oct. 13, when President Trump announced a new Baltimore shipyard and restated a maritime revival begun earlier in his second term. The argument is for an allied counterweight, not a floor vote.

Congressional Research Service briefed lawmakers on U.S. strikes against Iran's missile and nuclear programs

USNI News posted the Oct. 20 Congressional Research Service report, U.S. Military Operations Against Iran's Missile and Nuclear Programs. The report's opening, as quoted in the USNI feed, says that on Feb. 28, 2026, the United States and Israel launched military operations against Iran, and that President Donald Trump listed aims the same day. Culture

TruthScript says Lula's win and a same-day travel ban show what 'democracy' means to the Brazilian court

TruthScript's Wednesday essay says Lula da Silva's reelection, after Flávio Bolsonaro won the Oct. 4 first round, was followed within minutes by a Supreme Court order from Justice Alexandre de Moraes that Bolsonaro not leave the country and turn in his passport. The piece says Bolsonaro then left for the United States, where his brother Eduardo already lives in exile, and that the State Department has indicated a visa. The writer treats evangelical growth in Brazil, from about 5 percent in 1970 to nearly 30 percent in the 2022 census, as the political base that showed up for a Bolsonaro. It is an argument about courts and churches, not a certified tally.

TruthScript

Law & Liberty says a republic lasts on constitutional reverence, not on winning the last war

Matthew K. Reising's Wednesday review of Thomas F. Madden's The Fall of Republics says Madden, following John Adams, treats a republic as a mixed government of executive, legislative, and judicial power with real checks. The lesson Reising pulls out is Madden's: without reverence for the constitution, the text is words and habits that can be set aside. Reising notes Madden's contrast with James Madison, who defined a republic by representation rather than by mixing monarchy, aristocracy, and democracy. The piece is a book argument, not a vote.

Law & Liberty

George Weigel says the Church's message is the gospel, not listening

In a Wednesday First Things column, George Weigel writes that a theologian at the recent Roman meeting on marriage and the family said the gathering showed a Church where listening becomes the message. Weigel answers with the Emmaus road in Luke 24: the two disciples already had the stories, and they kept walking the wrong way until the risen Christ opened the Scriptures and broke the bread. He argues that mission assumes settled truths about marriage as a covenant and the family as the first school of freedom. Listening shapes how the gospel is offered. It is not the gospel.
