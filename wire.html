USS San Antonio returned to Norfolk after a 4th Fleet deployment that included Venezuela relief

Naval Surface Force Atlantic said the amphibious transport dock USS San Antonio (LPD 17) returned to Naval Station Norfolk on Oct. 13, closing a deployment that began June 29. Capt. Matt Gleason, the commanding officer, said the crew had been out on two deployments across a little more than fourteen months, and that Capt. Tom Uhl led the ship through most of that stretch. The release said the ship served as the afloat command center for Southern Command disaster relief after two earthquakes in Venezuela, delivered two air-cushion landing craft loads of aid at La Guaira, and became the first amphibious transport dock since 2018 to transit the Panama Canal. In the Eastern Pacific the crew ran interdiction under Joint Task Force Western Hemisphere and transferred detained people to the Ecuadorian Coast Guard. The official still shows sailors at parade rest as the ship returns.

Tech / AI

An F-22 pilot directed an unmanned Fury drone in flight

Defense News reported that an F-22 Raptor completed airborne tactical command of a YFQ-44A Fury earlier this month. The pilot gave high-level directives; the unmanned aircraft's autonomy software flew the maneuvers and simulated combat tasks. Air Force Secretary Troy Meink said the point is turning the technology into combat capability at the pace the threat demands. The service wants 500 combat-ready semi-autonomous systems by 2032, and a fleet of 1,000.

Defense News

Army earmarked an air defense unit for Golden Dome, and did not name it

Defense News reported that Lt. Gen. John L. Rafferty, commanding general of Army Space and Missile Defense Command, told reporters at the Association of the United States Army meeting that an Army air defense unit has been earmarked for Golden Dome for America. He declined to name the unit. He described the shield as a way to pair any sensor with the best shooter and the right command node. Joint Task Force Gold, a roughly 100-person unit under Northern Command, is planning sites and a cyber piece. Pentagon officials have said they want an early version fielded by the end of next year.

Defense News

A Euronaval report says China built more than twice U.S. naval tonnage in 2024-25

Defense News reported from Paris that the World Defense Shipbuilding report, compiled by Flottes de Combat and the French industry group Gican, put Chinese combat-ship construction at 636,208 tons in the two years through December 2025, against 270,509 tons for the United States. China accounted for more than a third of global naval tonnage in that window. The value of Chinese construction was $43.5 billion, against $26.8 billion for the United States.
