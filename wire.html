Defense News: Air Force gives Boeing a $131 billion F-15 ceiling, not an order

Defense News, Friday: the Air Force awarded Boeing a sole-source F-15 Eagle Crest contract with a $131.23 billion ceiling to produce, modernize, and sustain F-15s for U.S. and foreign customers through 2037. The Aug. 24 War Department notice obligated $343,740 in fiscal 2026 research money at award. Orders may run through Aug. 24, 2031, with an option to 2036. The notice names foreign military sales to Japan, Israel, Saudi Arabia, South Korea, Singapore, Indonesia, and Poland. It does not buy a jet on signing. The fiscal 2027 F-15EX report raises the U.S. buy to 268 aircraft through fiscal 2031.

Defense News: India opens conventional missile tech to private firms

Defense News, Friday from New Delhi: India’s defense ministry approved transferring technologies for conventional missile systems from a state-run organization to private companies. A Tuesday ministry statement called it a milestone for operational capability and less import dependence. The government did not name the missiles. Analyst Manoj Joshi said a four-day fight with Pakistan last year showed how fast stocks can run down.

Pentagon awards X-Bow nearly $11 million for a cheaper interceptor

Defense News, Thursday from Kyiv: the Defense Department awarded New Mexico’s X-Bow Systems almost $11 million under a Missile Defense Agency effort aimed at an anti-ballistic weapon for less than $750,000 a shot. X-Bow prints solid rocket motors in-house. The award funds further motor work and a first flight. The agreement calls for a prototype flight demonstration by late 2027, not a fielded round.

Judge Lin rules the Pentagon’s Anthropic supply-chain label illegal

Associated Press, Friday: U.S. District Judge Rita Lin wrote Thursday night that the Pentagon acted illegally when it designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk. Lin said the move was based on a desire to make a public example of the company for criticizing the department’s views on AI use, not on an articulable sabotage risk. Anthropic sued in San Francisco and Washington. The D.C. case is still open, so the designation is not fully lifted. The White House did not immediately comment. Nvidia’s quarter stays with Carnegie.

Law & Liberty: Dolly Parton’s American life outlasted the fashion

Law & Liberty, Friday: Titus Techera reads Dolly Parton’s death on Aug. 25 as the close of a distinctly Southern American life — a one-room cabin, a long marriage, hymns, and Dollywood as family entertainment rather than a lecture. The essay says she refused to politicize the feelings at the core of her songs and still built an Imagination Library that has delivered hundreds of millions of books. It is an obituary reading, not a chart claim.
