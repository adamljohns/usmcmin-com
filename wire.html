The Navy's next-generation nuclear attack submarine, the SSN(X), will be built from the start as a mothership for undersea drones, with the hull, power and payload designed around launching, recovering, recharging and controlling multiple unmanned underwater vehicles, the Navy's top submarine-requirements officer said this week

Rear Adm. Andrew Miller, director of the Navy's Undersea Warfare Division, told the Naval Submarine League conference in Arlington, Virginia, that the Virginia-class successor will combine Seawolf-class speed and weapons capacity, Virginia-class acoustic stealth and Columbia-class operational availability, according to USNI News. Unlike the Virginia class, which has to retrofit existing torpedo tubes and missile launchers to carry unmanned underwater vehicles, the SSN(X) will have dedicated handling, recharging and command-and-control infrastructure built into a wider hull without sacrificing weapons stowage. The Navy completed its analysis of alternatives in November 2025, got requirements approval in March 2026 and is targeting Milestone A authorization by the end of this year, with the first boat not due for procurement until the early 2040s. Navy officials have tied the push toward autonomous undersea capability to expanding Chinese submarine construction and drone programs.

All passengers and crew were accounted for Friday after a river cruise boat capsized on the Mississippi River in New Orleans following a collision with a tanker, with more than 30 people hospitalized but none critically injured

New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno said at a Friday afternoon press conference that everyone aboard the riverboat American Heritage was accounted for after the tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the vessel, which listed onto its side with more than 125 passengers and crew aboard, according to WORLD. American Cruise Lines confirmed the American Heritage was involved and said it was working to house survivors in hotels. Moreno called the outcome a true miracle on the Mississippi and credited first responders; the U.S. Coast Guard responded to the scene and closed three miles of the river to vessel traffic during the rescue.

The House advanced a bill that would let combat-disabled veterans draw full retirement pay alongside their disability compensation, after a discharge petition timed to Veterans Day forced a vote

The House passed the discharge petition 245 to 175 Wednesday, sending the Major Richard Star Act to the floor; nearly all Democrats backed it while Republicans split 34 in favor to 175 against, per WORLD. The bill, named for a post-9/11 Army combat engineer whose cancer was linked to toxic exposure overseas, would end the current offset that reduces retirement pay by the same amount as disability compensation for veterans who served less than 20 years. The Congressional Budget Office estimated eligible retirees would gain about $1,450 more a month, at a cost to the government of roughly $13 billion from 2027 to 2036.

British counterterrorism police arrested a U.S. Army soldier near Gloucester, England, on suspicion he was passing protected information to Russian intelligence

Officers took the 41-year-old soldier, assigned to NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps headquartered in Gloucester, into custody and searched two nearby locations, WORLD reported. Brig. Gen. Sean Stinchon of U.S. Army Counterintelligence Command described the suspect as a senior soldier entrusted with sensitive information, and officials said the case is not connected to last month's thwarted terror plot against a nearby air base. FBI Director Kash Patel said the arrest followed cooperation between American and British intelligence services and that the bureau has no tolerance for those who break their oaths.

Japan agreed to give the Philippine military a coastal surveillance radar system and unmanned aircraft under a security grant, the first time Tokyo has supplied finished defense equipment abroad

Japanese Ambassador to the Philippines Katsuro Kitagawa and Philippine officials signed the roughly 4.3 billion yen (about $27 million) security assistance deal, with the radar going to the Philippine Navy and the drones to the Philippine Air Force, according to USNI News. The package is the latest in a string of Japanese security assistance moves as Tokyo and Manila both face rising tension with China over disputed waters in the South China Sea.

Italy more than tripled its planned spending on the GCAP next-generation fighter program this year while cutting funding for new army tanks and fighting vehicles, newly released budget documents show

Rome allocated about 875 million euros to the Global Combat Air Programme, the joint sixth-generation fighter project with the United Kingdom and Japan, while army vehicle funding fell to about 177 million euros, according to Defense News. The cuts land on planned purchases of Lynx infantry fighting vehicles and Panther and Ariete tank modernization, even as those programs remain valued at roughly 23 billion euros over their full run.

A new collection of essays by Wendell Berry, who died in September, arrived in late October as a quiet farewell that returns to the anti-industrial, pro-farm themes of his career

Reviewing Dispatches from Rural America for Law & Liberty, Nadya Williams writes that the collection arrives near the 50th anniversary of Berry's The Unsettling of America and reflects on a 1967 federal commission that found the country's biggest farm problem was a surplus of farmers — a diagnosis Berry spent his career arguing got the real tragedy backward: the hollowing-out of small towns, farms and rural communities that followed policies built on that premise.

The Mosaic law still has something to say about politics, a new essay argues, even though most Christian political debate leans on the New Testament alone

The essay argues that while Jesus and Paul's teachings on loving neighbors and honoring rulers matter, they leave open the harder question of what rulers and laws should actually accomplish — a question Moses and the prophets addressed directly through instruction on judges, kings, property and the vulnerable. Earlier Protestants, the piece notes, refused the choice between reestablishing Israel's civil constitution and discarding its political teaching altogether, asking instead which obligations of justice endure once Israel's specific institutions are gone.
