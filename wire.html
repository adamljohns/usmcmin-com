Walter Reed cadre put fourth-year medical students through a field test at Fort Indiantown Gap

A Defense Department still posted Friday morning shows medical students treating a simulated casualty while instructors watch during Operation Bushmaster at Fort Indiantown Gap, Pennsylvania, on Oct. 21. The release says the exercise is the Uniformed Services University capstone: five days of four-hour leadership cycles, with drone noise, prolonged casualty care, and problems drawn from modern conflict. Navy Capt. Sherri Rudinsky, who chairs the military and emergency medicine department, said about 380 faculty and cadre ran it. At least nine Walter Reed staff from the Army, Navy, and Air Force were among them. Army 2nd Lt. Lalah Allen, a medical-surgical nurse on Walter Reed's 4 East, proctored the point-of-injury and casualty-collection lanes and graded tactical combat casualty care.

Political

A former French captain says the FDI frigate's inverted bow cuts slamming and vibration

Defense News, Friday, from Euronaval in Paris: Nicolas Guiraud, who commanded the lead FDI frigate Amiral Ronarc'h for four years until July and now works for Naval Group, said the inverted bow lets the ship plow through waves without impact. He said fewer vibrations mean a hull that ages more slowly, fewer cracks, and connectors that stay seated. French Navy chief Adm. Christophe Cluzel told lawmakers last month the design has proved maneuverable and stable. Sweden announced in May that it plans to buy four of the frigates, already in French and Greek service. France took delivery of its first FDI in October 2025.

The Royal International Air Tattoo is leaving RAF Fairford for Boscombe Down

Defense News, Thursday: organizers said Wednesday the show will move from RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire to MOD Boscombe Down in Wiltshire after four decades. They canceled the 2026 edition in late May, citing uncertainty over access while the base was tied to the war in Iran. Defense News reports the U.S. Air Force housed a dozen B-1B Lancers at Fairford during Operation Epic Fury for strikes on Iranian missile sites that were attacking ships in the Strait of Hormuz, then pulled aircraft after a suspected plot against the base.

Kim Jong Un inspected a warship under construction at Nampho that is larger than North Korea's destroyers

USNI News, Thursday: state media said Kim inspected the ship on Monday at Nampho Shipyard. USNI reports the hull under construction is believed to be a 10,000-ton cruiser, larger than the country's 5,000-ton destroyers. The visit was reported Wednesday and filed Thursday.

Tech / AI

Shield AI agreed to buy radar maker Echodyne for $5.3 billion

Defense News, Thursday: Shield AI agreed to acquire Echodyne in a $5.3 billion deal announced Thursday. Echodyne builds solid-state radar it patents as Metamaterials Electronically Scanned Array, used to detect drones and to feed lasers, microwave weapons, and other systems. In September the Defense Department's counter-drone task force, JIATF-401, awarded Echodyne a $250 million contract to supply that technology over 36 months. CEO Eben Frankenberg told Defense News in July that demand is red hot and could grow tenfold by 2030.

Defense News

Vector rolled out a one-way attack package that pairs its Mace drone with an arming device and a controller

Defense News, Thursday: Utah-based Vector said Nov. 2 that the Warfighter's Stack combines the Mace quadcopter, an electronic arming device, and a controller. Co-founder Andy Yakulis said the package is meant for operators who can employ it and for production at the scale the mission needs. Defense News ties the product to Vector's place in the Pentagon's Drone Dominance Program, the competition used to expand the domestic commercial drone industry and place orders with top makers.

Culture

A Law & Liberty essay says the Facebook reckoning film describes a judgment that never arrived

Law & Liberty, Friday: the writer, a former fact-check intern whose program was funded by Facebook, argues that The Social Reckoning, the sequel to The Social Network, shows leaders facing consequences for misinformation that did not in fact arrive. He says a newsroom team filing short debunks into a platform tool could not cover billions of posts, and that Facebook's later rebrand to Meta is not the same thing as a reckoning. The piece treats the film as a story about a judgment the record does not support.

Law & Liberty

A Providence review of In the War Room says Israel recovered a stronger military position after Oct. 7

Providence, Friday: a review of Yonah Jeremy Bob and Elliot Kaufman's In the War Room says the book, built on interviews with Benjamin Netanyahu, Yoav Gallant, Benny Gantz, and unnamed Israeli military and intelligence officials, traces a recovery from the defeat of Oct. 7 to a stronger military-strategic position than Israel has held in decades. The reviewer says the authors concede the deterrence failure that allowed the Hamas attack and put that failure on overconfidence in surveillance and neglected ground-force readiness.
