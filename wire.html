Amphibious assault ship USS Makin Island and transport dock USS John P. Murtha left Singapore this week, bound for the Indian Ocean with Marines from the 13th Marine Expeditionary Unit aboard, as the Makin Island Amphibious Ready Group continues its run through the Indo-Pacific

A wave of foreign-born lawmakers elected in this month's midterms, including Colorado's Ethiopian-born Melat Kiros, share a political thread pulling Congress further left, a Heritage Foundation commentary argues, warning of more anti-Westernism, socialism and Islamism in the incoming Congress

The piece argues that whatever one thinks of the midterm results, the newly elected cohort of immigrant-background lawmakers is poised to push more anti-Westernism, socialism and Islamism into Congress, naming Colorado's Ethiopian-born Melat Kiros among the examples it cites alongside a broader list of immigrant-origin Democrats now in office.

Heritage Foundation

Turkish leaders' talk of 'liberating' Jerusalem the way they liberated Damascus draws on a romanticized reading of Ottoman history that U.S. officials, including envoy Tom Barrack and President Trump himself, have increasingly echoed, a Providence essay argues

The essay cites Turkish interior minister Mustafa Çiftçi's pledge to one day 'liberate' Jerusalem and ally Devlet Bahçeli's argument that the road to Jerusalem ran through a now-fallen Damascus, tracing both to historian İlber Ortaylı's influential claim that four centuries of Ottoman rule brought the Middle East an enduring peace that protected the rights of all religions. It notes U.S. ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack has praised the Ottoman millet system as a model for the region, while President Trump congratulated Erdoğan on absorbing Syria through surrogates and has floated F-35 sales to Ankara, as Turkey's post-2013 foreign policy leans further into neo-Ottoman, anti-Western rhetoric.

Providence

Catholics swung toward Democrats by roughly the same margin as the nation as a whole in this month's midterms, undercutting any claim of a unified 'Catholic vote,' a First Things analysis finds — while a sharp ethnic split between white and Hispanic Catholics tells the real story beneath that number

The essay notes Catholics backed Democratic House candidates 51-48 this month, a steep reversal from Trump's 55-43 edge among Catholics in 2024 and the 56-43 Republican edge in the 2022 midterms — a shift nearly matching the 17-point national swing toward Democrats. It argues that similarity to the national trend leaves little sense in talking about a single 'Catholic vote,' since the roughly 54 percent of American Catholics who are non-Hispanic white and the 36 percent who are Hispanic increasingly vote as two distinct, often opposing, blocs despite sharing one faith.

The Trump administration is asking Congress to fund the redesigned Ford-class carrier program and the Trump-class battleship through anomaly language in the next stopgap spending bill, according to a White House budget office document obtained by USNI News

USNI News reports the Office of Management and Budget has compiled a list of major shipbuilding programs it wants Congress to keep funded through anomaly provisions in the next continuing resolution, including the redesigned Ford-class aircraft carrier program and the Trump-class battleship — part of a recurring pattern in which OMB has pushed similar carrier and battleship funding anomalies into earlier stopgap bills this year.

USNI News

Czech jet engine maker PBS Aerospace plans to double production capacity at its Roswell, Georgia plant over the next three years, betting on booming U.S. demand for compact propulsion systems that power drones and cruise missiles

Chief government relations and strategy officer Jan Havránek told Defense News that global conflicts, including the war in Ukraine, have underscored the need for a reliable domestic supply of these engines, and that PBS plans to scale Roswell's annual output to between 10,000 and 20,000 engines within three years — including up to 5,000 next year alone — backed by roughly $100 million in added capacity. The company, whose U.S. partners include Lockheed Martin and Pratt & Whitney, opened the Georgia plant in September 2025 with a $20 million investment while continuing to build engines at its home factory in Velká Bíteš, Czech Republic.

Culture

Grand Theft Auto 6's near-photorealistic polish doesn't make it safe entertainment, a TruthScript essay argues, warning that Christians — and everyone else — should skip the game because its immersive world trains the imagination and appetites toward vice no matter how impressive the production

The essay concedes GTA 6 is a genuine technical achievement — thirteen years and enormous sums spent on a beautiful, near-photorealistic world — but argues beauty does not equal virtue, invoking Ephesians 5's call to walk wisely and make the best use of time because 'the days are evil.' It traces the franchise's evolution from a cartoonish 1990s crime game to a multibillion-dollar, first-person world saturated with drugs, prostitution and casual violence, and rejects any 'eat the meat, spit out the bones' defense of playing it.

TruthScript

The real story of the Pilgrims and America's First Peoples defies both the Hallmark-movie myth of peaceful Thanksgiving concord and the opposite caricature of pure colonial conquest, a Law & Liberty review of a new history of Puritan New England argues, days before the holiday itself

The review of Matthew Tuininga's 'The Wars of the Lord: The Puritan Conquest of America's First People' says the English settlers in Plymouth, Salem, Boston and the other early colonies were neither avaricious conquerors nor purely pious missionaries, and that readers hunting for a clean moral binary — angels or demons — will come away disappointed. It frames that mixed, complicated history, not a tidy legend in either direction, as the honest backdrop to the holiday now days away.
