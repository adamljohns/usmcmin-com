About 120 Indiana National Guard soldiers with the 38th Military Police Company came home to Indianapolis on Nov. 25, wrapping up a yearlong deployment to the U.S. southern border in Texas just ahead of Thanksgiving

DVIDS reports the soldiers, with the 38th Military Police Company, 81st Troop Command, returned to Indianapolis after a yearlong deployment with Joint Task Force-Southern Border in support of Operation Ardent Vanguard, where they assisted the Department of Homeland Security and U.S. Customs and Border Protection in monitoring the southern border in Texas for unlawful mass migration, narcotics trafficking and human smuggling. "Today, we welcome home the Soldiers of the Indiana National Guard who answered the call to serve our nation along the southern border," said Maj. Gen. Larry Muennich, the Indiana National Guard's adjutant general, praising their "professionalism, dedication, and service." Indiana Gov. Mike Braun added he was "very pleased" the soldiers would be home "in time to enjoy Thanksgiving dinner with their loved ones" as they now transition back to civilian careers, families and communities.

New Jersey lawmakers are weighing a bill that would make the state the first in the nation to formally define 'Islamophobia' in law, a move a new Heritage Foundation commentary warns functions as a disguised blasphemy law

The bill under consideration in New Jersey would be the first in the nation to legally define 'Islamophobia,' and Heritage Foundation's commentary argues the definition is written so overbroad that it would make it all but impossible to evaluate Islam and the culture it produces in any meaningful way, effectively functioning as a blasphemy law under a different name.

James Bryce, the Belfast-born scholar who served as British ambassador to Washington under Teddy Roosevelt, diagnosed the demagoguery and decayed public opinion now unsettling American democracy more than a century before last week's midterms, a new Providence essay argues

The essay draws on Bryce's 1888 study The American Commonwealth, written as an industrial-age successor to Tocqueville's Democracy in America, in which Bryce defined healthy democracy as 'the participation of the whole of the nation in its own affairs' built on shared civic responsibility rather than class or party interest. Bryce warned that demagogues feed on democracy's vices — 'indolence, private self-interest, and party spirit' — turning them into false virtues, and he criticized Conservative leader Benjamin Disraeli and Liberal defector Joseph Chamberlain by name, even as his own Liberal colleagues William Gladstone and David Lloyd George were accused of the same demagoguery. Bryce trusted an educated public opinion — built on strong universities, public schools and a vibrant print culture — as the best defense, an 'ethical' remedy he considered more durable than any legal fix. The essay argues today's America has high voter turnout but little of the 'sense of common interest' Bryce prized, pointing to divisive midterm rhetoric and decayed civic education, while citing the campus 'civics centers' movement and Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa's pledge to reinvest in public schools as reasons for optimism.

More than 700,000 Russian soldiers have been killed in the war in Ukraine since February 2022, according to leaked Rosstat data analyzed by Russian opposition outlets Mediazona and Meduza — a toll now higher than all combined U.S. military deaths since World War I

Defense News reports the updated estimate, based on leaked data from Russia's statistical bureau analyzed by Belarusian and Ukrainian hackers, shows Russian fatalities jumped from about 490,000 at the end of September to more than 720,000 since the war began — roughly one death every four minutes, per CNN. Meduza says the data was cross-checked against lists of the deceased, inheritance-case registries, civil registry leaks and court records. The Kremlin has not updated its own death toll since early in the war, when then-Defense Minister Sergei Shoigu acknowledged about 6,000 dead. By comparison, official DoD figures put total U.S. military deaths at 624,547 across more than a century of conflict, from World War I and World War II through Korea, Vietnam, the Gulf War, Afghanistan and Iraq — a toll Russia's five-year war in Ukraine has now surpassed, though it still falls far short of the estimated 8.8 million to 10.7 million Soviet military deaths, and roughly 24 million total Soviet deaths, in World War II.

Defense News

Japan will send troops to fire live long-range missiles on Indian firing ranges for the first time, under a new pact signed Nov. 19 that lets Tokyo test its 1,000-kilometer-range Type-25 surface-to-ship missile beyond its own cramped home ranges

Defense News reports Japan and India signed a Letter of Intent on Nov. 19, after a meeting between the two countries' foreign and defense ministers in Tokyo, that will let Japanese troops conduct live-fire missile training on Indian ranges for the first time. Japan Ground Self-Defense Force Chief of Staff Gen. Masayoshi Arai said the goal is testing the roughly 1,000-kilometer range of Japan's Type-25 surface-to-ship missile, which Japan's own cramped home ranges can't accommodate; Tokyo has been deploying long-range missiles on the southern island of Kyushu this year amid concerns over Chinese military activity in the region. The two countries also agreed to accelerate talks on their first defense-technology cooperation deal, co-producing a radar-signature-reducing antenna system for Indian Navy warships, building on a September exercise in which Japan sent F-2A fighter jets to India for the first time.

Culture

Two Norman Rockwell Thanksgiving-era paintings capture a theological tradition most Americans have forgotten — that every presidential Thanksgiving proclamation since Washington has named God, not human effort alone, as the ultimate source of the nation's blessings, a First Things essay argues

The essay contrasts Rockwell's well-known 1943 painting Freedom from Want, which celebrates home, family and food, with his lesser-known 1943 Saturday Evening Post cover Thanksgiving, showing a newly liberated Italian woman in an Army greatcoat giving silent thanks to God amid wartime wreckage. It traces the doctrine of "secondary causality" — God as ultimate cause, humans as free secondary causes of the good — through Augustine, Aquinas, Calvin and the Westminster Confession, and through the Book of Common Prayer used by George Washington himself. The piece, drawing on the author's own Heritage Foundation report on presidential Thanksgiving proclamations, says that religious framing held from Washington through FDR but began fraying after World War II, with Obama and Biden recasting the holiday as generalized civic gratitude while Reagan and Trump reasserted its traditional focus on God as, in Washington's words, "the beneficent Author of all the good that was, that is, or that will be."

Fox Nation's new Christian reality dating series "The Vow," created by longtime Bachelor host Chris Harrison, drops the roses and elimination ceremonies but keeps the same dating-show formula underneath its crosses and modesty talk, a new WORLD review says

The review follows 10 Christian women competing for one bachelor, 30-year-old Jace Cates, in a six-episode series hosted by Chris Harrison, who ran ABC's The Bachelor for 19 years and pitches this show as free of "roses, elimination ceremonies, no gimmicks." WORLD's reviewer notes the premiere undercuts that pitch within its first two minutes with lingering shots of Cates showering and lifting weights, and says that despite contestants affirming no-sex-before-marriage and stay-at-home parenting, reading Bibles and forming prayer circles on camera, the show remains, at heart, "polyamory in pretty packaging" aimed at a church-adjacent audience. The series is rated TV-MA on Fox Nation.
