Navy awards Raytheon up to $24.4 billion for more than 1,900 SM-6 missiles

The Navy contracted RTX’s Raytheon to build more than 1,900 Standard Missile-6 interceptors over five years, with two option years, in a deal worth up to $24.4 billion announced Thursday. The SM-6 handles air defense, ballistic-missile defense and ship strikes, and the award follows months of heavy munitions use in the Middle East. The open question is money: Congress still has to appropriate the funds before the plant can invest at full scale.

Pentagon tells Senate it will build an area-denial network along the First Island Chain

In a Sept. 22 report to the Senate Armed Services Committee on the 2026 National Defense Strategy, the Pentagon restated its plan for a forward-deployed anti-access network along the First Island Chain to deter China. The report also presses the need for more high-end munitions, the same shortfall behind this week’s SM-6 buy.

Taiwan receives its first two F-16V Block 70 fighters after years of delay

The first two of 66 F-16V Block 70 jets from a 2019 U.S. sale landed at Chihhang Air Base in Taitung on Friday. Software problems held up the $8 billion order. Defense Minister Wellington Koo said more aircraft would arrive soon but gave no schedule.

Navy closes fiscal 2026 with at least 45,112 enlisted recruits

The Navy’s final numbers show at least 45,112 active-duty enlisted sailors brought in during fiscal 2026, above its 45,000 goal. The service had announced in July that it reached the goal three months early.

A Heritage commentary pushes back on the K-shaped-economy account, arguing that gains in home values and retirement savings belong in any measure of how the middle class is doing. It is an economic argument from Heritage, not a new government data release.

France plans to fly fighters with AI-driven combat drones in 2028

France’s Air Force, its armament directorate and the defense AI agency plan a 2028 flight pairing crewed fighters with combat drones under a project called Hypairion. Officials said the aim is government-owned, open software for command and control and mission autonomy, rather than relying on a contractor’s closed system.
