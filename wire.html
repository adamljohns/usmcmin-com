CENTCOM commander flew over the Strait of Hormuz as Gulf oil exports passed 1.5 billion barrels

U.S. Central Command said Adm. Brad Cooper flew a U.S. Army CH-47 Chinook over the Strait of Hormuz on Oct. 25 and watched commercial traffic in the waterway. CENTCOM said U.S. forces have helped Gulf partners export more than 1.5 billion barrels of crude since summer, and that more than 3,000 commercial vessels transited with that support in the last six months. Cooper said primary transit routes remain free of Iranian sea mines after a clearance mission. The release says more than 95 percent of ships sailing the strait in recent months have gotten through. It does not say how many did not.

Political

Two Marines sued the Pentagon over the manpower data breach

Defense News reported that Marine veteran Tristyn Edwards and active-duty Marine Morgan Stampfli filed a class-action complaint Tuesday in federal court in the District of Columbia. They say fraudulent charges and more spam followed a Defense Manpower Data Center breach. The agency said in September it found a July 16 vulnerability in a file-sharing system that let unauthorized users reach unencrypted files with names, Social Security numbers, birth dates, and military occupations. The complaint says the hole was open about nine months before it was found. The Pentagon has not filed an answer in the piece.

Defense News

A European shipyard tally says China built more than twice U.S. naval tonnage in 2024-25

Defense News reported from Paris that Euronaval's World Defense Shipbuilding report puts Chinese combat-ship construction at 636,208 tons in the two years through December 2025, against 270,509 tons for the United States. The same tally still has the U.S. fleet larger on Jan. 1, 2026: 3.11 million tons to China's 1.44 million. The numbers are an industry compilation, not a Navy ship count.

Defense News

About 4,000 JROTC cadets finished a four-day raider challenge at Fort Knox

U.S. Army Cadet Command said the JROTC National Raider Challenge ran at Fort Knox from Oct. 22 to 25, with about 4,000 cadets. The course included obstacle lanes, a run of more than five kilometers, a one-rope bridge, and a 4.2-mile carry of 102 pounds for the top teams. The release names the event. It does not name a champion.

Tech / AI

The Army opened a sandbox so vendors can get software scanned before it reaches the network

The Army said Friday it launched Project Gatekeeper, a testing sandbox for commercial software. Acting chief information officer Gabe Chiulli said companies used to navigate ad-hoc routes just to get code evaluated. Gatekeeper lets developers submit code and containerized applications into isolated environments and get automated security feedback in near-real time.

DVIDS

The Air Force wants cameras and software to grade aerial refueling crews after the pass

Defense News reported Friday that the Air Force wants artificial intelligence to watch aerial refueling from cameras on the tankers and give crews a hot-wash afterward. The request is in an Air Force small-business solicitation. It is a want, not a fielded system.

Defense News

Washington told the U.N. a Russian satellite closed on a U.S.-registered radar craft

Defense News reported Friday from Kyiv that Washington blamed Moscow at the United Nations on Wednesday for moving Russian military satellite Cosmos 2612 close to ICEYE-X36, a U.S.-registered radar satellite the paper says is believed to serve Ukraine. Officials said they would defend American interests in space. The piece does not quote a new treaty.

American Reformer says the fertility drop is a friendship problem, not a married-couple one

Wednesday's American Reformer essay by Lyman Stone says the U.S. fertility rate fell from 2.1 to 1.6 children per woman since 2007. Stone writes that married couples are not foregoing children more than they did in 2007, and that married fertility has slipped only slightly. The rest of the argument sits behind a subscription wall.
