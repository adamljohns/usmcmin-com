Two airmen died last week in non-hostile incidents, lifting the Iran-war toll to 23

Defense News, Wednesday: the Pentagon's casualty file lists both deaths as non-hostile, one illness or injury and one still pending. An Air Force spokesperson told Military Times one airman was active duty and one was in the Air National Guard. One died in U.S. Central Command's area; the other died in the United States. Names have not been released. Defense News counts 15 deaths under Operation Epic Fury and eight under Overseas Operations, four hostile and four non-hostile in that second list. Future USS Harrisburg finished builder's trials Saturday, Ingalls said

USNI News, Tuesday: the first Flight II San Antonio-class amphibious transport dock, future USS Harrisburg (LPD-30), completed multi-day builder's trials on Saturday. Ingalls Shipbuilding tested propulsion, steering, communications, and the ship-control, mission, and combat systems. Acceptance trials still come before delivery to the Navy later this year, the yard's release said.

USNI News, Monday: the Royal Navy and Royal Air Force tracked Russian ships near the United Kingdom across three operations spanning two weeks last month. Tech / AI

MBDA says a European hypersonic interceptor demonstrator flies by the end of 2029

Defense News, Thursday, from Euronaval near Paris: MBDA vice president Lionel Mazenq said the Hydis consortium wants a full-systems demonstrator by the end of 2029, with production in the first half of the 2030s. A European funding decision on about 100 million euros for the second phase is due at the end of this month. Mazenq described a four-stage missile about 7 meters long, compatible with the Mark 41 launcher, and aimed at less than a third the unit price of an SM-3. Germany is dropping out as a partner country for phase two. He said the only equivalent U.S. program is the Glide Phase Interceptor, awarded to Northrop Grumman.

The Air Force wants mobile concrete printers that can stand up to missiles and drones

Defense News, Wednesday: an Air Force small-business solicitation, closing in January 2027, says troop housing at Middle East bases was never designed for the threat now overhead. Hundreds of U.S. military buildings across eight Middle Eastern countries were damaged by Iranian attacks, the story says, citing a September 2026 account. The service wants printers that lightly trained engineers can run in austere conditions, using local cement where they can, and shapes meant to cut blast reflection and fragmentation. The Air Force already used 3D printing at hurricane-damaged Tyndall in 2023.

Culture

Harvard's Catholic chaplain says more than forty were received last year, and sixty are expected this year

Father John P. Nolan writes in First Things, dated Thursday, that he is the undergraduate Catholic chaplain at Harvard. Last year more than forty students and faculty were baptized or received into the Church. He expects at least sixty this year. A few decades ago the number was consistently under ten. He names grace, a countercultural church, thin alternatives, and ordinary witnesses — an athlete inviting teammates to a Bible study, FOCUS missionaries in the Yard — as the reasons. He also says Mass attendance is nowhere near what it was thirty years ago.

First Things

An essay says Sayyid Qutb saw communism and liberalism as the same materialist sickness

Providence, Thursday: the piece follows Qutb from an Egyptian village school to a 1948 trip through America, then into the Muslim Brotherhood and the 1964 book that jihadist groups later treated as a roadmap. Qutb wrote that the last contest would be Islam against materialism, and that communism would beat capitalism because it was a more complete system. He was executed in 1966. The essay notes a June Cato/Morning Consult poll in which 38 percent of Americans under thirty viewed communism favorably.
