USS McFaul pulled into Norfolk after a deployment to 5th and 6th Fleet

A Navy photograph posted Sunday shows the Arleigh Burke-class guided-missile destroyer USS McFaul (DDG 74) pulling into Naval Station Norfolk on Oct. 23. The caption says the homecoming closes a deployment to the U.S. 5th and 6th Fleet areas of operations. USNI News reported Friday that the ship had been gone 332 days, most of that time in the Middle East, after leaving Norfolk on Nov. 25. The photo page does not list a crew count or a port-call tally.

Political

Two Marines sued the Pentagon over the manpower data breach

Defense News reported that Marine veteran Tristyn Edwards and active-duty Marine Morgan Stampfli filed a class-action complaint in federal court in the District of Columbia. They say fraudulent charges and more spam followed a Defense Manpower Data Center breach. The agency said in September it found a July 16 vulnerability in a file-sharing system that let unauthorized users reach unencrypted files with names, Social Security numbers, birth dates, and military occupations. The complaint says the hole was open about nine months before it was found, and that some people were told two or three months after the find. The piece does not quote a Pentagon answer.

Defense News

A European shipyard tally says China built more than twice U.S. naval tonnage in 2024-25

Defense News reported from Paris that Euronaval's World Defense Shipbuilding report puts Chinese combat-ship construction at 636,208 tons in the two years through December 2025, against 270,509 tons for the United States. The same piece says China accounted for more than a third of global naval tonnage built in that window, and that worldwide combat-vessel construction rose to 1.47 million tons from 959,671 in the prior two years. The numbers are an industry compilation, not a Navy ship count.

Law & Liberty says party politics turns privately honest men into knaves

Monday's Law & Liberty essay says Hume and Hamilton treated parties as inescapable in free politics, and as a reason checks and balances matter. The piece draws a line between Hume's sensible knave, for whom a love of reputation is constructive, and the party man, for whom that same love turns a privately honest man into a knave in politics. It is an argument about constitutional design, not a vote count.

Tech / AI

The Army opened a sandbox so vendors can get software scanned before it reaches the network

The Army said its Office of the Chief Information Officer launched Project Gatekeeper on Oct. 23, a testing sandbox for commercial software. Acting chief information officer Gabe Chiulli said companies used to navigate ad-hoc routes just to get code evaluated, and that the new pipeline is meant to cut evaluation cycles from months to minutes. Developers can submit code and containerized applications into isolated environments and get automated security feedback. Access is currently limited to vendors with a Department of War common access card. Public access is not open yet.

DVIDS

The Air Force wants cameras and software to grade KC-46 refueling crews after the pass

Defense News reported Friday that the Air Force wants software to watch aerial refueling from cameras on the tankers and give crews a hot-wash afterward. The request is a small-business solicitation aimed at the KC-46, with applications due Nov. 25. It is a want, not a fielded system.

Defense News

Washington told the U.N. a Russian satellite closed on a U.S.-registered radar craft

Defense News reported Friday from Kyiv that Washington blamed Moscow at the United Nations on Wednesday for moving Russian military satellite Cosmos 2612 close to ICEYE-X36, a U.S.-registered radar satellite the paper says supplies imagery to Ukraine. Undersecretary of State Thomas G. DiNanno called the maneuver threatening and consistent with on-orbit weapons operations. The paper says the Russian craft shadowed the other from as close as a third of a mile through the weekend of Oct. 17. The piece does not quote a new treaty.
