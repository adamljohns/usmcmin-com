A B-21 Raider stealth bomber released a weapon in flight for the first time during a recent test, the Air Force and Northrop Grumman confirmed Friday, marking the aircraft's shift from a flight-test program toward a combat-ready weapons platform

The Department of the Air Force released a new photo Friday through the Defense Visual Information Distribution Service showing a B-21 Raider landing at Edwards Air Force Base, California, to announce the milestone. Edwards AFB said in a statement that the Raider released a conventional weapon during a recent test mission from the base, validating its digital models and the maturity of its open systems architecture; the Air Force did not disclose the type of weapon, or the exact date or location of the test. Northrop Grumman, the aircraft's manufacturer, said in a statement that the B-21 "has demonstrated weapons release capability, marking a major step forward for America's next-generation bomber," adding that it shows the aircraft "can carry and employ combat power" as the program advances toward full warfighting capability. The B-21 is intended to replace the Air Force's B-1 and B-2 bombers and is designed to carry both conventional and nuclear weapons.

Political

The FAA grounded every U.S. flight for more than four hours Friday after air traffic controllers began losing stable radar and communications data, then lifted the stop that evening

The Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop Friday afternoon after controllers started experiencing unstable radar data and aircraft communications beginning at 3:36 p.m. Eastern, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said at an evening press conference; the agency lifted the stop at 8:15 p.m. Duffy said controllers never lost all communications and that the ground stop was issued out of caution, while Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the public and flights were never at risk and officials do not believe a cyberattack was involved. Duffy said the deeper problem is an aging air traffic control system — old routers began glitching Friday after getting overloaded with data — and called on Congress to provide up to $50 billion for system upgrades.

WORLD

The Supreme Court agreed to take up a challenge to President Trump's order freezing the federal asylum system, its second major refugee-policy case this year

The U.S. Supreme Court agreed Friday to consider two points of argument in a lawsuit over Trump's executive order halting the federal system for granting asylum to refugees, agreeing to review a federal appeals court ruling against the administration. The justices already ruled in a related case in June, siding with Trump on his authority to end Temporary Protected Status for Haitian and Syrian nationals who had been allowed to remain in the country.

WORLD

International Criminal Court judges formally closed the part of their Afghanistan investigation that had examined alleged crimes by U.S. troops, narrowing the case to the Taliban

ICC judges issued a decision closing part of the prosecutor's Afghanistan investigation that had looked at alleged crimes by U.S. troops and a separate probe into the ISIS-K militia, leaving only the inquiry into the Taliban since 2021, court documents show. The prosecutor's office told Reuters it has appealed. The decision follows U.S. sanctions against the court over arrest warrants for Israeli officials and the Afghanistan probe into American troops; judges said closing the case does not change the court's founding statute, which still allows prosecution of anyone accused of atrocity crimes on the territory of member states, so it is unclear whether the move will satisfy American concerns about jurisdiction over U.S. nationals.

Tech / AI

SpaceX planned to attempt catching both its Starship spacecraft and its Super Heavy booster for the first time, a step toward a fully reusable launch system

SpaceX planned to launch its unmanned Starship spacecraft and Super Heavy V3 booster from Starbase, Texas, Friday afternoon and attempt to catch both pieces with the launch tower's mechanical arms, something the company has never done with Starship and has done only with the earlier V1 booster. SpaceX has previously caught three Super Heavy V1 boosters, two of which flew again, but had never tried to catch the newer V3 version or the Starship upper stage itself. If the flight went well, Starship was set to orbit Earth about 14 times over 22 hours before attempting reentry along a path meant to keep it over sparsely populated parts of northern Mexico and southern Texas; the flight plan also included ocean-splashdown options if something went wrong, since SpaceX has only tested Starship in orbit once before.

WORLD

The Air Force adopted a new classified Force Design 2026 that shifts its approach to air defense from shooting down individual drones to networked sensors, autonomy and AI

The Air Force replaced its 2024 "One Force Design" guidance with a new Force Design 2026, which remains classified but which the service says more sharply defines the capabilities it needs against sophisticated threats, Lt. Gen. Chris Niemi, the deputy chief of staff for Force Modernization, said in the service's release. The 2024 document had organized counter-drone defenses into short-range, long-range and intercontinental categories; the new one instead emphasizes integrating autonomy, sensors and command-and-control with aircraft to operate in denied environments, a shift Air Force Chief of Staff Gen. Ken Wilsbach called "a fundamental shift" in approach. An Air Force spokesperson told Military Times the document itself has no releasable copy.

An A24 documentary pairing Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes with manipulation-prone comedian Nathan Fielder succeeds mainly at making viewers question whether they can trust the film itself, Law & Liberty's review argues

"You Can See Everything," produced by A24, follows Holmes through the final 34 days before her prison sentence and pairs her with Fielder, the Canadian comedian known for tricking and manipulating people on camera, Law & Liberty's review says. The review argues the nearly three-hour film succeeds less as a conventional documentary that imparts information than as an exercise in audience manipulation, inviting viewers to spend the runtime questioning whether they can trust Holmes, Fielder, the filmmakers, or the documentary form itself.

Law & Liberty

The church has a duty to address civil authorities even where Christians are a small minority, Nathanael Devlin writes, challenging secularism's claim to be a neutral public square

Writing at American Reformer, Nathanael Devlin — senior pastor of Beverly Heights Presbyterian Church in Pittsburgh — opens with missionary theologian Lesslie Newbigin's line that "even where the church is a tiny minority with no political power, it has the duty to address the governing authority of the civil community with the word of God." Devlin argues secularism's promise to protect believers' and unbelievers' freedom alike by fostering religious tolerance is itself a claim about ultimate truth, not a neutral starting point, and that the church cannot accept that framing without surrendering its voice in public life.
