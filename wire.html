USNI: Abraham Lincoln makes Guam after 302 days; first U.S. soil in nine months

Defense News: Coast Guard Arctic chief is watching Chinese research ships off Alaska

Defense News, Monday via Reuters: Rear Adm. Bob Little, commander of the U.S. Coast Guard’s Arctic District, said the United States is watching China’s presence in its Arctic zone very closely. He told Reuters four Chinese research vessels have worked offshore in the U.S. Arctic so far in 2026, versus five in all of 2025, including waters off Alaska, the Aleutians, the Bering Strait, and as far north as the Pole. The ships stayed in international waters and did not give advance diplomatic notice, he said.

USNI News, Monday: the head of the Royal Navy called for Britain to expand its maritime capabilities and strengthen the U.K.-led Joint Expeditionary Force in preparation for a potential conflict with Russia in the North Atlantic. He said last week the reason is simple: Russia is the greatest threat. Speech, not a new NATO tasking.

Defense News: Denmark says a Greenland security deal puts NATO on Arctic watch

Defense News, Monday via Reuters: Denmark, Greenland, and the United States are set to sign a Greenland security agreement Tuesday at the U.N. General Assembly. Danish Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen told TV2 the Arctic would not be left solely to Copenhagen or Washington, and that NATO would largely look after the region. The text is not public. President Trump said Friday the deal grants the United States permanent control; Denmark has not answered that claim.
