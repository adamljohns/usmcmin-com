An Idaho Guard Black Hawk hoisted an injured hiker out of country no vehicle can reach

The Idaho Army National Guard said an HH-60 Black Hawk crew hoisted a 46-year-old woman with a broken ankle near Goose Creek Trail, southwest of Brundage Ski Resort, and flew her to McCall Airport. McCall Fire then took her to St. Luke's McCall Medical Center. Meadows Valley Fire and EMS Chief Doug Buys said the ground is so remote that even a dirt bike cannot get in, and that a previous rescue near the same spot took seven to eight hours. Riggins Emergency Medical Services also assisted. The Guard said this was the second remote rescue it has flown for that department in as many months, after a September hoist near Council Mountain. It operates the only hoist-capable helicopters in the state and has completed 17 search-and-rescue missions this year, four of them in October. New Meadows has fewer than 1,000 people and sits about 120 miles north of Boise.

The Navy accepted a new landing craft, LCAC 118, from Textron on Oct. 28

The Navy's Portfolio Acquisition Executive for Maritime said the service accepted Ship-to-Shore Connector Landing Craft Air Cushion 118 from Textron Systems on Oct. 28. It is the fourth connector delivered this calendar year. Capt. Chris Causee, program manager for the Amphibious Assault and Connectors Program Office, said the craft moves the Navy closer to the ship-to-shore capability Sailors and Marines need. The craft completed acceptance trials by the Board of Inspection and Survey before delivery. The program is replacing the legacy landing-craft fleet. LCACs 119 through 131 are under construction. The new craft keeps the old well-deck fit, carries a 60- to 75-ton payload, and moves vehicles, cargo, and assault troops across open water and onto the beach.

Ukraine says its share of a new missile shield is done, and the partners still owe parts

Defense News reported Friday that President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine has finished its share of Freyja, a multinational antiballistic shield, and that partner countries still have to deliver the remaining components in time for initial tests by year's end. Fire Point is building the FP-7.X interceptor and launcher. Norway's Kongsberg and Germany's Diehl Defence are responsible for parts that find, track, and guide the missile. Zelenskyy told journalists on Oct. 17 that ten participating countries are still in process. The report said Ukraine is short of Patriot interceptors while Russia fields faster weapons, including jet-powered Shahed drones.

An essay says Washington Irving taught America to see its own ghosts

Law & Liberty, Friday: Michael Lucchese writes that the United States is marking 250 years of independence and still tells ghost stories the way older countries do. He traces that habit to Washington Irving, whom he calls the founder of American literature. Irving, the essay says, took the Romantic manner he met abroad, especially in Sir Walter Scott, and looked for haunted ground in American history and landscape. Lucchese says The Legend of Sleepy Hollow, set in 1790, is still read because it is also an early picture of American identity, haunted by the Revolutionary fighting in the Hudson Valley.

A review says the Roman world still feels alien because Christianity rewired the West

First Things, Friday: a review of Robert Harris’s Agrippa opens from Tom Holland’s Dominion, published seven years ago, and Holland’s claim that Christianity rewired the Western mind. Holland has said he grew up on the side of the empires in the Bible stories, and that Greek gods seemed more interesting than the Christian God, until writing about Greeks, Romans, and Spartans made their morality feel alien: little pity, little concern for the poor, nothing like a doctrine of human rights. The review treats that gap as the point of reading Rome now.
