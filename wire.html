USS McFaul is home in Norfolk after an 11-month deployment

Naval Surface Force Atlantic said Friday that the Arleigh Burke-class destroyer USS McFaul returned to Naval Station Norfolk, ending an 11-month deployment to the U.S. 5th and 6th Fleet. The ship left Norfolk on Nov. 25, 2025. During the deployment it used the Aegis Combat System for anti-air warfare, surface warfare, and naval surface fire support, and operated with the Abraham Lincoln, George H.W. Bush, and George Washington carrier strike groups during Operation Epic Fury. Commanding officer Cmdr. Adam Samson said the crew protected the strike group under pressure. The ship is named for Engineman Chief Donald L. McFaul, a SEAL killed in Operation Just Cause in 1989. The official still, taken and posted today, shows sailors manning the rails at the homecoming.

The Army activated a Guam air-defense battalion to replace Task Force Talon

The Army said soldiers of 3rd Battalion, 43rd Air Defense Artillery, took part in an activation ceremony Oct. 16 at Andersen Air Force Base, Guam. The battalion will support the defense of Guam with air and missile defense. Task Force Talon had held that watch since 2013 and deactivated in July. Lt. Col. Joshua Urness, the battalion commander, said the unit now stands up the Army's contribution to the Guam Defense System. USNI News reported Friday that the battalion is meant to meet air and missile threats from China and North Korea. The fold item points at the Army release.

DVIDS

Washington says a Russian satellite shadowed a U.S.-registered radar satellite

Defense News reported Friday that Undersecretary of State Thomas G. DiNanno told the United Nations on Wednesday that Russia's Cosmos 2612 maneuvered dangerously close to ICEYE-X36, a U.S.-registered radar satellite whose operator supplies imagery to Ukraine. The Russian satellite spent more than two weeks closing in, then shadowed the American craft from as close as a third of a mile through the weekend of Oct. 17. DiNanno called the behavior consistent with on-orbit weapons operations. Space historian Jonathan McDowell said he believes it is the first unapproved rendezvous of two active satellites in low Earth orbit.

Keen Sword is still running, with Theodore Roosevelt in the Philippine Sea

U.S. Forces Japan said Keen Sword 27 opened Oct. 19 and runs through Oct. 29, with about 45,000 Japan Self-Defense Force personnel, 11,000 U.S. troops, 260 aircraft, and 40 ships, including the Theodore Roosevelt Carrier Strike Group. USNI News said Friday the strike group was still in the Philippine Sea as of Wednesday. The fold item points at the official exercise release.
