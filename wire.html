Pacific Command hosted Micronesia's president at Camp Smith

Lt. Gen. George B. Rowell, deputy commander of U.S. Pacific Command, hosted Federated States of Micronesia President Wesley W. Simina at Camp H.M. Smith on Oct. 9. The DVIDS release says the two sides discussed maritime security, cybersecurity, and long-range threats in the Indo-Pacific, and the work of strengthening deterrence under the Compact of Free Association. No new treaty or deployment order is in the release.

American Reformer says Cornell's sex scandal is a failure of consent rules

Thursday's essay says the facts released so far, including a signed statement from the woman, describe consensual conduct, not rape. The writer argues Cornell encourages sexual liberation and then punishes students when the results become a public problem. Consent, the piece says, is too thin a rule to govern relations between young men and women. It is an argument about university policy, not a court filing.

Law & Liberty says a forgotten Washington statue in Budapest was paid for by immigrants

Friday's essay says the George Washington statue beside a lake in Budapest's City Park was commissioned and financed by Hungarian immigrants in the United States, not by a treaty between governments. The Paris and London statues were diplomatic gifts. This one was raised by people who had left Hungary. The writer puts the story against the current argument over immigration and assimilation, on the 250th anniversary of the country's birth. It is an essay, not a bill.

Tech / AI

The Pentagon signed a $6.3 billion deal to replenish SM-3 interceptors

Defense News reported Friday that the Pentagon signed a five-year contract with Raytheon, plus two option years, for a continuous supply of Standard Missile-3 Block IB interceptors, built mainly in Tucson and Huntsville. Raytheon President Phil Jasper said the missile has been used in combat and the contract is meant to refill inventories. The Block IB is a hit-to-kill interceptor for short- and intermediate-range ballistic missiles. Navy destroyers first fired it in combat in 2024 against Iranian missiles aimed at Israel. Delivery totals are not in the story.

Defense News

The Marine Corps reactivated its oldest flying squadron at Cherry Point

A DVIDS still from Oct. 9 shows Sgt. Maj. Ricardo L. Santos marching the colors at an activation ceremony for Marine Fighter Attack Squadron 231 at Marine Corps Air Station Cherry Point. The caption says the squadron was first commissioned in 1919, and that the ceremony marked a step in 2nd Marine Aircraft Wing's aviation modernization. The photo is the official record posted so far. A separate news release with the aircraft type was not on the page.

First Things says phone gambling trains a man to bet against his neighbor

Joshua Topham's Friday essay starts from a semiannual conference of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, where D. Todd Christofferson said gambling is morally wrong and asked members to leave it. The piece names three markets feeding the habit: sports betting, casino-style games, and prediction markets, all now on a phone. Christofferson's point, as Topham reads it, is not only the loss of money. It is what the habit does to how a man sees the person next to him.

WORLD reviews John Mark Comer's book on silence

WORLD's Wednesday review of Into the Quiet says Comer is doing with noise what his earlier books did with hurry: naming a cultural habit and asking Christians to step out of it. The review quotes him saying this is the first generation that can banish quiet from ordinary life. He prescribes solitude, silence, and stillness. The book is 304 pages from WaterBrook. It is a review, not a new study.
