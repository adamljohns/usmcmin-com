USNI: Marines rehearse moving an air-defense radar in the Philippines

USNI, Thursday from Manila: the Marine Corps is forward-deploying an expeditionary air-defense radar in the Philippines as Manila modernizes coastal defense. A Marine Corps release says Marine Rotational Force – Southeast Asia tested the mobility of an AN/TPS-80 Ground/Air Task-Oriented Radar on Aug. 17. The set is a medium-range, 360-degree radar. 1st Lt. Arturo Perez, the air command-and-control officer in charge, said the work is about taking slack out of setup so U.S. and Philippine forces can share airspace. The Corps did not publish a permanent site.

Defense News, Thursday, citing a Pentagon Terms of Reference seen by Reuters: a review of U.S. troop deployments in Europe will produce at least four sets of options for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth by Nov. 6, ahead of a December completion date. USNI: Marines publish Ground Combat Element 2040

The Marine Corps released Ground Combat Element 2040 on Wednesday; USNI posted the document Thursday. Lt. Gen. Jay Bargeron told reporters the plan keeps conventional infantry at the center even as littoral regiments get the headlines. It adds a fifth infantry company for reconnaissance and fires, converts anti-armor gunners toward a precision-strike job with loitering munitions, and puts more mortars and a heavy-machine-gun platoon in the battalion. Bargeron said squad Marines should be able to find and strike targets out to about 20 kilometers. The 23-page paper does not change the MOS title.

Defense News: Japan orders mass production of 3D-printed interceptor drones

Defense News, Thursday from Manila: Japan’s Acquisition, Technology and Logistics Agency signed a mass-production deal for Terra B1, a rocket-launched interceptor drone, after a Maritime Self-Defense Force demonstration last month. ATLA did not publish the unit count or the price. The May tender sought drones that can stop one-way attack drones such as Shahed-type aircraft. CEO Toru Tokushige said 3D printing shortens prototyping and lets the company spread production so one strike does not halt the line. Tokyo wants a large unmanned fleet by 2027.

Marines begin fielding Squad Aiming Lasers at School of Infantry-East

Headquarters Marine Corps, from Camp Lejeune: Program Manager Ground Weapons Systems began fielding the Squad Aiming Laser to School of Infantry-East on July 27–28. Harry Mandeles, the optics project officer, called the near-infrared beam a generational leap from the PEQ-16, with less eye strain through night-vision goggles. The set is about three ounces lighter and nearly a half-inch shorter, mounted on the M27. Defense News notes a February 2025 award to Envision Technology worth $249 million through 2030. The Corps did not publish a fleet-wide fielding date.

Law & Liberty: Fellowes treats the Gilded Age as gold, not only glitter

Law & Liberty, Thursday: John O. McGinnis reads Julian Fellowes’s HBO series The Gilded Age against Mark Twain’s insult. The essay says the show’s railroad builder, the founding of the Metropolitan Opera, and a footman’s patented alarm clock argue that new fortunes unsettled old New York and still left public goods — cheaper transport, more seats, a working patent system. McGinnis contrasts that with Succession’s zero-sum heirs. It is a reading of a drama, not a claim that the 1870s had no graft.
