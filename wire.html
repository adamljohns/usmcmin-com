European Command told troops and families to watch for suspicious behavior and cover their digital tracks

Defense News, Friday: U.S. European Command branded the push "See Something, Do Something" and said it is meant to build a proactive security mindset. The command told troops, family members, and civilian employees to stay alert in crowded places, report unusual behavior or unattended items, review social-media privacy settings, turn off geotagging, and vary commute routes. Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, the commander, said personnel and families are the priority. Defense News reports the command did not name a specific threat that prompted the campaign.

About 350 U.S. troops and more than 400 Colombian soldiers opened Exercise Southern Vanguard in Colombia

Defense News, Friday, from an Army release this week: the exercise, the 41st iteration, runs through mid-November and is hosted by the Colombian Army and led by U.S. Army Western Hemisphere Command. After Monday's opening, the drills cover jungle warfare, counter-IED work, command-post training, maritime interdiction, cyber skills, and ground security. Brig. Gen. Edilberto Cortés of the Colombian National Army welcomed the participants and called the gathering a shared commitment to collective readiness. Medical evacuation drills were planned from Tolemaida to the Cavalry School in Bogotá.

Ukraine's military intelligence says Russia is already using its Rassvet satellite network over Ukraine

Defense News, Friday, citing Ukrainian military intelligence and Reuters: GUR spokesman Andrii Yusov said Russia is using the Rassvet constellation over Ukraine for military purposes, and that coverage is still intermittent because the satellites only pass over at times. GUR says Russia has launched 32 Rassvet satellites this year, in batches in March and July, and plans more launches by the end of 2026. Yusov said the network may let surveillance drones send data in real time and let pilots adjust attack-drone routes in flight. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy last week sanctioned 10 companies tied to the project, including Bureau 1440.

The Air Force wants more than 6,000 aircraft operations a year at four airfields in the Northern Marianas

Defense News, Friday: the Department of the Air Force published a notice of intent to study training at Saipan International Airport, Tinian International Airport, Tinian North Field, and Rota International Airport. The proposal supports Agile Combat Employment, dispersing aircraft so one strike cannot shut the operation down. The study looks at up to 1,200 fixed-wing and 320 rotary-wing operations a year at each field, or an alternative of 1,400 and 384, plus up to 1,000 people and 550,000 gallons of fuel per field. Public meetings are set this month on Saipan, Tinian, and Rota, with written comments due Nov. 29. A draft statement is planned for fall 2027.

A WORLD review says Drummer Boy starts with a real argument and ends with a cheap rescue

WORLD, Friday: the PG-13 film, made by For King & Country and produced by Angel Studios, follows brothers Jeremiah and Benjamin Clem on opposite sides of the Revolutionary War and a slave, Emmaline, caught between them. The reviewer says the mix of war picture, Christmas movie, and contemporary Christian music works more often than not, and that both sides pray to the same God and claim Him. The review then says the film forces a thoughtful story into a generic morality tale, shoving a complex character into a villain role so the ending can deliver an unearned forgiveness. Mild war violence and the cruelty of slavery are in the picture.

A Law & Liberty essay says Norm Macdonald aimed at manufactured seriousness, not at the laugh count

Law & Liberty, Friday: the writer says Judd Apatow's Netflix documentary The Tale of Norm Macdonald is built from archived footage and Macdonald's own narration, and that the humor often starts before the punch line. The essay treats his targets as Hollywood self-importance, therapeutic talk of an authentic self, and political correctness. It notes he lost the Weekend Update chair after he and Jim Downey kept telling O.J. Simpson jokes against NBC's order, and that he defended friends during later cancellation fights. The piece also records his own line that faith seemed like the only salvation and that he did not know how to get it.
