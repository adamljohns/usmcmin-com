U.S., Japanese, Indian and Australian sailors ran joint boarding drills aboard the amphibious transport dock USS John P. Murtha and the Japanese destroyer JS Ise during Malabar 2026, the four-nation naval exercise now underway in the Pacific

Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force sailors assigned to the Hyūga-class helicopter destroyer JS Ise practiced a visit, board, search and seizure drill aboard the San Antonio-class USS John P. Murtha on Nov. 8, and U.S. Navy sailors ran the same drill aboard JS Ise, according to official U.S. Navy photos released this week. Malabar 26 is a combined field training exercise with the Indian Navy, Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, Royal Australian Navy, Royal Australian Air Force and U.S. Navy, built to strengthen partnerships and interoperability among the four militaries in the Indo-Pacific.

California's Supreme Court ruled this year that the state constitution guarantees criminal defendants a right to affordable pretrial bail, a reading that requires trial judges to set bail at a level most defendants can actually pay rather than simply high enough to secure appearance

The ruling marks the second time in recent years the California Supreme Court has read new rights into the state constitution's bail clause, according to a Heritage Foundation legal analysis, which argues the court is substituting its own policy preference for the text judges are supposed to interpret. The piece contends that by requiring affordability as a constitutional floor rather than leaving it to the legislature, the court has effectively rewritten pretrial release standards for every courtroom in the state.

Heritage Foundation

November's lopsided midterm losses for Republicans — a roughly 30-seat net swing in the House and at least seven Senate seats — reflect something deeper than anger over gas and grocery prices, according to a conservative commentary, which reads the results as a sign of spiritual drift among voters

A TruthScript column contends that despite widespread complaints about Iran-war-driven fuel costs and high grocery prices under the current administration, a larger share of Americans voted for the same policy direction they had rejected two years earlier, and argues that outcome says more about the nation's underlying convictions than about any single issue. The piece calls on Christians to read the results as a call to clearer public witness rather than purely a political setback.

TruthScript

The American Declaration of Independence's ideas of popular sovereignty and consent were not simply exported abroad but reshaped by the nations that adopted them, taking on new meanings inside each country's own constitutional tradition

Drawing an analogy to biological homeostasis, a Providence essay argues constitutional orders influenced by 1776 maintained their own political identity even as they absorbed the Declaration's language of equality, consent and legitimate government, rather than simply copying the American model wholesale. The piece frames this as a 250th-anniversary meditation on why the Declaration's ideas proved durable enough to travel while still being adapted rather than imposed.

Japan's Defense Minister said Tuesday that suspected Russian aircraft violated Japanese territorial airspace twice last Thursday, prompting Japan to scramble fighter jets in response

Between 4:07 and 4:10 a.m. Thursday, one suspected Russian aircraft violated Japanese airspace northwest of Okinoshima, according to USNI News, citing the defense minister's Tuesday announcement; Japan responded by scrambling fighter aircraft. The incident is the latest in a string of airspace incursions Tokyo has logged against Russian military aircraft operating near Japanese territory.

The Trump administration formally nominated Gen. Christopher LaNeve, the Army's acting chief of staff, to hold that post permanently, filling a role left vacant since Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth fired Gen. Randy George in April

A Monday congressional notice moved to confirm LaNeve, who has served as acting chief of staff while also holding the vice chief of staff post, according to Defense News; the administration separately nominated Lt. Gen. Marcus Evans to take over as vice chief. Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle called LaNeve 'the right leader at the right time to modernize the Army,' and the nominations arrive after the Army has gone months without Senate-confirmed top civilian or uniformed leadership, following former Army Secretary Dan Driscoll's September resignation amid reported tension with Hegseth.

Culture

The uncensored director's cut of Ken Russell's 1971 film 'The Devils' is drawing praise from critics as a triumph over censorship, but a First Things essay argues the film is less a warning against authoritarianism than a celebration of its own competing, anti-religious creed

The essay argues 'The Devils,' re-released in theaters after decades of censorship fights, invites audiences to cast whatever authority they resent today as the villain in a seventeenth-century French witch-trial story, while itself demanding the same uncritical devotion it claims to oppose. The piece concludes the film leaves viewers with no positive vision of the freedom it celebrates, ending on an image of abandonment rather than resolution.

First Things

A Missouri congregation's drive-through live Nativity, started as a pandemic-era improvisation with cardboard sets and an unruly camel, is still drawing carloads of visitors to a church pasture each December six years later

Doug and Kim Chatman built the production at their Liberty, Mo., church so services could continue when indoor gatherings were restricted, narrating the story of Jesus' birth across a series of live scenes as visitors drove through, according to WORLD. The event proved popular enough to outlast the pandemic restrictions that started it, now requiring about 30 volunteers each performance and standing as one answer to why a live, in-person telling of the Christmas story still draws families in a digital age.
