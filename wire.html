The Pentagon put $450 million into a U.S. tungsten producer

The Department of War said Wednesday it is putting $450 million of redeemable preferred equity into The Elmet Group, through the Industrial Base Analysis and Sustainment program, to expand domestic tungsten processing. The release says Elmet is the only U.S.-owned, fully integrated producer of tungsten and molybdenum and that it supports more than 100 defense programs, including the F-35, Patriot PAC-3, and Virginia- and Columbia-class submarines. The money will also stand up the only independent ammonium paratungstate plant in North America. Defense News notes a federal rule that bars tungsten mined or processed in China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea from defense use is due Jan. 1, and that the United States has not mined tungsten since 2015. Official still is a Patriot interceptor resupply in the Middle East on Sept. 8, not the plant.

Ukraine’s former defense minister wants battlefield robots in six months

Defense News, Wednesday, from Lviv: former Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov told a tech conference Saturday that Ukraine needs what he called a new Manhattan Project for ground robots. He told Defense News he wants a first confirmed battlefield result from a humanoid-like robot within six months, and within a year an assault in which robots take the most dangerous parts of a mission with no infantry beside them. He named three lines of work: sensors and robots, cheaper anti-Shahed missiles, and AI strike drones and missiles. He said he will start by backing companies whose weapons have already been tested, not by funding a long research program. A proposal, not a signed U.S. contract.

The Navy picked Boeing to build the next carrier fighter

Defense News, Tuesday night: Boeing was selected to build the Navy’s sixth-generation carrier fighter, called F/A-XX, over Northrop Grumman. The contract is valued at $20 billion for the full-scale development phase and buys test aircraft for ground, airworthiness, systems, and weapons testing. Under Secretary Michael Duffey said the jet is meant to dominate contested airspace and extend the reach of the carrier. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao called it a generational leap. The program does not yet have an official designation. USNI reported the same Pentagon announcement Tuesday evening. The art Defense News ran is a Boeing concept rendering, not a photograph of an airplane.

Law & Liberty says Fulton Sheen belongs to the friends of political freedom

Law & Liberty, Wednesday: the essay marks the Catholic Church’s beatification of Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen on Sept. 24, the last step before canonization. The writer says Sheen’s radio and television work, including the 1950s program Life Is Worth Living, made hard ideas accessible, and that readers who are not Catholic should still weigh what he argued for political freedom. Essay, not a Vatican decree and not a vote.
