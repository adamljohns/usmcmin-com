USNI News

Secretary of War Pete Hegseth ordered a sweeping overhaul of America's senior military colleges, scrapping the written thesis for war-gaming

USNI News and Stars and Stripes, Monday: Hegseth announced 33 directives at the Naval War College in Newport, R.I., aimed at what he called ending war colleges' avoidance of hard questions and realigning them with warfighting. "We will evaluate our educational institutions by a singular standard: Does this program forge leaders who will win America's wars?" he told students and faculty. The directives drop the 80-page written thesis as the capstone project in favor of AI-aided war-gaming and high-stress decision exercises, create new "warfighter chair" positions filled by O-5 and O-6 commanders fresh from deployment, and end lifetime tenure for newly hired civilian faculty in favor of three-year performance contracts. Hegseth also directed the colleges to stop treating recent strategic failures, including the Afghanistan withdrawal and the military's COVID-19 mandate, as subjects too sensitive to study.

Political

France ordered two new spy satellites for more than 1.5 billion euros to keep its own eyes on the world past 2035

Defense News, Tuesday: France's Directorate General of Armament signed off on the IRIS program, building two next-generation military Earth-observation satellites from Airbus and Thales Alenia Space to replace the current CSO constellation. The new satellites will take high-resolution images day and night in visible and infrared light; France is one of the few European NATO members running its own high-end optical spy satellites rather than leaning on U.S. intelligence, and President Emmanuel Macron said in January France supplies two-thirds of the imagery intelligence Ukraine receives from allies.

Defense News

The SS United States, once a secret Cold War troop transport, was towed from its Mobile berth ahead of being sunk as a reef off Florida

USNI News, Monday: the storied ocean liner, which doubled as a classified wartime troop transport capable of carrying 15,000 soldiers, was towed from its longtime berth in Mobile, Ala., ship spotters reported, a step toward its planned sinking to form an artificial reef off Florida's coast. Okaloosa County, Fla., bought the hulk in 2024 from a preservation group that had been unable to fund restoring the ship or finding it a permanent berth.

USNI News

Putin's two decades of pro-family rhetoric have served Russia's military buildup more than its families, as a record war budget shows

Providence, Tuesday: the author points to Russia's draft 2027 budget, which sets military spending at 17.1 trillion rubles, 27 percent above the Kremlin's own prior plan and roughly 35 percent of federal spending, a post-Soviet record, even as Putin has spent twenty-five years invoking faith, motherhood and large families in speeches on demography. The piece argues the pattern shows family policy has functioned as cover for imperial ambition rather than a genuine priority, since a government that spends over a third of its budget on war is not one that prizes the cradle over the trench.

Providence

West Virginia's Supreme Court blocked 33 congregations from reviving their suit to leave the United Methodist Church with their buildings

WORLD, Monday: in a Nov. 4 decision, the state's high court barred a lower court from reopening the churches' lawsuit against the UMC's West Virginia Conference, ruling that resolving it would require a civil judge to interpret church law, which the state and federal constitutions forbid. The 33 congregations sued in 2024 alleging fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, breach of contract and conspiracy, saying conference officials told them they did not qualify under the denomination's temporary disaffiliation rule, which expired in 2023, and could leave after the 2024 General Conference instead. That conference removed the UMC's bans on same-sex marriage and LGBTQ clergy, and the churches were then unable to leave with their property. More than 7,600 churches left the denomination from 2019 through 2023, according to UM News. The conference welcomed the ruling as a reaffirmation of church self-governance; the churches' lawyers had not responded to WORLD.

Tech / AI

Christian scholars at a 650-person AI conference are adopting the tools faster than they are willing to admit using them

First Things, Tuesday: reporting from the Wisdom in the Age of AI conference at Calvin University, the author found Christian scholars and professionals broadly as comfortable with AI as the general public, even as a recent Barna poll shows Christians adopting AI faster than the overall population. In one session, a presenter asked roughly seventy attendees how many keep generative AI out of their writing; only seven hands went up, even though many of the scholarly journals and periodicals those same attendees publish in ban or restrict AI-generated work.

Culture

The West's stagnating economies keep blaming free markets for problems that big government caused

Law & Liberty, Tuesday: the author surveys low growth, high debt and weak productivity across Western economies and notes leaders like British Prime Minister Andy Burnham blame "neo-liberalism," while even a 2024 European Union report from former ECB President Mario Draghi, which identified over-regulation as central to Europe's economic sclerosis, still called for more Brussels-driven regulation and government spending as the fix. The piece argues social democratic policy commitments are now imploding under their own contradictions, and the stagnation will worsen until that mindset's grip on policymakers is acknowledged as the problem rather than the solution.

Law & Liberty

Taking smartphones away will not fix children's mental health without giving them back real independence and free play

Public Discourse, Tuesday: reviewing psychologist Peter Gray's new book "Restoring Childhood," the author agrees that banning smartphones alone will not reverse the decline in children's mental health, but argues smartphones and social media remain part of the problem when compared with what kids actually need, which Gray calls self-directed, child-chosen free play. Gray, a co-founder of the nonprofit Let Grow alongside Jonathan Haidt and others, frames that kind of play as the practice space for adulthood, without which children grow more anxious and less developed, short and long term.
