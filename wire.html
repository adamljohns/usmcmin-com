Defense News

Marines will put anti-ship missiles on Yonaguni for Keen Sword

Defense News reported Monday that the Marine Corps will send its ground-based anti-ship launcher and air-defense system to Yonaguni for exercise Keen Sword, Oct. 19–29. It is the first time those systems go to Japan’s southwesternmost inhabited island, about 110 kilometers from Taiwan. Yonaguni’s local government disclosed the plan Friday. Japan’s Ministry of Defense says nine countries will join 45,000 Self-Defense Force personnel. USS George Washington, which has joined past iterations, is in the Arabian Sea instead.

Pentagon says every B-1 that was at RAF Fairford is back in the United States

A Pentagon spokesman wrote Sunday that all U.S. bombers deployed to RAF Fairford have returned to home stations. A dozen B-1s had been at the Gloucestershire base, used for strikes on Iranian missile sites firing on ships in the Strait of Hormuz. The acknowledgment came a week after arrests at the base on suspicion of preparing terrorist acts. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Wednesday that Britain has strong indications Iran was involved. Tehran called the claim baseless.

Defense News

Army counted 378 drone crossings on the southern border in 30 days

Defense News reported Monday that the Army tested radars, interceptor drones, and gun systems at Falcon Peak in Arizona last month. Brig. Gen. Brian Filler, deputy commander for operations at Joint Task Force Southern Border, told reporters the military detected 378 drone incursions in the previous 30 days, mostly commercial aircraft used for surveillance alongside smuggling. The military said earlier this month it had downed 11 cartel drones with lasers since August.

Monday’s fleet chart puts Theodore Roosevelt in the Pacific and Mason in 7th Fleet

USNI News posted the Oct. 5 Fleet and Marine Tracker with approximate positions of deployed carrier strike groups and amphibious ready groups. The chart’s accompanying Navy still shows sailors on USS Theodore Roosevelt (CVN-71) working the flight deck. USS Mason (DDG-87), homeported at Naval Station Mayport, is listed in U.S. 7th Fleet.

Heritage says the Education Department is finally tightening student-loan rules

Monday’s commentary argues that student loans have been a millstone for college graduates, and that the Department of Education is enacting reforms meant to keep more young people out of that debt. It notes the irony that some colleges, the institutions that exist to educate students, have resisted the change.

Tech / AI

Germany will stand up its first drone regiment in 2027

Defense Minister Boris Pistorius said Oct. 1 at the Bergen training area that ground forces will begin forming the regiment next year. Army Chief Lt. Gen. Christian Freuding put the first footprint at 300 to 350 troops, growing toward 600, with full operational capability targeted for 2029. The unit is assigned to the 10th Panzer Division. A permanent base and the long-range platform contracts are not yet set.

Law & Liberty asks what is left of the doctor if an algorithm is the better diagnostician

Monday’s essay takes a JAMA argument that AI could soon outperform physicians at much of medicine’s cognitive work, and that putting a doctor back into those decisions could make care worse. It grants the coordination case, then asks what the patient loses when the person who treats him is no longer the one who decides.

Public Discourse makes the case for waiting before a new device enters the house

Monday’s essay argues that most households cannot drop off the grid, but they can wait before buying the next device. The writer treats those small delays as a way to use technology instead of being used by it, and to count the loss that comes with the gain.
