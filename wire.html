U.S. and Colombian troops ran an air assault and insertion exercise at Tolemaida as Southern Vanguard 27 moved into its second week

A DVIDS photo release Saturday showed South Carolina Army National Guard UH-60 Black Hawks from the 1-111th Aviation Regiment dropping troops for an air assault and insertion exercise with the Colombian National Army on Nov. 7 at Tolemaida, Colombia. Southern Vanguard 27 is the 41st iteration of the bilateral exercise, which opened Nov. 2 and runs into mid-November with about 800 total participants, including roughly 350 U.S. troops, Defense News reported Friday. The exercise is built to strengthen security cooperation, build rapid-deployment readiness, and improve interoperability between the two militaries, part of what U.S. forces describe as a commitment to a stable Western Hemisphere.

Political

Virginia voters approved a constitutional abortion amendment Tuesday, part of what pro-life groups are calling their worst midterm setback in years

First Things reported Thursday that Susan B. Anthony Pro-Life America president Marjorie Dannenfelser called the Nov. 3 midterm results a 'disaster for the unborn' in a memo issued the day after the election, warning Democrats are on track to control two of the three levers needed to legalize taxpayer-funded abortion nationwide once they take office in January. Virginia's Question 1, a constitutional amendment protecting abortion through the second trimester with broad third-trimester exceptions, passed with 62.4 percent of the vote; the Founding Freedoms Law Center has an ongoing suit arguing the ballot question's wording was materially misleading. Nevada voters approved a similar amendment, Question 6, with 63.4 percent, while Missouri's Amendment 3, which would have repealed the state's 2024 abortion-protection amendment, failed, and Idaho voters rejected a measure to legalize abortion until birth.

Britain and France lead 2,800 troops from 11 nations into Poland Monday to rehearse a postwar Ukraine deployment

Defense News reported Saturday that the exercise, KOALICJA 26.2, runs Nov. 9 to 22 across Poland, France, and Turkey under the Coalition of the Willing's military arm, the Multinational Force-Ukraine. American troops will not take part. A British Ministry of Defence statement says the drills combine air, land, and sea forces to test how to move, protect, and supply a force that would help rebuild Ukraine's armed forces once the fighting ends. Belgium, Estonia, Germany, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Spain, Sweden, Turkey, and Britain are also taking part alongside France. Defense News reports U.S., Russian, and Ukrainian negotiators are set to meet in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10 and 11.

A First Things essay argues social media influencers are becoming the 19th-century party boss for a new political era

First Things, Friday: the essay notes newly elected Michigan Sen. Abdul El-Sayed, the first Muslim U.S. senator, won a grueling Democratic primary with campaign support from social media influencer Hasan Piker, whose actual impact on the race was hotly debated. A Providence review says a new book on Israel's war cabinet shows the country clawed back a stronger strategic position after the October 7 failure

Providence, Friday: the review assesses In the War Room: The Inside Story of Israel's Fight Against Hamas and the Iranian Axis, by Yonah Jeremy Bob of the Jerusalem Post and Elliot Kaufman of the Wall Street Journal, built on interviews with Prime Minister Netanyahu, then-Defense Minister Yoav Gallant, and Cabinet Minister Benny Gantz. The book traces Israel's path from the October 2023 Hamas attack through the Hezbollah leadership strikes of September 2024, the killing of Hamas's Yahya Sinwar that October, the June 2025 strike on Iran's nuclear program, and the broader Iran war, arguing Israel emerged from the catastrophe with a stronger military-strategic position than it has held in decades. The authors fault Israeli overconfidence in surveillance technology and partisan political conflict for the initial failure, and describe the Biden administration's wartime diplomacy with Israel as inconsistent.

Tech / AI

Ukraine says Russia is already using its homegrown Rassvet satellite network for military communications to sharpen strike-drone accuracy

Defense News, Friday, citing Ukraine's Main Intelligence Directorate (GUR): Russia has launched 32 Rassvet satellites this year in two batches, in March and July, and plans more launches by the end of 2026 to expand coverage toward a full low-orbit communications network. GUR spokesman Andrii Yusov said the constellation currently gives only intermittent coverage over Ukraine as satellites pass overhead, well short of the continuous coverage SpaceX's Starlink/Starshield network gives Kyiv. Starshield operates in Ukraine but not Russia, and Russia's own Scientific and Expert Council has urged faster Rassvet deployment, warning of the risks Starlink poses to Moscow's forces. Russia's roughly 32 satellites remain a tiny fraction of Starlink's network of more than 10,000.

A TruthScript essay turns to Absalom's rebellion against David to make sense of a disastrous Election Day for conservatives

TruthScript, Thursday: the essay points Christians to 2 Samuel 15:1-6, where Absalom stationed himself at the city gate to flatter and win the loyalty of Israelites with grievances against King David's court, as a parallel for how political movements can win hearts through flattery rather than truth. A Law & Liberty review recovers Alexander Hamilton's forgotten opposition to the French Revolution's radicalism

Law & Liberty, Thursday: reviewer Michael Lucchese writes that Hamilton, best remembered as a war hero and architect of the American economy, quickly broke from many of his countrymen's early enthusiasm for the French Revolution, seeing how its radicalism differed from the American Founding. Unlike European reactionaries, Hamilton never abandoned his republicanism or commitment to natural rights, arguing instead, like Edmund Burke, that religion and self-government mutually reinforce a free and responsible society. The review centers on a re-released collection of Hamilton's 1790s writings edited by the late Morton J. Frisch, published by the American Enterprise Institute for the nation's 250th anniversary.
