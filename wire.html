Military Times

New program lets troops train as military chaplains without leaving the service

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the Chaplain In-Service Procurement Program on Friday, and the Navy issued its implementing message the same day, Military Times reports. Service members who feel called to ministry can pursue graduate theological education while staying in uniform. Officers need two to eight years of active duty and a rank of O-3 or below. Enlisted applicants need four to 10 years and at least the rank of E-5. Each military department can fund up to 25 new participants a fiscal year, with the Navy and Marine Corps sharing one cap. Participants owe two years of active duty for each year of training, up to six. The Navy has not yet announced application dates or selection board schedules.

Political

Virginia votes until 7 p.m. on three constitutional amendments, a U.S. Senate seat and 11 House seats

The Department of Elections lists Sen. Mark Warner, a Democrat, against Republican Bert Mizusawa. In the 7th District, which includes Fredericksburg, Democratic Rep. Eugene Vindman faces Republican Doug Ollivant, Libertarian Taner Demirci Lopez and independents Alaha Ahrar, Joshua Ertle and Randall Terry. Question 1 would protect decisions on abortion, birth control and fertility care, while allowing third-trimester abortion restrictions with exceptions. Question 2 would remove the state ban on same-sex marriage. Question 3 would restore voting rights automatically on release from incarceration. Polls are open 6 a.m. to 7 p.m., and anyone in line by 7 may vote.

Navy's top enlisted leader says the newest deployed strike group was told to expect 10 to 11 months

Master Chief Petty Officer of the Navy John Perryman said last week that shorter deployments are unlikely given what the fleet has available, Military.com reports, citing remarks first reported by Forbes. Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Daryl Caudle said sailors will accept extensions when they know how long they will last. He tied the strain to the Iran war, saying it began without planning in the fiscal 2026 budget and that the Navy needs "a year's long recovery." The warning follows the USS Abraham Lincoln's long deployment, during which the Navy confirmed eight crew members attempted suicide.

Tech / AI

Germany warns lawmakers their iPhones may have been targeted by government-backed spyware

Germany's federal cybersecurity office, the BSI, said over the weekend that members of the Bundestag and other notable people received threat notifications from Apple, and that anyone who gets one should assume the device may be compromised. A government spokesman would not say who in the federal government was targeted, TechCrunch reports. Apple does not name governments in these alerts. One recipient's notice said this batch reached users in 99 countries. Who is behind the attacks remains unclear.

TechCrunch

More than 1,000 GitLab servers remain unpatched against a maximum-severity flaw under active attack

CISA added CVE-2026-97229 to its catalog of exploited vulnerabilities on Oct. 29 and gave federal agencies until Nov. 1 to fix it. The bug, in GitLab's CI/CD regular expression compiler, can let an unauthenticated user run code on the server. GitLab released patches Oct. 12. The Shadowserver watchdog said Sunday it tracks over 1,000 instances still exposed, and over 11,300 exposed to a second maximum-severity bug, out of about 39,000 it sees daily.

BleepingComputer

Musk says Grok Bot cost too much to serve after users' weekly limits suddenly maxed out

Some subscribers reported over Halloween weekend that their weekly usage jumped, in some cases from 50% to 100%, even when they had not been using the AI agent overnight. Elon Musk confirmed on X on Sunday that it was no glitch, writing that the cost of serving Grok Bot "was unsustainably high relative to revenue," TechCrunch reports. He has not said how subscribers' limits will work from here.

An 1852 painting of a county election shows a republic's flaws and what still redeems them

Writing for Election Day, Law & Liberty revisits George Caleb Bingham's The County Election. The canvas sets an Irishman swearing an oath near a Revolutionary War veteran coming down the steps, farmers and merchants arguing in the crowd, and a drunken man held up in the voting line. Above them a banner reads "The Will of the People the Supreme Law." The essay argues the abuses Bingham painted never outweigh the good.

High school essayist says endless access to the world's troubles is costing her generation its peace

Abigail Biggins of Massachusetts took second place in Public Discourse's annual high school essay contest. She contrasts today's scrolling with Saint Kateri Tekakwitha, who served the people in front of her with few distractions. Biggins asks her elders for help deciding which burdens are hers to carry, so that caring about the world does not harden into cynicism.
