The guided-missile destroyer USS Michael Murphy came home to Hawaii Thursday after 346 days at sea, among the longest combat deployments of the Iran war, earning a Presidential Unit Citation for the crew

The Arleigh Burke-class destroyer and its embedded helicopter detachment returned to Joint Base Pearl Harbor-Hickam after deploying with the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln last December, the Navy's Commander, U.S. 3rd Fleet said in an official release. The crew earned the Presidential Unit Citation and Combat Action Ribbon for executing dozens of Tomahawk strikes, defending against drone and missile attacks, and completing 12 contested transits of the Strait of Hormuz while commanding a 14-ship coalition surface group as part of Operation Epic Fury. USNI News reported in September that the ship's prior commanding officer, Cmdr. Caitlin Cunningham, was relieved for loss of confidence before Cmdr. David Nicolas took over; Nicolas told reporters dockside Thursday the crew did not experience the kind of acute mental-health crises the Navy has reported elsewhere in the strike group, where eight sailors attempted suicide during the deployment.

A gunman killed one man and wounded four others at a Washington, D.C., gym about a mile from the White House, then took his own life after an hour-long standoff

WORLD, Thursday: Interim Metropolitan Police Chief Jeffery Carroll said officers reached the gym 30 seconds after the first 911 call and found one man dead. While clearing the gym and a neighboring apartment building, they heard more shots from the seventh floor, where the gunman had barricaded himself in an apartment. Negotiators talked with him for more than an hour before a final shot. Police forced entry and found him with what they believe is a self-inflicted wound. Carroll did not name the shooter or a motive. He described him as a white male, and said investigators believe he had a conflict with the apartment building's ownership or management.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO, president and board member after his affair with a Chinese businesswoman set off national security fears and an internal probe

WORLD, Thursday: Nvidia's board announced the resignation days after opening an investigation into allegations that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress takes over on an interim basis while the board searches inside and outside the company for a permanent CEO. Nvidia shares fell more than 7 percent on the news, extending a slide that began when reports of the affair surfaced. The company still plans to report third-quarter earnings Tuesday, Nov. 17. The woman previously worked for Singapore-based Megaspeed, which sells AI computing power. Megaspeed said Thursday it takes U.S. export-control law seriously, will cooperate with the government, and is reviewing the sources of all its graphics processors.

Japan pledged drones and a coastal-defense radar system to the Philippines this week, the largest grant yet under Tokyo's security-assistance program to help Manila watch its coastline against Chinese activity

USNI News reported Friday that Japan's ambassador announced the transfer of unmanned aerial vehicles and a coastal-defense radar system under Tokyo's Official Security Assistance program. Philippine outlet BusinessMirror put the grant's value at roughly 4.3 billion yen, the largest award yet under that program. The handoff follows a string of American and allied equipment transfers to the Philippines this year, including the U.S. sea-drone transfer to the Philippine Marine Corps that led Friday morning's board, as Manila works to track Chinese vessels in the South China Sea without matching Beijing ship-for-ship.

Forgiving one's enemies is close to impossible without first knowing God's forgiveness, John Stonestreet says after Trump told '60 Minutes' he does not forgive those who wrong him

Breakpoint's John Stonestreet, discussing the president's remarks on WORLD Radio's Culture Friday, said forgiveness runs “somewhere between difficult and impossible” and that most people, including the president's critics, claim to forgive without actually doing it. Stonestreet argued Scripture ties the ability to forgive directly to having been forgiven by God, pointing to the Lord's Prayer and the parable of the unforgiving servant, and said the president at least avoided the more common move of claiming a forgiveness he never offers.

The surest way to save competitive balance in college sports may be to limit competition through a bipartisan bill granting the NCAA a conditional antitrust exemption

Attorney Asheesh Agarwal writes that NIL dollars and the transfer portal let wealthy programs stockpile talent year to year, citing Indiana's 51 scholarship transfers and an all-transfer Michigan basketball championship roster, while Group of Five athletic departments spend a fraction of what Power Four programs pay for a single transfer quarterback. He argues the Cruz-Cantwell Protect College Sports Act, which has passed the Senate and has President Trump's backing, would cap revenue sharing, guarantee one penalty-free transfer and five years of eligibility, and require schools to keep funding women's and Olympic sports — trading some free-market purity for a competitive landscape that keeps smaller programs viable.
