Coast Guard icebreaker Storis is back in Seattle after 133 days as the only U.S. surface ship in the High Arctic, a patrol that included the first polar-icebreaker law-enforcement mission in more than 20 years

Storis returned to its temporary home port at Coast Guard Base Seattle on Saturday after covering more than 17,000 nautical miles, according to the official Coast Guard release posted Tuesday. On Operation Frontier Sentinel the cutter spent more than 300 hours watching foreign-flagged research ships and tracked four Chinese-flagged research vessels over the U.S. extended continental shelf. The crew also ran the first live-fire gunnery exercise by a Coast Guard surface ship in ice-covered Arctic water in decades, boarded fishing vessels in the Bering Sea with NOAA, and sailed with Canadian and U.S. cutters on Operation Tundra Merlin.

Political

A walk through old European squares left one writer arguing that a good statue is hard to find because the squares still belong to poets, astronomers, and philosophers

A Heritage commentary published Monday says what stayed with the writer was not the cathedrals or the cafes. It was the company the bronze kept: the squares belonged to poets and playwrights, astronomers and philosophers, figures in stone. The piece treats public statues as a record of who a city still thinks is worth standing with, not as decoration.

Heritage Foundation

Vice President J.D. Vance is wrong to blame the Iran war itself for the midterm losses, an essay argues: the war was warranted, and the way it was fought was the problem

The essay, published Wednesday morning, takes Vice President J.D. Vance's claim that the war in Iran drove the party's losses and splits the point: the war was warranted, the conduct was not. It argues the White House never made the national-interest case, overpromised a quick end, and stopped operations too soon, then notes that Iran's closing of the Strait of Hormuz showed the cost of leaving the fight unfinished.

Providence

Australia's High Court left in place a ruling that sex in federal discrimination law no longer means biological sex, a Law & Liberty essay says, after the court refused to hear Giggle v. Tickle

The essay, published Wednesday, walks through the Sept. 10 refusal of special leave after a May loss in the Full Federal Court. It traces the change to 2013 amendments that repealed the Sex Discrimination Act's definitions of man and woman and inserted a definition of gender identity, and argues Australia's courts were never meant to manufacture rights the parliament did not pass.

Senate Armed Services Chairman Roger Wicker told Navy secretary nominee Hung Cao that putting steam catapults back on Ford-class carriers is a non-starter

USNI News reported Tuesday night from Washington that Wicker rejected the White House plan to revert Ford-class carriers from electromagnetic catapults to steam, after a defense of President Trump's preference for steam. Cao, acting Navy secretary since April, was before the committee for confirmation the same day.

USNI News

Hanwha fired two Chunmoo missiles from a containerized launcher on a ship off Mississippi, a test of putting a land rocket battery in a shipping box

Defense News reported Tuesday that Hanwha Defense USA launched the missiles Sunday from a test-range vessel operated by the Spaceport Company. The company said the launcher can fire guided and unguided rounds. The report ties the shot to the Navy's containerization push: in April, Adm. Daryl Caudle told the fleet that payloads should come off single-purpose ships and fit on whatever hull can carry a box.

Culture

Augustine's peace of order needs just laws and consent, not the quiet of a police state, an Advent essay argues, and Germany and Japan after 1945 show what imposed order has to grow into

The essay, published Wednesday, takes the City of God's tranquillitas ordinis — the tranquillity of order — and says that kind of peace needs just laws and consent, not the quiet of a police state. It points to Germany and Japan after 1945 as cases where order was imposed after aggression and then had to grow into something freer, and to John Paul II's Centesimus Annus for the claim that politics and markets only work when enough people practice the virtues that hold them.

Two new Oxford books take up C.S. Lewis as Netflix prepares another pass at Narnia, including a scientist's look at how Lewis answered the claim that science and religion cannot share a truce

The piece, dated Monday, notes the steady trade in Lewis books and says upcoming Netflix adaptations have drawn more argument about what it means to get him right. It focuses on Alister McGrath's Science and Religion in the Imagination of C.S. Lewis, from a writer who started in molecular biophysics, and on Lewis's answer to the claim that science and religion cannot share a truce.
