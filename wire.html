Defense News

Military analysts say China has sent an outsized share of its far-seas naval deployments toward Japan's southernmost islands since a political dispute erupted in November 2025, gradually normalizing a Chinese naval presence close to Japanese waters

Taiwan's National Security Council tracked more than 100 Chinese military-linked vessels near the island in early November, and experts told Defense News the scale of that flotilla may have had Japan in its sights as well. They trace the shift to a dispute that began when Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi told a House of Representatives committee that a Chinese naval blockade or armed conflict over Taiwan could be a 'survival-threatening situation' for Japan. Stephen Nagy of the Japan Institute for International Affairs said there is 'a direct link' between Takaichi's statement and the quality and quantity of deployments since, while Katsuya Yamamoto of the Sasakawa Peace Foundation said China is 'gradually normalizing' naval activity within the first island chain through live-fire drills, drone missions and carrier-group voyages that build a long-term pattern of accepted presence without breaching international maritime law, according to Defense News.

Political

California's Supreme Court created a new constitutional right to pretrial release at an affordable bail amount this year, requiring judges to weigh a defendant's ability to pay before setting most non-capital release conditions

Heritage Foundation's legal analysis says the court mistook 'judicial invention for constitutional interpretation' when it held in April that the California Constitution guarantees most criminal defendants a right to pretrial release on bail, and that judges must now set bail at a level defendants can actually afford rather than one tied strictly to flight risk or public safety. The piece argues the ruling effectively rewrites bail policy from the bench rather than through the legislature.

Heritage Foundation

For two decades Vladimir Putin sold Russians a story about traditional values and growing families, but Providence argues Russia's draft 2027 budget — with military spending up 27 percent to 17.1 trillion rubles — tells a different story about where the regime's priorities actually sit

The essay, titled 'It Was Never About Family: Putin's Fodder Doctrine,' argues the Kremlin's pro-natalist rhetoric was always secondary to its willingness to spend heavily on the war in Ukraine and treat Russian lives as expendable toward that end, according to Providence. The piece reads the new budget figures as the clearest evidence yet that family policy was messaging, not priority.

Providence

Embryonic stem cell research's most promising ethical breakthrough ultimately came from old-fashioned moral wisdom about respecting human life at every stage, not from utilitarian calculation of costs and benefits

The essay opens from Pope Leo XIV's new encyclical, which warns against letting ethical decisions be reduced to a 'calculation' of consequences and calls instead for cultivating hearts that 'pursue wisdom rather than immediate results,' then applies that warning to the decade-long U.S. fight over federal funding for research that destroys human embryos to obtain pluripotent stem cells. The piece traces the dispute from the 1995 Dickey Amendment, which still bars such federal funding, through Dr. James Thomson's 1998 breakthrough in culturing embryonic stem cells, arguing the moral case against destroying embryos proved not just ethically sound but scientifically prescient as non-destructive alternatives advanced, according to Public Discourse.

Tech / AI

The United States and Russia look set to oppose a push at a global meeting to open negotiations on legally binding rules for autonomous weapons, even as other major arms producers signal openness to talks

Defense News reports the two countries, among the world's largest arms producers, are positioned to block momentum toward a binding treaty governing autonomous weapons systems at the meeting, continuing a pattern of resistance to formal limits on the technology even as battlefield use of drones and AI-assisted targeting accelerates. Supporters of talks argue the absence of agreed rules leaves a widening gap between the pace of deployment and any shared constraints on it.

Culture

C.S. Lewis fused rigorous argument with imaginative storytelling into a body of apologetics and fiction that still shapes how Christians read, reason and write about faith decades after his death

The piece looks at Lewis's intellectual formation and argues his lasting influence rests on his refusal to separate rigorous argument from imaginative storytelling, a combination the author says is rarer in Christian writing today than many assume, according to WORLD.

WORLD

A chatbot companion built to be endlessly agreeable and always available answers real loneliness with a counterfeit relationship, training users away from the friction that genuine friendship actually requires

The essay imagines the appeal of a companion who is 'awake whenever you are, never grumpy, and always interested in what interests you,' then argues that very design is the danger: it teaches users to expect frictionless affirmation on demand, eroding the patience and mutual sacrifice that real relationships depend on, according to First Things. The piece calls for treating AI companions as a distinct category requiring restriction, not just another app to use in moderation.

First Things

Justice Samuel Alito's new collection of speeches joins a growing shelf of books by sitting Supreme Court justices, testing whether a narrower, occasional-remarks format can still illuminate the biggest constitutional questions of the day

The review traces a tradition running from Chief Justice William Rehnquist's histories of the Court through Antonin Scalia's originalist writings to recent books by Neil Gorsuch, Amy Coney Barrett and Stephen Breyer, then places Alito's 'So Ordered: An Originalist's View of the Constitution, the Court, and the Country' in that company. Unlike most of his predecessors' books, the reviewer notes, Alito's is not built around one unified theme but gathers public speeches he delivered on constitutional, legal, historical and cultural questions over the years — a narrower form the reviewer argues is not, for that reason, any less enlightening, according to Law & Liberty.
