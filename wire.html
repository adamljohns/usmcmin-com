USNI News

U.S. and Philippine Marines rehearsed standing up a mobile coastal radar on a Palawan beach this month, part of a growing bilateral effort to track ships moving through contested South China Sea waters

According to USNI News, Marines with Marine Rotational Force-Southeast Asia and Philippine marines with the 3rd Marine Brigade set up the Ground Based Maritime Systems-Light radar on a beach facing the South China Sea during a maritime strike-targeting drill earlier this month. The exercise was part of Archipelagic Coastal Defense Continuum, a recurring series of bilateral training events that supports the Philippine Marine Corps' Archipelagic Coastal Defense Concept and strengthens interoperability between Philippine and U.S. forces as both militaries track shipping and Chinese activity in the South China Sea.

Political

A surge in homeschooling, classical schools and Christian schools is reshaping American education, and the movement is converging on two 19th- and 20th-century thinkers rather than a single modern curriculum

The essay argues education savings accounts and nonprofits that fund school choice have fueled a rapid expansion of independent schooling, and that two figures increasingly anchor the movement's philosophy: Dorothy Sayers, the mystery novelist and Inklings member who helped revive the classical trivium, and Charlotte Mason, a Lake District education reformer whose idea that "children are born persons" underlies today's emphasis on "living books" over textbook "twaddle." It credits legislators who passed education savings accounts and groups like the Goldwater Institute with making the shift possible, and says new publishers are building curricula directly on Mason's principles.

Law & Liberty

Italy's ban on full-face coverings in schools, aimed at burqas and niqabs while allowing the hijab, raises a question facing Western democracies generally: when does enforcing a shared civic culture cross into suppressing religious minorities

The essay notes a Milan teacher told Al Jazeera she had never actually encountered a student wearing a full-face covering in her career, even as Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government frames the school dress ban as part of a broader integration push. It argues governments have legitimate reasons to require that students be identifiable and able to participate in class, but says officials owe the public an explanation of how such bans actually advance assimilation rather than pushing immigrant communities toward unassimilated enclaves. Drawing on theologian Reinhold Niebuhr's warning that groups defend self-interest in the language of universal principle, the piece frames the controversy as a recurring test for liberal democracies: whether shared civic commitments can be upheld without forcing religious minorities to conform to the majority's way of life.

Tech / AI

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed humans must stay in control of major military decisions, even as the two countries keep racing each other on artificial intelligence

Bessent told CNBC after returning from Trump's meeting with Xi in Shenzhen that the agreement was among the major achievements of the Asia trip, saying both leaders agreed "super intelligence" should not be allowed to launch major military operations without a human being involved, and that the agreement would not affect the two countries' separate competition for an AI edge. He said U.S. labs need to keep their technological lead as the best defense against "dangerous" Chinese AI models already being used in cyberattacks, and that Nvidia's H200 chip remains the most advanced chip China can legally buy under current export limits, with Chinese orders for it running thin as Beijing tries to build its own chip stack.

Defense News

The Congressional Budget Office says the Navy's own fiscal 2027 shipbuilding plan would cost taxpayers about 18 percent more than the Navy itself projects, with a new proposal for 15 nuclear-powered guided-missile battleships driving much of the growth

CBO's analysis, released Nov. 19 and reposted in full by USNI News, estimates the plan covering fiscal years 2027 through 2056 would average $53.8 billion a year in 2026 dollars, including $48.9 billion annually for new-ship construction — about 41 percent above the shipbuilding budget of the last five years, according to CBO's own published summary. CBO has found in prior years that Navy shipbuilding plans systematically understate their true cost, and this year's plan is notable for adding, for the first time, a proposal to build 15 nuclear-powered guided-missile battleships, a new ship class that would significantly add to the cost of the Navy's large-surface-combatant program.

Culture

America's tradition of presidential Thanksgiving proclamations, running back to George Washington, reflects a distinctly American habit of treating national gratitude as a civic duty owed across political and religious lines

The commentary, from The Heritage Foundation, argues Thanksgiving is honored so widely across American political and religious lines that its deeper meaning is rarely examined, tracing the holiday's roots through a long line of presidential proclamations calling citizens to gratitude as a shared civic practice rather than a partisan or sectarian one. It frames the tradition as a case study in how a broadly popular holiday can still carry serious intrinsic meaning worth recovering each year, even as it has become primarily a day of family gatherings and football.

Heritage Foundation

Pope Leo XIV's recent journey home to Peru, Argentina and Uruguay showed a pontiff developing his predecessor's emphasis on evangelization while reading it through a sharper, more explicitly Christ-centered lens

The essay notes the trip came after the pope's surgery to remove a cancerous growth on his lung, and that Leo repeatedly cited Pope Francis's teaching, especially the exhortation Evangelii Gaudium, while addressing civil authorities, youth, bishops and residents of the Andes highlands and Amazon rainforest. It highlights Leo's address to young people in Buenos Aires, where he took Francis's familiar phrase "todos, todos, todos" ("everyone, everyone, everyone") and reframed it in explicitly Christological terms — "everyone toward Christ, everyone with Christ, everyone like Christ" — which the piece reads as Leo developing Francis's missionary vision more consistently than Francis himself managed, rather than breaking from it.

First Things

A new book from retired Oxford theologian Alister McGrath argues C.S. Lewis used literary imagination, not just argument, to show that science and religion were never the enemies a famous 1920s Cambridge declaration claimed they were

WORLD's review covers McGrath's "Science and Religion in the Imagination of C.S. Lewis," noting McGrath began his own academic career with a doctorate in molecular biophysics before becoming a historian and theologian who has written extensively on Lewis. The book answers geneticist J.B.S. Haldane's 1920s declaration to Cambridge's Heretics Society that "there can be no truce between science and religion" by tracing how Lewis's fiction, including the Space Trilogy and That Hideous Strength, dramatized a medieval and Renaissance sense of a universe full of beauty and meaning against a modern, detached scientific gaze that the review says can otherwise drain the world of enchantment.
