Defense News

Kadena Air Base took delivery of its first two permanently assigned F-15EX Eagle II fighters this week, making the Okinawa installation the Air Force's first overseas base to field the jet as the service works to replace its aging Pacific fighter fleet

The two F-15EX Eagle IIs, assigned to the 67th Fighter Squadron, landed at Kadena Air Base on Nov. 15, making the installation the first base outside the continental United States and only the second location worldwide to field the aircraft, according to Defense News and the 18th Wing's own release. The wing, which is approaching its centennial in 2027, said the arrival opens a new chapter in its nearly century-old legacy of Pacific operations as the Air Force phases newer Eagle IIs in alongside long-serving F-15C/D airframes based at Kadena.

Political

Illinois became the first state to restrict card swipe fees on purchases, but its law reads federal banking rules so narrowly that an out-of-state bank can escape the restriction simply by opening a physical branch inside Illinois

Merchants have long resented the cut that card-issuing banks and payment networks take from every sale, and consumers resent the fees added at checkout; under that pressure, Illinois moved first to legislate against interchange fees, according to a Heritage Foundation analysis. The piece argues Illinois' law reads federal preemption rules in a way that leaves an unusual workaround built in: an out-of-state bank can sidestep the state's restriction just by establishing a branch within Illinois, a result Heritage says amounts to compliance gamesmanship rather than a fix for the underlying fee dispute.

Heritage Foundation

A new book from three Foundation for Defense of Democracies analysts argues Washington has no coherent strategy for countering a deepening, informal cooperation among China, Russia, Iran and North Korea that has hardened through both the Trump and Biden administrations

Reviewing 'Axis of Aggressors: Countering the Cooperation of China, Russia, Iran & North Korea' by Bradley L. Bowman, Elaine K. Dezenski and retired Rear Adm. Mark Montgomery, Providence's review says the authors document rising coordination among the four powers across multiple domains since the 2020s, even as most of Washington's foreign-policy establishment has offered only 'managed decline' or retreat. The review credits the book with sharpening the picture of the danger rather than supplying a single fix, saying its proposed solutions ought to draw bipartisan support.

Providence

A keynote address marking the Declaration of Independence's 250th anniversary argues the Founders grounded their claim that human equality and unalienable rights are 'self-evident' in natural law and public theology, not in English common-law rights or the political fashions of their day

Adapted from a keynote speech at the Catholic International University's annual gala, the essay traces the Declaration's famous second paragraph to the Founders' conviction that truth exists, that reason can know it, and that human nature itself points toward an eternal standard of right and wrong, according to Public Discourse. The piece argues that conviction — not an appeal to popular opinion or elite consensus — is what the signers actually invoked in declaring independence, and ties it to the mission of Catholic and faith-based education 250 years later.

Tech / AI

The Army wants to build 17 reinforced concrete bunkers across Israel, Saudi Arabia and Lebanon to replace converted-shipping-container shelters, after Iranian missile and drone strikes killed American troops sheltering in those containers

An Army Sources Sought notice posted Friday calls the requirement 'urgent' and 'high-priority,' citing elevated regional force-protection conditions, and seeks precast, reinforced bunkers able to withstand indirect-fire attacks at five primary installations — one in Israel and two each in Saudi Arabia and Lebanon — according to Defense News. The push follows a March 2026 Iranian strike on an Army command post in Kuwait that killed six service members after reports found the site was poorly protected against aerial bombardment; the Army says the project is meant to safeguard U.S. and coalition troops across the Central Command area of responsibility, with contractor responses due Nov. 25.

A career submariner has taken command of U.S. 2nd Fleet, the Virginia-based command responsible for naval forces in the Atlantic, following the retirement of Vice Adm. Doug Perry

Vice Adm. Oliver Lewis assumed command of 2nd Fleet on Monday, taking over from newly promoted Vice Adm. Mark Behning, who had been overseeing the command following Perry's retirement, according to USNI News. The change of command continues a recent string of senior Navy leadership turnover across the fleet's Atlantic-facing commands.

Culture

A 379-page Arthurian verse epic from an Anglican priest and Cambridge scholar sold out its first print run in two days, a surprise success a First Things reviewer says reflects a growing hunger for tradition, craftsmanship and Christian meaning in contemporary art

Malcolm Guite's 'Galahad and the Grail,' published by the small Rabbit Room Press (founded by songwriter Andrew Peterson), caught the publishing world off guard with almost no advance reviews, according to the First Things review, written by a bookstore owner who said his distributor couldn't keep up with demand once word spread. The reviewer argues the rhyming verse epic taps an emerging 'counterculture to secular elitism' built on tradition, national history, heroism and re-enchantment with God, and says the poem succeeds as more than a nostalgic exercise.

First Things

A new book argues the Supreme Court has quietly revived a centuries-old classical legal tradition rooted in Aristotelian and Christian thought through its recent history-and-tradition rulings, but a Law & Liberty reviewer says the Court's actual practice is far more modest than that

Reviewing Catholic University law professor Marc O. DeGirolami's 'The Constitution of Practice,' the Law & Liberty review credits the book's central claim — that the Court has taken a distinct 'traditionalist' turn reflecting a classical, largely Thomistic legal tradition — but argues the history doesn't support it: the Court has appealed to history and tradition since the early Republic, the reviewer writes, and recent tradition-based rulings remain one interpretive tool among several rather than a free-standing doctrine, making the shift 'a decidedly more modest, more positivist, move' than the book claims.
