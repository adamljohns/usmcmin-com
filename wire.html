USNI News

The Philippine Marine Corps received two American-made sea drones at Subic Bay last week, part of Washington's push to help Manila track Chinese activity in the South China Sea

USNI reports the U.S. formally handed over two MARTAC-built Devil Ray T38 unmanned surface vessels, worth more than $8 million combined, at a Nov. 4 ceremony at Subic Bay, along with a new training and maintenance center to support them. The delivery is the latest in a string of American drone handoffs meant to give the Philippines more affordable ways to watch its coastline and the waters around contested reefs without matching China vessel-for-vessel. The catamaran-hulled craft, capable of bursts over 60 knots, are built to support surveillance missions across the country's roughly 7,000-island archipelago.

Political

The Pentagon has told Poland it's ready to make a permanent U.S. troop presence there official, with the White House expected to decide by early December

Poland's defense ministry said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth told Deputy Prime Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz at the Pentagon that "everything is going in a good direction" on a permanent American base, after months of Pentagon review of U.S. force posture across Europe. Kosiniak-Kamysz called the permanent presence Poland's "absolute priority" and said his government would keep pressing for it; President Trump told reporters last month the base was under consideration. No site or troop numbers have been announced.

Mosaic law and the Hebrew prophets spent far more time than the New Testament instructing judges, kings and the treatment of the vulnerable, and earlier Protestants worked out which of those obligations still bind once Israel's covenant institutions are gone

The piece, published by American Reformer, says most contemporary Christian political talk rests on Jesus' call to love neighbors and Paul's instruction to honor rulers, but leaves open what rulers and laws are actually supposed to accomplish. The author argues Mosaic law and the prophets answer that question in detail, even though Israel's covenant land, priesthood and civil order don't transfer directly to any modern nation, and that earlier Protestants worked out which obligations of justice still bind once those particular institutions are gone rather than simply discarding the Old Testament's political teaching.

The Navy's next nuclear attack submarine, the SSN(X), is being designed to be faster to build, quicker to repair and a mothership for swarms of undersea drones

USNI reports the Navy's head of submarine-force requirements said Thursday the service is finishing its analysis of alternatives for the SSN(X) design, meant to eventually succeed the Virginia-class boats with a hull built around supporting a constellation of unmanned underwater vehicles rather than treating them as an add-on. No contract award, cost estimate or construction timeline has been set; the analysis of alternatives is the formal Pentagon study step that precedes a program's official design phase.

Culture

The embryonic stem cell fight showed that cost-benefit ethics failed even on its own terms, and moral restraint produced the breakthrough

Public Discourse, Friday: the author, a former staffer for the U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops, starts from Pope Leo XIV's warning in Magnifica Humanitas against reducing moral judgment to calculation. He then retraces the decade-long fight over federal funding for research that destroys human embryos. Supporters justified it with promised 'miracle cures' and a five-year timeline that slipped to 'decades.' Hyped claims followed, including a Nature paper whose authors had to retract key claims and a South Korean cloning result that Science retracted as fraudulent. The author argues that an ends-justify-the-means ethic invites that kind of pressure. The breakthrough came instead from Shinya Yamanaka's induced pluripotent stem cells, reprogrammed from ordinary body cells, which raise no moral problem and avoid tissue rejection. Yamanaka has said that seeing a human embryo under a microscope reminded him of his daughters.

Public Discourse

Wendell Berry's last book, published two months after his September death, revisits the 1967 federal report on a 'surplus of farmers' that first set him against industrial agriculture half a century ago

The Law & Liberty review of "Dispatches from Rural America" says Berry opens the collection admitting he has "become an aged man," then delivers a hard anti-war, anti-industrialism essay titled "Against Killing Children." The book arrives near the 50th anniversary of Berry's "The Unsettling of America," which challenged a 1967 federal commission's finding that the country's "biggest farm problem was a surplus of farmers" — a verdict Berry spent his career arguing mistook the hollowing-out of rural towns for progress.

Law & Liberty

British comedians Russell Brand's rape conviction and David Walliams' grooming allegations expose a modern 'benefit of clergy' that still shields celebrities from ordinary moral accountability

Brand was sentenced Nov. 9 to more than ten years in prison on one count of rape and three of sexual assault; Walliams faces a separate Guardian/ITV investigation alleging assault and grooming. The essay borrows George Orwell's term for the medieval privilege that let clergy face lighter church courts, arguing society now grants entertainers the same immunity, and leans on sociologist Philip Rieff's argument that as personal happiness became society's goal, family and church, which demand self-sacrifice, declined while entertainment and medicine, which relieve pain, rose to fill their place as the new "holy men."
