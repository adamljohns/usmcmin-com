Kadena Airmen and Marines stood up a joint fuel site and hot-pit refueled two F-22s

An 18th Wing release from Kadena Air Base says Airmen of the 18th Logistics Readiness Squadron and Marines of Marine Wing Support Squadron-172 built an above-ground fuel site on the airfield during Exercise Beverly Sword 27-1. The site extends R-11 truck capacity of 6,000 gallons to a Marine Corps 20,000-gallon bladder, then pumps fuel at 150 gallons a minute through an R-18 pump and an R-19 filter to two R-20 servicing platforms. Tech. Sgt. Nicholas Rockwell, the fuels NCO in charge, said the kits fit an MC-130J Commando II. The site was tested with a hot-pit refuel of two F-22 Raptors from the 525th Expeditionary Fighter Squadron, deployed from Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson.

USS Oscar Austin hosted Slovenia's prime minister in Koper on Sovereignty Day

U.S. Sixth Fleet said the Arleigh Burke-class destroyer USS Oscar Austin (DDG 79) ended a scheduled port visit to Koper, Slovenia, on Oct. 27 during the country's Sovereignty Day observance. Sailors hosted Prime Minister Janez Janša, U.S. Ambassador Asel K. Roberts, and Chief of Defense Maj. Gen. Boštjan Močnik for a ship tour. Cmdr. Gerry Sellars, the commanding officer, said the visit was a chance to mark the observance as guests of a NATO ally. The ship is assigned to Sixth Fleet.

The Air Force moved the ARRW hypersonic missile from test to a $694 million production contract

Defense News reported that the Air Force awarded Lockheed Martin a $694 million contract in late September to produce and field the AGM-183A Air-Launched Rapid Response Weapon. Air Force Secretary Troy Meink called the move a turning point, saying the weapon is meant to penetrate advanced anti-access systems. Chief of Staff Gen. Ken Wilsbach said it gives the joint force a rapid option in contested airspace. The announcement did not say how many missiles Lockheed will build or when they will be delivered, citing operational security. The service had paused ARRW buys in 2023 after failed tests, then put new funding in the fiscal 2026 request.

Law & Liberty says Andy Burnham's localism is a delivery model for a larger state

Daniel Pitt's Wednesday essay says Britain's new prime minister talks like a localist and governs like a centralizer. Pitt writes that Burnham promised the biggest rebalancing of power the country has seen, and that growth cannot be ordered from Whitehall. He then argues the plan still enlarges the national government, using Jeffersonian language for a Hamiltonian blueprint. Pitt grants that Britain is overcentralized. The piece is an argument about the British state, not a vote count.

Tech / AI

The Army asked industry whether it can build nearly 21,000 Infantry Squad Vehicles

Defense News reported that an Army market survey published Monday asks whether industry can deliver 9,003 nine-passenger ISV-9s and 11,784 five-passenger ISV-Utility trucks, 20,787 vehicles in all, subject to funding and foreign sales. The vehicle, from GM Defense, is based on the Colorado and Silverado. A contract modification last month for ISV-U engineering changes was valued at $697.7 million, bringing the cumulative value to $915.6 million. The survey's timeline starts in fiscal 2028 with 106 vehicles and runs to fiscal 2036 at about 2,000 to 2,100 a year. Both versions are listed at a 5,200-pound curb weight and a 10,200-pound gross rating.

Defense News

Kadena medics and Japanese troops practiced moving wounded onto a C-130 during Keen Sword

An 18th Wing release says the 18th Medical Group and the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force simulated patient movement and aeromedical evacuation at Kadena on Oct. 27 during Keen Sword 27. Maj. Shayna Jayde Alisasis, en route care flight commander of the 18th Operational Medical Readiness Squadron, said the team is the link between field hospitals and evacuation aircraft. Once patients were stable, medics from both nations moved them by medical bus onto a C-130J Super Hercules of the 36th Airlift Squadron from Yokota. A JGSDF medical officer said the point of the drill is to find differences in procedure before a real casualty flow.

The Pentagon will spend $1.4 million on a council that advises only on human performance

Defense News reported that a Federal Register notice Tuesday creates a federal advisory committee focused exclusively on human performance, operator syndrome, and injury prevention. The notice calls the council a low-cost way to tap outside expertise. It sits beside a Warfighter Performance Optimization division stood up this year under Under Secretary Anthony Tata. In May, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth told Tata to report on service-level optimization work and to treat cognitive performance as a readiness item. The article does not name the committee's members.

George Weigel says the message on marriage is the Gospel, not a listening session

A Wednesday First Things essay looks at the Roman meeting on the Church's mission to marriage and the family. Weigel points to a short post by Bishop Erik Varden of Trondheim, on the bishop's blog Coram Fratribus, as the clearest statement to come out of a process that began in 2014–15. He says Varden explains marriage as the primordial sacrament, with biblical depth and a light touch of popular culture, including the Tevye and Golde duet from Fiddler on the Roof. It is a theological argument, not a synod vote tally.
