Heritage says the Trump-Xi summit bought time and kept leverage

Friday's Heritage report covers Chinese General Secretary Xi Jinping's Sept. 23–25 visit to Washington for a summit with President Donald Trump. An earlier Heritage analysis had expected tactical agreements, not a breakthrough. The report says the outcomes that followed bought time and preserved American leverage.

American Reformer says America is reacting to a global order coming apart

Friday's American Reformer essay says the plates of the global order have been shifting since the 2010s and have not settled. It argues that blaming Donald Trump and MAGA misses the point: in most respects they are reacting to events, not controlling them. American power is real, the piece says, and still not enough to hold a system that is coming apart.

The Navy turned six flag billets over to civilians and cut six more to captain

USNI News reported Thursday that the Navy changed six billets held by flag officers into civilian posts, following Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's call for fewer generals and more troops. The service identified 14 flag billets in the review and reduced six of those to captain.

Tech / AI

Defense News says the order to slow Starshield for Ukraine came from the White House

Defense News, citing Reuters, reports that two U.S. officials and a Western official said the order to limit SpaceX's Starshield service for Ukraine came from inside the White House. Defense Department leaders were told the service needed to be slowed. The officials did not name who decided. Ukrainian officials said military-grade terminals stopped working on fast-moving objects, which hit drones that use them for guidance. A Ukrainian Defence Ministry person said Thursday the terminals were working again in motion, but only up to 100 miles per hour.

Defense News

Special Operations is fielding a half-mask and goggles instead of a full respirator

Defense News reports that the Pentagon and Avon Protection announced a contract on Oct. 9 for the MITR half-mask and goggle system. Avon says it is meant to guard against chemicals, lead powder from gunfire, and substances such as fentanyl, and that it does not replace a full-face respirator. The goggles and mask can be worn together or apart. Avon said some elite units are already wearing it. The story ties the buy to a November 2025 SOF Cancer Study that found special-operations personnel face an 18 percent higher cancer risk than other personnel. The photograph with the story is a company image, not an official still.

Culture

WORLD reports the Muellers still have no grave for Kayla

WORLD reports that Carl and Marsha Mueller wrote President Donald Trump on Nov. 6, 2025, before his meeting with Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa, asking for help bringing their daughter home. ISIS took Kayla Mueller hostage in Syria on Aug. 4, 2013, and reported on Feb. 6, 2015, that she had been killed. The couple told WORLD they have no grave and no marker. The Foley Foundation told WORLD that at least 38 American citizens are held by terror groups or hostile governments, and that two recent hostages, Robert Gilman and Kevin Rideout, came home in August.

WORLD News Group

WORLD follows a Liberty runner whose kratom habit ended in a Richmond detox call

WORLD reports that Cameron Francis, then a distance runner at Liberty University, started mixing a green powder into his water as a junior, looking for a plant-based alternative to alcohol. In an emergency room in 2021, a doctor told his father, Dean Francis, that the powder was akin to an opioid. When Dean called a detox center in Richmond, Virginia, where the family lives, the person on the line equated kratom with heroin. WORLD says eleven states and dozens of cities have banned it, and that the FDA does not approve selling it. It is still sold online and in convenience stores, smoke shops, and health-food stores.
