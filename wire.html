USNI News

The Navy relieved the entire command triad of the submarine USS South Dakota last month after an October grounding, a member of Congress says

USNI News reported Thursday that the commanding officer, executive officer and chief of the boat of the Virginia-class attack submarine were all relieved from command on Oct. 14, following a grounding incident the prior month. A member of Congress disclosed the relief in a statement; the Navy has not detailed the cause of the grounding or confirmed the congressman's full account. Removing an entire command triad at once, rather than just a commanding officer, is an unusually broad accountability action and the second submarine command shakeup USNI has reported this week alongside separate questions about the destroyer USS Michael Murphy.

Political

Putin agreed to let European representatives join the next round of Ukraine peace talks, while Zelenskyy said U.S. restrictions are again disrupting Starlink's military Starshield service

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff announced the move after meeting Putin in the Kremlin, calling European participation in the next session important since the two days of direct U.S.-mediated talks this week in Abu Dhabi were the first in nine months. Putin said Moscow has never refused to talk with Europe and warned that any plan to send Western troops into Ukraine after a deal would be seen as a threat and could restart the war. Separately, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said new restrictions on the military version of Starlink, called Starshield, are slowing Ukraine's jet-powered-drone interceptors along the entire front line; SpaceX declined to comment.

Defense News

A Christian commentator argues the scale of this month's Republican midterm losses says less about voter fraud than about how easily Americans are deceived by economic promises

The essay notes Republicans lost roughly 30 House seats and at least seven Senate seats on Nov. 3, a reversal from the 2024 result that the writer says shows a larger share of Americans voted for the same policies they had rejected two years earlier once grocery and gas prices became the dominant complaint. The piece argues Democrats never explained how they would lower prices even as historical tax-and-spend policy made costs worse under the prior administration, and frames the swing as evidence of national spiritual drift rather than a single fraud explanation.

TruthScript

Texas elected its first Democratic governor in more than 30 years, as Gov. Greg Abbott conceded to Gina Hinojosa before the race was even called

WORLD reports Abbott stunned his own election-night crowd in Austin by calling Hinojosa, a state House member, to concede before the Associated Press had called the race, and that Democrats went on to flip the Texas House and win every statewide office on the ballot. A Cedarville University political scientist called it a significant shift in what had been a longtime Republican stronghold, part of midterm losses nationwide that exceeded even what most political observers expected heading into Nov. 3.

Tech / AI

A Polish AI firm, SATIM, won a contract to automatically analyze Poland and Germany's military satellite radar imagery, claiming 90 to 95 percent accuracy identifying objects

SATIM's software supports Poland's new MikroSAR satellite-reconnaissance program and Germany's SPOCK-1 program with Rheinmetall, now in operational testing. The company's CEO said access to synthetic-aperture-radar imagery is only useful once intelligence can be extracted quickly and at scale; a company spokesman said interest grew after cloud cover repeatedly complicated interpreting imagery over Ukraine, and that the system can classify roughly 300 object types including vessels, aircraft and vehicles.

Culture

A review of Joshua Jipp's 'How (Not) to Be a Tyrant' asks whether a Jesus reduced to gentle moral examples still leaves room for the Lamb who returns as a Lion to judge the world

WORLD's reviewer credits the book's list of tyrants Jesus wasn't like — Pharaoh, the Herods, Pilate, Caesar Augustus, Nero — but argues popular 'What Would Jesus Do' theology tends to flatten Christ into only the gentle teacher who welcomed children, losing the Son of Psalm 2 and the risen Lord of Acts 17 who will return to judge. The review frames that fuller picture, not a one-sided meekness, as the harder and more complete answer to tyranny.

WORLD

A new reissue of Vladimir Bukovsky's dissident memoir 'To Build a Castle' is offered as a handbook for resisting the illiberalism and cynicism of today

The review walks through Bukovsky's account of Soviet psychiatric repression of political prisoners and the daily indignities of life in a fear-ruled, corruption-plagued economy, arguing the memoir's real value is the precise mix of prudent strategy, courage and memory it took to win real victories against totalitarian power. The reviewer, Jacob Howland, says the book's new introduction makes the case that Bukovsky's description of Soviet life reads uncomfortably close to present-day drift toward nihilism and ideological control.

Law & Liberty

An essay on the National World War I Memorial in Washington describes it as the only U.S. memorial where a live bugler sounds Taps every evening in honor of the fallen

The writer describes the daily 5 p.m. ceremony at Pershing Park, where a lone bronze Doughboy statue and the sound of Taps mark the day's end, inspired by the nightly Last Post at Belgium's Menin Gate and Australia's war memorial in Canberra. French and American officials at a companion ceremony called the two nations' military partnership the oldest continuous one America has ever known, an echo of Gen. Pershing's own debt-repaying arrival in France with the American Expeditionary Forces in World War I.
