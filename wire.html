USNI: Lincoln is on track for the second-longest carrier cruise since the Cold War

USNI reported Tuesday that USS Abraham Lincoln (CVN-72) is weeks from a 295-day mark on Sept. 12, which would be the second-longest carrier deployment since the Cold War, behind only USS Gerald R. Ford’s cruise that ended in May. As of Tuesday, Lincoln, its escorts, and Carrier Air Wing 9 were in the Indian Ocean. The Navy has not published a homecoming date.

Defense News: the first North Korea–Russia road bridge is nearly finished

Defense News, Wednesday from Vienna: Planet satellite imagery shows the first road bridge across the Tumen River at Tumangang is nearly complete on the North Korean side, including a customs building and a controlled entry. Construction on the Russian approach, the road, and border facilities is still unfinished. No opening date for commercial traffic was published.

Defense News: Lincoln is due in Thailand next week

Defense News, Tuesday: a Thai official said Abraham Lincoln will dock in Thailand next week for rest after leaving the Middle East on Saturday. Thailand’s Navy confirmed a U.S. carrier strike group will make a brief stop in the east, with no exercises, and did not name the ships or the date. The U.S. Embassy in Bangkok did not immediately comment.

Defense News: the Navy’s next air-launched torpedo would glide in

Defense News, Tuesday: the Navy is asking industry for Silent Anvil, an air-launched anti-submarine torpedo that glides into the water. One path is a new compact round; the other is a glide kit on an existing torpedo. The request for information closes Sept. 29. A prototype is sought within 18 months of an award. The Navy did not publish a range.

Law & Liberty: Gorsuch’s Slaughter concurrence points past Humphrey’s

Law & Liberty, Monday: in Trump v. Slaughter the Court held the president may remove the FTC’s head, overruling Humphrey’s Executor. Justice Neil Gorsuch voted with the majority. His concurrence, which no other justice joined, argues the Court should next return legislative and judicial powers from agencies to Congress and the courts.
