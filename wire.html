Tech / AI

The Army took delivery of its first mobile cannon to replace the M777

Defense News reported Monday that the Army has taken delivery of its first Korean-made mobile tactical cannon, less than a year after the solicitation. Brig. Gen. Robert J. Mikesh Jr., the offensive fires portfolio executive, told reporters ahead of the Association of the United States Army meeting that the Hanwha system was on display at an Army Modernization Day on Capitol Hill earlier this month, and that the service wants to test it and get soldier feedback. The August prototyping contract runs four years and up to nearly $263 million, covering as many as 18 systems, with the first six on an accelerated schedule. The cannon is based on the K9, and Hanwha is building an integration site in Opelika, Alabama. This is a prototype in hand, not a fielded replacement.

Defense News

Vector and Nammo said they will scale munitions for small drones

Defense News reported Monday that Vector and Nammo Defense Systems expanded a partnership, announced at the Association of the United States Army meeting, to integrate munitions into small drones for U.S. military use. The companies first paired Nammo's munitions with Vector's unmanned aircraft earlier this year. Vector chief executive Andy Yakulis said the systems will be produced at scale. Vector supplies an electronic safe-and-arm device that sits between the drone and the munition, and the company has a drone on the Defense Department's Blue List. This is a company announcement, not a contract award.

First Things reports from an Armenian village that says it will fight for its churches

Monday's essay is a dispatch from Svarants, a village in Syunik in southern Armenia, where the writer says Azerbaijan's president has threatened for years to take the region. He writes that Azerbaijan has already expelled Christian residents and bulldozed churches and cemeteries in other parts of historic Armenia, and quotes the village leader saying his people will fight. The piece treats Armenian Christianity, dating to the year 301, as the core of national identity. It is a reported essay, not a government statement.
