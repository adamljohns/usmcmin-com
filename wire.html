Camp Lejeune redesignated 1/10 as the Corps' first missile battalion

The 2nd Marine Division said 1st Battalion, 10th Marines redesignated on Oct. 9 from an artillery battalion to a medium missile battalion, the first of its kind in the Marine Corps. Lt. Col. Joseph Mozzi, the commanding officer, said the battalion will employ the Navy-Marine Expeditionary Ship Interdiction System, a ground launcher that carries two Naval Strike Missiles. The Corps says those missiles have an effective range of more than 130 nautical miles and can hit ships or fixed ground targets. Col. William Soucie, commanding officer of 10th Marines, said the system complements cannon artillery rather than replacing it, and that another battalion will keep the howitzers. The official still shows Mozzi speaking at the ceremony on WPT Hill Field.

Providence says software never replaced shipyards

Monday's Providence essay argues that three decades of defense planning treated information dominance as a substitute for factories, shipyards, and drydocks. It points to Government Accountability Office and Congressional Research Service reports on nuclear-submarine delays driven by workforce shortages and thin supplier pools. The piece says emergency appropriations cannot recreate welders, machinists, and pipefitters on a budget cycle. It treats skilled trades as a strategic resource, not a cost to be cut.

Providence

Law & Liberty says originalism's strength is that it stays positivist

Monday's Law & Liberty essay takes up the natural-law critique of originalism from Hadley Arkes, J. Joel Alicea, and Adrian Vermeule. The writer grants that the method is detached from a full moral theory, then argues that detachment is why it works in a pluralistic state. Dropping it now, the piece says, would leave no widely accepted alternative to living constitutionalism. The essay treats public moral language as thinner than the natural lawyers want, and says that is structural, not a bug to be patched.

Law & Liberty

American Reformer says Washington is reacting to the order, not steering it

Friday's American Reformer essay says the global order has been shifting since the 2010s, and that blaming the change on one administration misses the point. The writer argues the United States has more power than other nations and still spends most of its time reacting to events. When alliances were tighter, Washington could move influence with more effect. The piece treats that older smoothness as gone.

Tech / AI

The Pentagon and BAE set a path to 25,000 rocket-guidance kits a year

Defense News reported Monday that the Defense Department signed a framework with BAE Systems for up to 25,000 Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System kits a year, for as long as seven years. The kits turn unguided 2.75-inch rockets into laser-guided rounds, and the services have used them as a cheaper way to shoot down drones. Congress still has to fund the buy, and neither side named a price. The same framework sketches a path to 35,000 kits a year for allies through foreign military sales. BAE said in February it had delivered its 100,000th kit.

Defense News

Army Cyber still keeps a person on every AI task

Defense News reported Monday that Lt. Gen. Christopher Eubank, head of Army Cyber Command, said last week at the AUSA conference that every AI agent in the command still checks in with a human team daily. A governance board decides later whether a routine task can run without that check. Eubank said an agent finished in two days work that had taken an analytics support officer six months. None of that work is running unsupervised yet.

WORLD reports the Muellers are still trying to bring Kayla home

WORLD reports that Carl and Marsha Mueller wrote President Trump on Nov. 6, 2025, ahead of his meeting with Syria's Ahmed al-Sharaa, asking for help locating their daughter. ISIS took Kayla Mueller hostage in Syria on Aug. 4, 2013, and said on Feb. 6, 2015, that she had been killed. The couple still has no grave. The Foley Foundation, cited in the piece, counts at least 38 American citizens held by terror groups or hostile governments, and says two recent hostages came home in August.
