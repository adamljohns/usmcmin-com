The Army activated the 4th Multi-Domain Task Force at Fort Carson

DVIDS reported that the 4th Multi-Domain Task Force was activated in a ceremony at Fort Carson, Colorado, on Oct. 8. Col. Hugh W. A. Jones commands a formation of nearly 2,100 soldiers. The unit follows the 1st Multi-Domain Task Force at Joint Base Lewis-McChord, the 2nd in Germany, and the 3rd in Hawaii. It is built to synchronize long-range precision fires against enemy anti-access networks, with an operational focus on the Indo-Pacific. Jones and the 1st Multi-Domain Task Force report to the 7th Infantry Division, the multi-domain headquarters.

Congress still has not taken back the decision to go to war

Monday's essay says President Trump ordered the 2025 strikes on Iran's nuclear sites without consulting Congress, and that a joint Israeli and U.S. bombardment campaign resumed on Feb. 28, 2026. Tech / AI

The Army wants its first high-energy laser counter-drone weapon by early 2028

Defense News reported Tuesday that Army officials at the Association of the United States Army meeting said the first Enduring-High Energy Laser weapon could be out of testing and ready for counter-drone work by early 2028. In September the Army awarded AeroVironment a $464.8 million contract for LOCUST X3, a palletized laser. Brig. Gen. Guy Yelverton, capability program executive for Defensive Fires, said a spring 2028 delivery is the far date and a December or January delivery would be a win. Kirk Williams, chief engineer at the Directed Energy Project Office, said capable counter-drone systems tend to get deployed rather than held for training.

Anduril showed a family of surface-to-air interceptor missiles

Defense News reported Tuesday that Anduril released Steelfin, a family of surface-to-air interceptor missiles, on Oct. 12. The company says a two-stage design, booster plus terminal interceptor, lets it change range and launch fit while reusing the same airframe, avionics, and software.

Culture

WWII Anabaptists refused the rifle and still joined the war effort

Tuesday's essay by Ruthanne Downey says Mennonites, Quakers, and other Anabaptists were accused of cowardice for refusing conscription, and that their objection was a claim of loyalty ordered God first and country second. She writes that they opposed Nazi Germany and petitioned to serve without bearing arms. The Selective Training and Service Act of 1940 let conscientious objectors do noncombatant service or work of national importance under civilian direction, which became the Civilian Public Service: soil conservation, state mental hospitals, and firefighting. Downey says that work was real and still less sacrificial than the front.
