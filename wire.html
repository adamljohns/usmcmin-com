Camp Lejeune redesignated 1st Battalion, 10th Marines as the Corps' first medium missile battalion

The 2nd Marine Division said 1st Battalion, 10th Marine Regiment redesignated on Oct. 9 from an artillery battalion to a medium missile battalion, the first of its kind in the Marine Corps. Lt. Col. Joseph Mozzi, the battalion commander, said the unit will employ the Navy-Marine Expeditionary Ship Interdiction System, a ground launcher that carries two Naval Strike Missiles. The Marine Corps said the missile has an effective range of more than 130 nautical miles and can hit surface ships and fixed ground targets. Col. William Soucie, commanding officer of 10th Marine Regiment, said the system adds fires in support of maneuver at sea and does not replace cannon artillery. The official still shows Mozzi and Sgt. Maj. Herbert Nicolas saluting the colors at WPT Hill Field during the ceremony.

Political

Heritage says the Greenland deal is a security win, not a concession

Tuesday's Heritage commentary treats President Trump's Greenland agreement with Denmark and Greenland as a diplomatic achievement that improves Danish, Greenlandic, and trans-Atlantic security. The writer tells readers to ignore the naysayers and calls the conclusion a major win for the United States. The piece does not reprint the treaty text.

Providence asks whether a Judeo-Christian coalition can hold in Michigan

Tuesday's Providence essay says hostility toward Israel is growing on the right and treats that shift as a rejection of a political inheritance that placed biblical Israel alongside Greece and Rome. The writer uses Michigan's Senate race, a Muslim progressive against a traditional pro-Israel Republican, as the test. He recounts Jake Lang's summer march in Dearborn and a Sept. 11 protest near Ground Zero where men identifying as neo-Nazis showed up to oppose Lang, not to join him.

Providence

Heritage says the OCC report puts the debanking scandal in Washington

Monday's Heritage commentary says the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency confirmed a long conservative claim. The writer says the OCC's 47-page report, after a review of 100,000 complaints and 50,000 internal bank records, found that between 2020 and 2025 the nation's nine largest national banks denied customers for reasons that tracked political and religious categories. The argument is that the pressure started with regulators, not with the banks acting on their own.

Tech / AI

An F-22 pilot directed an unmanned Fury drone in flight

Defense News reported that an F-22 Raptor completed airborne tactical command of a YFQ-44A Fury earlier this month. The pilot gave high-level directives; the unmanned aircraft's autonomy software flew the maneuvers and simulated combat tasks. Air Force Secretary Troy Meink said the point is turning the technology into combat capability at the pace the threat demands. The service wants 500 combat-ready semi-autonomous systems by 2032, and a fleet of 1,000. The official still of the YFQ-44A was taken at Edwards Air Force Base in April, not during this month's flight.

Defense News

Army earmarked an air defense unit for Golden Dome, and did not name it

Defense News reported that Lt. Gen. John L. Rafferty, commanding general of Army Space and Missile Defense Command, told reporters at the Association of the United States Army meeting that an Army air defense unit has been earmarked for Golden Dome for America. He declined to name the unit. He described the shield as a way to pair any sensor with the best shooter and the right command node, rather than keeping each package locked together. Joint Task Force Gold, a roughly 100-person unit under Northern Command, is planning sites and a cyber piece. The next planning sprint is set for the end of October. Pentagon officials have said they want an early version fielded by the end of next year.

Defense News

A Euronaval report says China built more than twice U.S. naval tonnage in 2024-25

Defense News reported from Paris that the World Defense Shipbuilding report, compiled by Flottes de Combat and the French industry group Gican, put Chinese combat-ship construction at 636,208 tons in the two years through December 2025, against 270,509 tons for the United States. China accounted for more than a third of global naval tonnage in that window. The value of Chinese construction was $43.5 billion, against $26.8 billion for the United States. In 2022-23 the numbers ran the other way: 202,520 tons for China and 245,703 for the United States.

Culture

First Things says the drop in young LGBTQ identification is not a collapse

Tuesday's First Things essay reads two CDC surveys and says sexual identity among young Americans has moved year to year, not in one direction. It notes that gay, lesbian, or bisexual identification among 18- to 24-year-olds rose from about 7 percent in 2014 to nearly 20 percent in 2022, then records declines from 2023 to 2025 among high-school boys and girls and among 18- to 24-year-old men. The writer says young women in that older band were still at all-time highs for bisexual and lesbian identification in 2025, and treats headlines about a free fall as an excessive reading of the data.
