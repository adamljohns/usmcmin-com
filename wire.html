Defense News: CNO looks at Africa and India port stops after Lincoln

Defense News, Wednesday from Norfolk: Adm. Daryl Caudle said the Navy is changing how it stocks a cruise and looking at different port calls, including on the African continent, after USS Abraham Lincoln’s long deployment. He said Lincoln went about 250 days without a port visit. He also said the service is stockpiling for Theodore Roosevelt’s next cruise, likely this fall. No new port list was published.

SOUTHCOM: four killed in a Caribbean go-fast strike

U.S. Southern Command said Tuesday that Joint Task Force Western Hemisphere struck a go-fast vessel on a known Caribbean trafficking route and killed four people it called narco-terrorists. Gen. Francis L. Donovan said the task force will keep hunting those networks. SOUTHCOM cited confirmed intelligence and did not publish cargo recovered from the boat.

USNI: U.S., Norway, and Britain open drills on Jan Mayen

USNI, Wednesday: U.S., Norwegian, and British forces deployed to Jan Mayen for a NATO Arctic Sentry drill that runs through Sept. 4. The island sits north of the Greenland-Iceland-U.K. gap. U.S. units include II MEF, 10th Marines, and a New Hampshire Guard HIMARS platoon.

Defense News: Army picks five firms for on-base microreactors

Defense News, Wednesday: the Army, with the Defense Innovation Unit, selected five companies to build nuclear microreactors at U.S. installations under the Janus program. The agreements are worth up to $2.2 billion combined. The service wants an operational reactor at a military base by Sept. 30, 2028. Officials said the reactors will not take the bases off the commercial grid.

Defense News: Rolls-Royce and Safran compete for India’s AMCA engine

Defense News, Thursday from New Delhi: India wants a foreign partner to co-develop a high-thrust engine for later Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft variants. Rolls-Royce and Reliance this month announced intent to build an indigenous engine and explore a gas-turbine complex in India. France’s Safran has a separate offer with India’s Gas Turbine Research Establishment. Early AMCA jets are still expected to use the GE F414.
