San Diego hosted a Navy working group on shipboard directed energy

The Naval Surface and Mine Warfighting Development Center said it hosted a directed energy working group at Naval Base San Diego on Oct. 19. Cmdr. Rob Toohig, the center's experimentation lead, said the point is tactics, training, and technical understanding so sailors can employ the systems. Warfare Tactics Instructors from integrated air and missile defense and the Directed Energy Community of Focus reviewed current capabilities and limitations. Col. Joseph Rosal, director of directed energy for the Office of the Secretary of War for Research and Engineering, said the advantage comes from putting the capability in sailors' hands and applying what they learn at sea. The official still shows the working group in San Diego that day.

Political

First Things says two Virginia amendments put the state over the church

Tuesday's First Things essay by Paul D. Scalia takes up the Catholics Vote No campaign against two proposed Virginia constitutional amendments. He writes that Amendment No. 1 would embed abortion in the state constitution with language that, in his reading, reaches to the point of birth, strips parental rights, and removes accountability for the industry. Amendment No. 2, he writes, does not lift a ban so much as erase a definition of marriage. He cites Bishop Michael Burbidge of Arlington urging priests to oppose both, and treats a no vote as a moral duty because the measures are referendums, not candidate races.

First Things

Providence says the Arctic fishing ban will not settle the contest with Russia

Friday's Providence essay notes that the United States, Denmark, and Greenland signed an agreement last month naming security challenges in the Arctic and the High North from adversaries' military activity, without naming Russia in the text. Ten countries, including the United States and Russia, agreed in 2017 to ban commercial fishing in the Central Arctic Ocean from 2021 to 2037 so the fishery could be studied. The writer treats that stretch of international water, more than one million square miles, as the next place Moscow and Washington will test fishing, shipping, and seabed claims.

Providence

Law & Liberty says Easterly's development history still lacks a rule

Tuesday's Law & Liberty review of William Easterly's Violent Saviors says the book treats five centuries of Western development policy as conquest dressed as help. Easterly's claim, as the review states it, is a Development Right of Conquest: the belief that outsiders may develop other peoples whether those peoples consent. The reviewer calls the history harrowing and says the book still does not answer what a development policy should rest on once GDP growth is set aside.

Tech / AI

Shield AI says Hivemind cut a drone autonomy build from 54 weeks to six

Defense News reported that Shield AI and Honeywell said Hivemind software let Honeywell fly a BFD-SE8 multirotor on its own after six weeks of training, against an estimate of 54 weeks. Honeywell's team started on the stack in late April and flew it autonomously by mid-June. The official demonstration was July 8 in Plymouth, Minnesota: the aircraft took off, followed a planned route, rerouted around a no-fly zone, and returned. A second test flew Oct. 9. The companies signed a memorandum of understanding in July.

Defense News

Army named five humanoid-robot winners, then heard the specs do not exist yet

Defense News reported that the Army's xTechHumanoid competition named five winners: ACES Group, IHMC Robotics, Hydroplane Limited, Ras Labs, and Velocity Explorations. Of 103 white papers, 10 finalists each received $25,000, then five were picked at a live event. Robert Griffin of IHMC told Military Times the listed requirements — a robot running 28 miles an hour, carrying 50 pounds, and operating for 24 hours — do not exist in one machine. Companies competed in the finals at Marine Corps Base Quantico on Sept. 10.

Defense News

Fort Benning surveyed a site for three nuclear microreactors

Fort Benning public affairs said leaders met Oct. 16 with the Army installations office and Radiant Industries on the first Janus Program site visit. The post is scheduled to receive three Kaleidos microreactors, which Radiant would install, operate, and maintain so selected facilities keep power if the commercial grid fails. Maj. Gen. Colin P. Tuley, commanding general of the Maneuver Center of Excellence, named Lawson Army Airfield and the mission command centers as the loads that have to stay up. Retired Rear Adm. Mark Sucato, now with Radiant, said the company is aiming for the Department of War's first operational nuclear microreactor on the post.

Culture

Law & Liberty says the AI scare lands harder because institutions are already weak

Monday's Law & Liberty essay by Andy Smarick says Americans have distrusted Congress, big business, and other institutions for years without treating that distrust as an emergency. He argues the break shows up when people need someone to tell them whether a new technology is dangerous. He cites public worry, somewhere between two-thirds and three-quarters, that AI could destroy humanity, and says a citizen without technical training has nowhere solid to turn once newspapers, campuses, and agencies have already spent their credibility.
