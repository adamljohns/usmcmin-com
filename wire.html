USNI News

The aircraft carrier USS George Washington left its three-month Middle East deployment this week after being relieved by the USS Theodore Roosevelt strike group, heading back into the Pacific as the Navy's carrier presence in the Gulf region rotates

A U.S. Pacific Fleet spokesperson confirmed to USNI News that George Washington is now in the 7th Fleet area, though the spokesperson did not say which of its escorts, destroyer USS Shoup and cruiser USS Robert Smalls, came along for the move. The Japan-based carrier strike group had been on a western Pacific deployment before diverting to the Middle East on Aug. 19 to relieve USS Abraham Lincoln and its strike group; it now hands the Gulf-region carrier watch to the Theodore Roosevelt strike group.

Political

A Baylor professor argues the homeschool and classical-school boom sweeping the country owes its intellectual backbone to two little-known education reformers, Dorothy Sayers and 19th-century British educator Charlotte Mason, whose idea that children are born persons rather than raw material to be shaped is reshaping how a generation of families teach civic virtue

The essay credits state-level education savings account laws and groups like the Goldwater Institute for fueling a surge in homeschools, classical schools and Christian schools, with the author noting his own university's most capable students increasingly come out of these alternative pipelines. It traces the movement's intellectual roots to Sayers, the Inklings-era writer who helped revive the classical trivium, and to Charlotte Mason, an English reformer whose insistence that children are capable persons has spread through homeschool curricula and new publishers like Chapter House built on her principles — arguing this style of education forms free, self-governing citizens rather than passive subjects.

Law & Liberty

President Trump's transactional, sovereignty-first foreign policy is colliding with Republican internationalists who still back NATO and Ukraine, a new foreign-policy essay argues, pointing to this month's midterm defeat as proof neither party has a coherent grand strategy for the two years ahead

The piece, published by Providence, argues America's post-Cold War order of alliances and open markets grew too costly even before Trump — citing allies grown dependent on U.S. security guarantees, a hollowed-out industrial base and China's rise — but says Trump's transactional diplomacy has accelerated the dismantling of old arrangements without building a coherent replacement. It points to contradictions in Trump's own record, from promising restraint while striking Iran to demanding stronger allies while straining the alliances that give America its leverage, and concludes the next two years will bring sharper contestation over America's role rather than simple drift.

Providence

The pro-life movement is grievously wounded heading into 2027, a new essay argues, after President Trump's second term — from softening the GOP platform's abortion language to keeping Biden-era mail-order abortion-pill rules in place — undercut ground pro-lifers built after the Dobbs decision

The piece argues the movement's setbacks predate this month's midterm losses but will now accelerate in newly Democratic-controlled state legislatures, which it expects to repeal unborn-protection laws and target pregnancy resource centers, while federal Democrats look for ways around the filibuster and an increasingly cautious Supreme Court to advance abortion access nationally. It argues pro-life leaders can no longer count on Trump's veto pen or on further favorable rulings from a Court reluctant to revisit Dobbs, leaving the movement's remaining legal protections on uncertain footing.

Tech / AI

The Navy's 30-year plan to grow the fleet from 286 ships to 398 by 2056 would cost about $234 billion more than the Navy's own budget estimates, a 41 percent jump in annual shipbuilding spending, a new Congressional Budget Office analysis finds

CBO's review of the Fiscal Year 2027 shipbuilding plan the Navy submitted to Congress in May found the service's own cost estimates fall roughly 18 percent short of CBO's projection for the 112 additional ships, a gap of some $234 billion over three decades. The report lands as lawmakers in both chambers debate how much to grow the fleet amid competing pressure to cut other shipbuilding accounts.

USNI News

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed during their Shenzhen meeting that humans must stay in the loop on any military use of artificial intelligence, even as the two countries keep racing each other for AI supremacy

Bessent told CNBC after returning from Trump's Asia trip that the leaders agreed no major military operation should be launched without a human being involved, calling it one of the trip's major achievements, and said the agreement would not affect the two countries' continued competition over AI chips and models. He added that Nvidia's H200 remains the most advanced chip China can legally buy after Trump's firm line on exports, said Chinese orders for the chip have been scarce as Beijing builds its own chip stack, and said spying allegations involving former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang did not come up in the Trump-Xi meeting.

Culture

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is a competent retelling of young Haymitch Abernathy's backstory, though its familiar message — that the oppressed share blame for not rising up against tyranny — is wearing thin on a fifth trip to Panem, a new review finds

The review credits Australian newcomer Joseph Zada with evoking real sympathy as a teenage Haymitch forced into the Capitol's games, and praises supporting turns from Ralph Fiennes as President Snow and Jesse Plemons stepping into the late Philip Seymour Hoffman's role as Plutarch Heavensbee, while noting Zada's Southern-tinged accent occasionally slips. It argues the film sticks to the franchise's established themes about oppression and resistance without surprising viewers who have already sat through four previous installments, making it a satisfying but unsurprising addition for existing fans rather than a fresh take on the material.

WORLD

A self-styled New Christian Right movement that gathered last week at a Texas conference featuring Nick Fuentes and Alex Jones is borrowing Christian language for an older hierarchy dressed up as doctrine, a new essay argues, pointing to its platform of anti-Zionism, race realism and male-only leadership

The piece names conference organizer Joel Webbon, who has called on followers to despise Israel and described slavery as a blessing, and catalogs other speakers including plagiarism-accused author Dale Partridge and the multiply-defrocked priest Calvin Robinson, arguing the movement blames the right's recent election losses on insufficient aggression rather than overreach. It traces the group's core claim — that society is fundamentally built on biological hierarchies modernity has suppressed — and argues putting Christ is King fourth on its own list of four pillars, behind anti-Zionism, male leadership and race realism, reveals Christian doctrine is being bolted onto a pre-existing ideology rather than driving it.
