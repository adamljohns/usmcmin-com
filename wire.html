USNI News

Camp Pendleton Marines turned counter-drone VR headsets into a suicide-prevention tool that has now reached 350 Marines

A 1st Marine Logistics Group battalion bought 20 OperatorXR virtual-reality sets in 2025 for $200,000 to train Marines, including supply administrators, to detect and defeat small drones. Since July the same headsets have also run a module where a trainee talks to an AI-driven Marine in crisis, built to teach active communication and suicide intervention. About 350 Marines had been through the suicide-prevention course by mid-October, and the battalion's executive officer wants at least 1,000 through it this year.

British police arrested a senior U.S. soldier stationed in the U.K. on suspicion of spying for Russia

The 41-year-old soldier was detained near Gloucester on suspicion of offenses under the National Security Act covering obtaining or disclosing protected information and assisting a foreign intelligence service. FBI Director Kash Patel called the espionage allegations a matter of the utmost concern, and the U.S. Army's top counterintelligence general called it a sobering reminder that insider threats are real. The arrest came a day after the U.S. pulled refueling aircraft from a British base over a separate, apparently unrelated threat tied to Iran.

Midterm exit polls show the Republican coalition eroding among young, high-income and college-educated voters

Republicans fell to about 43 percent of voters earning over $100,000 and just 25 percent of voters under thirty, erasing 2024 gains with both groups. Support among non-college-educated voters, the core of the party's 2024 House majority, also slipped to roughly 2018 levels. The analysis argues the president's hold on the party has weakened and the fight over what comes after him, between market-oriented conservatives and national populists, is now unavoidable.

A biotech startup backed by Colossal Biosciences and Anduril's founder wants to clone and gene-edit top-performing military working dogs

Warhound, co-founded by Ben Lamm and Palmer Luckey, says only about 13 percent of military working dogs come from domestic breeders and roughly half of the dogs procured are ultimately unfit for service. The company plans to biobank top performers, use Colossal's cloning and gene-editing tools to fix health problems rather than add novel traits, and start standardized training on puppies as young as two weeks old. The first Warhounds are expected in about two years.

A free year of ChatGPT Plus has now reached more than 20,000 transitioning veterans and servicemembers

OpenAI says veterans are using the free access to organize job searches, rehearse interviews and explore civilian career paths, with OpenAI Academy and community partners hosting more than a dozen trainings for nearly 1,000 members of the military community. Workshops in Reston, Virginia, and at the American Legion's national convention in Louisville drew veterans and military spouses who built custom tools to support their transitions. The company marked the program on Veterans Day.

Brian Kilmeade and Heritage President Kevin Roberts dug into the stories that forged America's founding

The conversation, tied to Kilmeade's new book on America's founding era, argues that as the nation marks 250 years of independence, recovering the people and defining moments behind the founding matters as much as celebrating it. Heritage frames the discussion as a chance to rediscover the principles that made the American experiment possible, rather than treat the anniversary as pageantry alone.

Heritage Foundation

Modern horror movies have quietly reversed Christianity's old story of good triumphing over evil

Early horror films like Dracula and The Wolf Man paired good against evil with good, often tied to Christian symbols, winning in the end. The essay traces how films from the late 1960s onward replaced that comfort with a world where God is absent and humanity is treated as prey for forces it cannot resist. It argues Christians should recognize that this newer horror tells a story that runs opposite to Scripture's arc of sin, redemption and restoration.

TruthScript

Washington's National World War I Memorial tells a bronze story of sacrifice worth revisiting this Veterans Day

The essay walks through sculptor Sabin Howard's 58-foot bronze relief, The Soldier's Journey, at Pershing Park, where a bugler sounds Taps each evening in honor of the 116,000 Americans who died in World War I. Howard used post-9/11 combat veterans as models so the faces would carry the real weight of what they had seen, calling the work a tribute meant to be revered rather than a glorification of war itself.
