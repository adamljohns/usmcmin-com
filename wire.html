USNI: Marine lab accepted the first Ancillary Surface Craft

USNI reported Tuesday that the Marine Corps Warfighting Laboratory accepted the first of two Ancillary Surface Craft prototypes, citing a lab release last week. The craft is built to move Marines and supplies onto shallow, unimproved shores. The Corps has not said when the second hull arrives, and it has not named a production buy.

Defense News: Lincoln is due in Thailand next week

Defense News, Tuesday: a Thai official said Abraham Lincoln will dock in Thailand next week for rest after leaving the Middle East on Saturday. Thailand’s Navy confirmed a U.S. carrier strike group will make a brief stop in the east, with no exercises, and did not name the ships or the date. Defense News: U.S. canceled next month’s Ssangyong landing drill

Defense News reported Monday that Seoul’s Marine Corps said Washington canceled next month’s division-level Ssangyong amphibious landing drill. A South Korean Marine Corps spokesperson said the U.S. Marine Corps notified Seoul in June that force availability was constrained by the war in Iran. The allies remain in consultations. U.S. Forces Korea did not immediately comment.

Defense News: Cao named Magliochetti to a new industrial-base office

Defense News, Tuesday: Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao named Andrew Magliochetti the first deputy assistant secretary of the Navy for the defense industrial base. Magliochetti, formerly the Navy’s small-business director, will advise ASN RDA William F. Mahan on shipbuilding, supply chains, and dual-use tech. The Navy says the office is new. It does not name a first contract or a yard.

Defense News: Dutch Navy will field Destinus interceptor drones

Defense News, Tuesday from Paris: the Royal Netherlands Navy plans to equip its fleet with Destinus Hornet B2 interceptor drones for ship defense. Destinus said Monday the kinetic counter-UAS program, including ship integration, is slated for completion in the second quarter of 2027. The Dutch Ministry of Defence said the contract is signed. It did not name which hulls get the first launchers.

Defense News: four allies signed a Polish Piorun framework

Defense News, Tuesday from Warsaw: Poland, Norway, Lithuania, and Latvia signed a framework to buy Piorun man-portable air-defense systems from Poland’s PGZ. The deal is worth more than 8 billion zloty, about $2.2 billion, for several thousand missiles and several hundred launchers, with deliveries through 2030. Poland leads the buy. No country-by-country allotment was published.
