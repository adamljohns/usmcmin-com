The Pentagon named USS Colorado the first U.S. boat for Australia's submarine rotation

Defense News reported Monday that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, with British and Australian counterparts, named the Virginia-class fast-attack submarine USS Colorado (SSN 788) as the first U.S. boat assigned to Submarine Rotational Force-West. The force is expected to be fully operational in 2027 at HMAS Stirling near Perth, with four U.S. boats and one British boat. The Pentagon statement said the rotation is meant to speed Australia's readiness to own and maintain a conventionally armed, nuclear-powered submarine.

The Army tested temporary battlefield graves at Fort Bragg so remains can still come home

An Army release on Phase II of Operation Dragon Covenant says soldiers from the 3rd Corps Sustainment Command, the 20th Engineer Brigade, and unit ministry teams practiced large-scale temporary interment near the St. Mere Eglise Drop Zone. The release says the Army has not done this at scale since the Korean War. Engineers had to dig and refill plots without collapsing mock remains, so later teams could identify and return every soldier. Defense News reported the exercise on Monday.

Tech / AI

Helsing offered two interceptor missiles aimed at Russia's faster Geran drones

Defense News reported from Cologne that German firm Helsing showed a pair of air-defense missiles, plus software to organize intercepts, priced under $100,000 a shot. Chief executive Gundbert Scherf said the Contra suite is in testing and aimed at market readiness in the second half of 2027. He said newer Geran-4 and Geran-5 drones fly at 400 to 700 kilometers per hour, and that almost half now get through. The jet-powered Inferno is listed at 700 kilometers per hour and a 20-kilometer range.

Law & Liberty measures 2026 against Magna Carta and the Mayflower Compact

A Monday Law & Liberty essay notes that 2026, the 250th year of the Declaration, sits farther from the Mayflower Compact (406 years) than that compact sat from Magna Carta in 1215 (405 years). The writer treats the Declaration as a summary of both documents: written limits on power, and a people governing themselves. It is an argument about continuity, not a bill or a vote.
