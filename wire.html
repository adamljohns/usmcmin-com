USS Abraham Lincoln is home in San Diego after 321 days and a record stretch at sea

The Nimitz-class carrier USS Abraham Lincoln returned to San Diego on Oct. 8 after leaving on Nov. 21, 2025. The Navy says the ship steamed more than 93,000 nautical miles, took on more than 20.5 million gallons of aviation fuel in 72 replenishments, and supported more than 13,000 sorties and 28,500 flight hours from Carrier Air Wing 9. In the U.S. Central Command area the strike group led Task Force 50 during Operation Epic Fury, then enforced two naval blockades before departing that theater on Aug. 22. Capt. Dan Keeler, the commanding officer, said the ship logged 286 consecutive days at sea between San Diego and a port call in Thailand, a modern-carrier mark. Sailors manned the rails as the ship came in.

Law & Liberty says a forgotten Washington statue in Budapest was paid for by immigrants

Friday's essay says the George Washington statue beside a lake in Budapest's City Park was commissioned and financed by Hungarian immigrants in the United States, not by a treaty between governments. The Paris and London statues were diplomatic gifts. This one was raised by people who had left Hungary. The writer puts the story against the current argument over immigration and assimilation, on the 250th anniversary of the country's birth. It is an essay, not a bill.

Providence says John Adams warned that envy is a threat to property

Thursday's essay says the Founders treated property rights as a condition of the prosperity Americans still live on, and that they also named envy as a threat to that order. The piece argues the warning still applies if the country wants to leave the same arrangement to the next generation. It is an argument from the founding record, not a new statute.

Tech / AI

Estonia's KrattWorks is building a Shahed-like dummy drone for target practice

Defense News reported Friday from Warsaw that KrattWorks is developing an aerial target meant to mimic jet-powered Shahed-type drones so crews can practice shooting them down. Business development manager Ranno Pajuri said a working prototype is expected by the end of 2027, and that the software can move to different hardware more easily than the airframe. The company already sells the DART and DART-R targets and has supplied one-way attack drones and the Ghost Dragon ISR drone to Ukraine. It is promoting the line at AUSA in Washington. A demonstration date is not posted.

Sig Sauer has a 25-round magazine for the Army's next rifle

Defense News reported Thursday from Epping, New Hampshire, that Sig product manager Josh Shoemaker said the company has built a 25-round magazine to replace the 20-round mag that shipped with the M7. The 6.8mm round cut what a soldier carries from about 210 rounds in seven M4 magazines to about 140. Shoemaker said paper targets do not show the difference the Army claims for the heavier round, so troops still count ammunition. The piece says it is not clear whether the new magazine will ship with the M8 when fielding starts. A fielding order is not in the story.
