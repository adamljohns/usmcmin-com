Marines and Japanese explosive-ordnance troops ran a live demolition range on Okinawa during Keen Sword 27

The Marine Corps said Marines with 3rd Explosive Ordnance Disposal Company, 9th Engineer Support Battalion, 3rd Marine Logistics Group, trained with Japan Ground Self-Defense Force members on a bilateral live-fire ground response range on Okinawa on Oct. 23. The range covered safety procedures, thermite grenades, M112 demolition blocks, detonating cord, water charges, and drones, then controlled detonations. Warrant Officer Logan Maar, an EOD diver officer with the company, said the first time the two forces figure out how to work together should not be a real-world crisis. Sgt. Daniel Arroyo, an EOD technician, said the point was for the partner force to see which tools the Marines use and how they employ them. The evolution was part of Keen Sword 27, the routine biennial field exercise with Japan.

Political

Defense Secretary Hegseth fired Joint Staff planner Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia over leak accusations

Defense News reported Thursday that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth fired U.S. Army Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, who led the Joint Staff Directorate for Strategy, Plans, and Policy, over accusations that he leaked classified information. A senior Pentagon official, speaking anonymously, said Sylvia failed three polygraphs about leaking. Chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said the decision followed an investigation by the Air Force Office of Special Investigations and that the matter, along with others, had been referred to the Justice Department. A Joint Staff spokesperson withdrew an initial statement thanking Sylvia for 32 years of service, saying it did not reflect the full circumstances. Sylvia could not be reached for comment. He previously led the 101st Airborne Division.

A Montreal draft would open Galileo talks and Arctic cooperation between Canada and the European Union

Defense News reported Thursday from a two-day summit that opened in Montreal that a draft joint statement would have Canada and the European Union deepen cooperation on satellite services, Arctic maritime capabilities, and critical materials for defense industries. The draft, first reported by Politico, does not announce new troop deployments or weapons purchases. Current wording promises Canada negotiations for access to Europe's Galileo satellite constellation and exploration of a role in the IRIS2 communications project. Language calling Canada the EU's first associate member was dropped in favor of an ever-closer association short of full membership. Prime Minister Mark Carney was quoted as saying the specific name does not matter.

Defense News

The Navy's shore commander told a Tampa forum that bases are still resourced for peacetime

Navy Installations Command said Thursday that Vice Adm. Brad Collins told the Association of Defense Communities Installation Innovation Forum in Tampa, Oct. 26–28, that bases are resourced for peacetime and that degraded infrastructure is already affecting mission requirements in critical areas. Collins said the shore cannot meet 2036 warfighting demands on traditional military construction alone, and named modular construction, other-transaction authorities, intergovernmental support agreements, and enhanced-use leases as alternatives the services are testing. He said installations are piloting facial-recognition gates to cut entrance waits and exploring predictive maintenance from existing facility data. CNIC oversees 10 regions and 71 installations.

Tech / AI

Army researchers tested coatings in Greenland meant to hide vehicles as the Arctic light changes

Defense News reported Thursday that the Army Corps of Engineers Cold Regions Research and Engineering Laboratory and the Army Research Laboratory tested novel coatings during ship-to-shore work at Pituffik, Greenland, as part of a polar-research series with the Office of Naval Research. Dr. Tom Douglas, a research chemist at the Alaska office, said Arctic light, sea ice, and snow can make a fixed camouflage pattern stand out, and that snow looks black in some wavelengths even when it looks white. Anthony Fragoso, the lab's cold-regions artificial-intelligence lead, said the work is aimed at reversible coverings so a unit is not painted white for the Arctic and then sent somewhere else. A rapidly deployable coating is slated for a test on Cold Weather All-Terrain Vehicles in Alaska, with the Alaska National Guard filming from helicopters, including thermal.

Culture

First Things says Aaron Sorkin's Facebook film flopped because the fight was political

A Thursday First Things essay says Aaron Sorkin's film The Social Reckoning, which he wrote and directed from Jeff Horwitz's book Broken Code, cannot decide whether Facebook deleting a post is an editor's choice or a seizure of a printing press. The film follows Horwitz and whistleblower Frances Haugen and charges that Facebook's algorithm steered conservative users toward extremist content and teen girls toward content that worsened their mental health. The essay says audiences did not buy the moral crusade: Sony Pictures pulled the movie from 2,000 theaters after three weeks. The writer argues the fight over Facebook was about politics, and that the side Sorkin treats as an underdog already won the misinformation argument between 2016 and 2024.

First Things

TruthScript says Lula's win and a same-night court order show democracy used as a code word

TruthScript wrote Wednesday that news of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's reelection followed Flávio Bolsonaro's first-round finish on Oct. 4 and endorsements from the other candidates. The essay says Lula's campaign secured a two-hour extension of voting hours, and that minutes after the result Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes ordered Bolsonaro not to leave the country and to turn in his passport. It says Bolsonaro then left for the United States, where his brother already lives in exile, and that the State Department has indicated a visa. The piece treats the sequence as a planned prosecution, not a functioning election, and argues that congratulations from progressive capitals used democracy as a label for the result they wanted.

TruthScript

Providence says a new book treats opposition to Hitler as the West's civil religion, on thin evidence

A Thursday Providence review of Alec Ryrie's The Age of Hitler and How We Will Survive It says the Reformation historian argues that since 1945, and in earnest from the early 1960s, the West's ethical focus has been Hitler and the Nazis, and that the culture's true religion is the Second World War. The reviewer calls the claim the least substantiated in the book. The lectures began as the 2022 Bampton Lectures at Oxford. The review says the argument is really about secularization, present-day populism, and a call for a new civil religion, and that the evidence offered for so broad a claim is thin for a scholar of Ryrie's reputation.
