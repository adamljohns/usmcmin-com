Defense News

The Pentagon's independent inspector general says eliminating fraud and finally passing a clean financial audit rank among the department's most pressing challenges, days after the Defense Department failed its ninth consecutive audit

The Defense Department Inspector General's office released its annual report this week naming seven top hurdles facing the Pentagon as it carries out the National Defense Strategy, ranging from deterring China to financial transparency, according to Defense News. The report lands as the Trump administration asks Congress for a record $1.5 trillion in national defense spending. On the audit itself, 14 of the Pentagon's 25 reporting entities passed their financial statements this year, up from 11 the year before, even as the department failed the audit overall for a ninth straight year; Congress set a 2018 deadline requiring a clean audit by Dec. 31, 2028. Auditors are now using artificial intelligence to process more data from the Pentagon's aging financial systems, and the department has stood up a Joint Task Force-Audit of civilian, military and private-sector experts to keep pushing toward that deadline.

Heritage Foundation

Reviewing a new book on the Supreme Court's 'traditionalist turn,' Law & Liberty disputes the claim that the justices have adopted a free-standing method of constitutional interpretation, arguing they have leaned on history and tradition since the early days of the Republic

Reviewing Catholic University law professor Marc O. DeGirolami's book 'The Constitution of Practice' for Law & Liberty, James R. Rogers writes that DeGirolami's two central claims don't hold up: that the Court only began a distinct, stand-alone 'traditionalist' jurisprudence starting with its 2021 term, and that this shift revives a classical legal tradition rooted in Aristotelian and Thomistic thought. Rogers argues the Court has made substantive appeals to history and tradition since the Republic's earliest years, treating them as one interpretive source among several rather than a tool that can trump constitutional text, and that the modern appeal to 'history and tradition' is a more modest, more positivist move than DeGirolami's framing suggests.

Law & Liberty

Gambling ads once confined to the 'seedy underbelly' of American life now run openly during MLB broadcasts, a TruthScript essay says, pointing to sportsbooks and prediction markets like Kalshi as evidence of a cultural shift

The essay describes a sexualized ad for the betting site Novig appearing during last month's MLB playoffs alongside spots for FanDuel, DraftKings and BetMGM, with broadcasters sprinkling odds into their commentary. It notes gambling was illegal and largely confined to casinos and reservations for roughly two centuries, carrying associations with scandal, addiction and organized crime, before becoming a mainstream part of American life. Kalshi, a 'prediction market' launched in 2021, lets users wager on sports, weather, economic indicators, elections, award shows and even military conflicts. The piece cites the New England Historical Society's account that the Massachusetts Bay Colony's Puritan settlers banned gambling almost as soon as they arrived, arguing that history explains a fence worth keeping up rather than tearing down.

The GOP's historic midterm losses show America is still mid-realignment rather than at its end, a new American Reformer essay argues, warning that Trump's two terms could prove a prelude rather than the destination

The essay, published Monday, argues the Republican Party's historic midterm loss is a signal the country remains in the middle of a political and cultural realignment, not evidence that Republicans are guaranteed to be its eventual beneficiaries. American Reformer

Catholic-Orthodox reunion talks have made more progress than the broader, stalled ecumenical movement, a First Things review of a new book on healing the Great Schism says, even as Russia's invasion of Ukraine complicates the thaw

The review covers Dmitri Solzhenitsyn's 'Reuniting Rome with the Orthodox,' noting that wider efforts at Protestant-mainline church unity have settled into what the piece calls friendly mutual indifference, with no effective way found to draw evangelicals into ecumenical dialogue. Catholic-Orthodox relations are a partial exception: progress has slowed but not for lack of hope, since dialogue over the last 50 years made real headway on the centuries-old disputes over the pope's authority and the Western addition of 'and the Son' to the Nicene Creed's description of the Holy Spirit's procession. New roadblocks appeared after the Soviet Union's collapse, as Eastern European Orthodox churches sought independence from Moscow, and Russia's invasion of Ukraine has deepened the strain. Solzhenitsyn, grandson of Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, wrote the book hoping to help break the broader logjam.
