Office of Naval Research seated the first class of Chief of Naval Research Fellows

The Office of Naval Research said it honored the inaugural Chief of Naval Research Fellows at a ceremony Oct. 15 at headquarters in Arlington. The first national selection drew more than 180 nominations from about 70 campuses. Eight fellows attended, including researchers from Duke, Carnegie Mellon, Rice, and the University of Texas at Austin. Dr. Thomas C. Fu, the executive director performing the duties of Chief of Naval Research, said the partnership is meant to turn discovery into capability for Sailors and Marines. Dr. Reginald Williams, the program officer, said fellows leave with a view of how their work supports the warfighter. The official still, posted today, shows three of them listening to remarks.

Tech / AI

GAO found the Pentagon's responsible-AI office had filled 3 of 10 seats

Defense News reported Wednesday that a Government Accountability Office report published Oct. 14 found the office stood up in 2022 to oversee responsible use of artificial intelligence had filled three of ten authorized positions as of February 2026. GAO said 29 of 64 efforts in the department's Responsible AI pathway were still incomplete as of April. The Responsible AI Working Council did not meet from late 2024 until August 2025, and still lacks a charter. The department agreed with the two recommendations: staff the division, and write the charter.

Defense News

Marine Corps opened Maven to every command, including the reserves

Defense News reported Wednesday that a new license lets every Marine Corps command and organization, reserves included, use the Maven Smart System on any network they are already cleared for. Before the Oct. 1 agreement, access was limited to named commands and to the secret network. The service said about 5,500 Marines used the system in the month before an August summit. Commands are being told to name an implementation lead. A fuller plan is still unpublished.

I Corps commander said the NGC2 fielding clock depends on money

Defense News reported Wednesday that Lt. Gen. Matt McFarlane, commanding general of I Corps, told reporters Oct. 13 that a hope of fielding Next Generation Command and Control across the formation by the end of fiscal 2027 depends on resources he does not control. He said scaling and speed follow what the service can provide. The Army awarded Anduril a contract worth up to $1.8 billion for the common data baseline. The fiscal 2027 request includes $904 million for research, development, and scaling. The Senate has not finished the defense bill.

Culture

American Reformer says the fertility drop is a friendship problem

Wednesday's American Reformer essay by Lyman Stone says the U.S. fertility rate fell from 2.1 children per woman in 2007 to 1.6, and that married couples are not the ones who suddenly stopped having children. Married fertility slipped only slightly. The argument is that collapsing social life is what is making family formation harder. The full essay sits behind a free subscribe wall.
