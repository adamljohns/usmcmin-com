U.S. Government Accountability Office

The Pentagon still cannot reliably account for a $31 billion pool of F-35 parts, the GAO found

The Government Accountability Office found that Defense Department records for roughly 26 million F-35 parts remain outdated, inaccurate and incomplete seven years after watchdogs first identified the problem. About 17,000 parts worth as much as $2.5 billion were absent from the department's property system as of March, contractor data can be more than three months old, and the Joint Program Office still could not establish the status of more than one million parts reported lost from 2018 through 2022. GAO recommended stronger loss-monitoring procedures, trend analysis and F-35-specific training for accountable property officers.

Political

Britain put £90 million behind a drone-and-sensor network to hunt Russian submarines and guard North Atlantic cables

The Royal Navy's Atlantic Net will combine autonomous systems, underwater sensors, communications, remote operations and data services into one surveillance capability. The British government says the system will give submarine-hunting warships and Astute-class submarines earlier warning, help protect the nuclear deterrent and critical undersea infrastructure, and work alongside NATO and Joint Expeditionary Force allies. Hampshire-based Ocean Infinity Solutions is due to lead the framework, supporting nearly 200 skilled jobs.

House Republicans elected Jim Jordan as their minority leader for the next Congress

Judiciary Committee Chairman Jim Jordan of Ohio defeated Louisiana Rep. Steve Scalise 106-73 in a closed-door vote Tuesday afternoon, according to preliminary totals reported by WORLD. Republicans also elected Minnesota Rep. Tom Emmer to remain in leadership as minority whip; Emmer received 103 votes to Texas Rep. August Pfluger's 76.

WORLD

The European Commission withdrew its plan to make hate speech an EU-level crime after member-state resistance

The proposal would have placed hate speech and hate crimes in the same EU category as terrorism, human trafficking, drug trafficking and organized crime. Alliance Defending Freedom International called the withdrawal a major free-expression victory, warning that vague incitement standards can be used against peaceful religious speech. The Commission is considering a narrower proposal focused on online incitement to violence and hatred.

Air Force collaborative combat aircraft flew as a coordinated formation and practiced beyond-visual-range tactics for the first time

Shield AI's Hivemind autonomy software coordinated multiple semi-autonomous aircraft during an Oct. 26 demonstration, though the company did not disclose the number or models involved. The test moved the program beyond single-aircraft milestones toward team tactics and rapid software updates. The Air Force says it aims to field 500 combat-ready collaborative aircraft by 2032 and ultimately build a fleet of 1,000.

Defense News

Anthropic set a $70-to-$78 share range for its planned Nasdaq debut before Thanksgiving

A Tuesday SEC filing says the Claude developer plans to list Class A shares under the symbol ANT and offer roughly 801 million new shares, while existing holders may sell about 24 million more. The filing warns that costs and losses are expected to remain high even as the company projects more customers and improved technology. The planned offering would test public-market demand for a frontier-AI company at extraordinary scale.

Workforce Pell Grants put short job credentials within reach without requiring a four-year degree

The grants are new this academic year and provide thousands of dollars in federal aid, prorated by program length, for short-term credentials tied directly to work. Heritage's Adam Kissel argues that the program can help fill millions of open jobs while giving students who do not finish college a faster route to useful training. He also calls for measuring whether the benefits justify the cost and warns Congress against answering Pell shortfalls with a broad spending increase.

Heritage Foundation

Joyce Carol Oates's attack on Flannery O'Connor mistakes a coherent Catholic life for performance

First Things traces Oates's online dismissal of O'Connor's belief in the Eucharist and her wider claim that O'Connor never meant what she said about faith. The essay argues that the deeper error is projection: a culture accustomed to shifting public poses struggles to recognize integrity when a writer's words and life match. Its practical conclusion is simple and unfashionable—listen closely and take people at their word.
