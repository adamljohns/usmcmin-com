U.S. and Canadian staffs closed a five-day command-post drill in Norfolk

U.S. 2nd Fleet said Navy, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard staff officers from the United States and Canada finished the Frontier Sentinel 2026 command-post exercise on Oct. 30. The drill ran in Norfolk from Oct. 26 to 30. No ships or aircraft deployed. Staff from 2nd Fleet, Coast Guard Atlantic Area, Canada's Joint Task Force Atlantic, and the Canadian Coast Guard worked a simulated Atlantic and Arctic crisis under the Atlantic Maritime Security Coalition. Rear Adm. Mark Behning, acting commander of 2nd Fleet, said sharing maritime awareness early lets the two countries address a crisis before it develops. Royal Canadian Navy Rear Adm. David Mazur, vice commander of 2nd Fleet, called the exercise the place where the staffs build collective muscle memory before a live exercise at sea in the summer of 2027. U.S. Marine Corps Maj. Logan Baisden, the lead planner, said the scenario used simulated undersea threats to find friction in joint processes.

Norway and Germany started building Leopard tanks under the Hansa pact

Defense News reported Friday that German tank maker KNDS and Norway's Ritek began producing Leopard 2A8 tanks in Levanger, Norway, on Oct. 15. The plant will build 37 of the Norwegian Army's 54 new tanks and took 18 months to stand up, with a stated capacity of up to 36 tanks a year. The work sits inside the Hansa Arrangement the two governments signed in February. Norway's Defense Ministry listed the priorities as maritime operations, combined-arms land warfare, rapid reinforcement, defense industry, and space-based surveillance. Both countries plan to operate six identical Type 212CD submarines, with shared training and new maintenance facilities at Haakonsvern, near Bergen. Germany sent about 1,800 troops to Norway's Cold Response exercise in March. Norway said in February that talks were underway on pre-positioning German equipment on Norwegian soil.

Hanwha Philly Shipyard won a $269 million job to build a Marine landing ship

USNI News reported Friday that Hanwha Philly Shipyard won a $269 million contract to build the Marine Corps' Landing Ship Medium. TOTE Services, which won a $2.2 billion contract in July to serve as the vessel construction manager, made the award. The ship is meant to move Marines onto islands and coasts. It is the first Navy vessel the yard has been selected to build under that construction-manager arrangement, and the first U.S. Navy shipbuilding contract for Hanwha Defense USA.

Halloween candy is a way of shrinking death, an essay argues

A Thursday Law & Liberty essay by Itxu Diaz says Halloween, with its sugar-coated dead, shows where death sits in the postmodern West. Diaz cites Bertram S. Puckle's 1926 book on funeral customs and the medieval soul-cake, the precursor of trick-or-treat. He quotes G. K. Chesterton that scattering flowers on a grave is how an ordinary person says what only a great poet can put in words. The essay says Christian culture treated death as terrible and still overcome by the hope of salvation, and that the culture after the Enlightenment put earthly life at the center and made people less willing to keep the dead near the living.

A review says Hanson's book on Trump arrived before the story was finished

WORLD, Tuesday: the review of Victor Davis Hanson's The Counterrevolution says the book was built to mark Trump's return, then events including the war with Iran ran ahead of the pages.
