The Navy's next class of nuclear attack submarines is being designed to be faster and quicker to repair than today's boats, while serving as a mothership for a swarm of undersea drones, the service's submarine requirements chief said this week

The Navy's head of requirements for submarines told reporters in Arlington, Va., that the SSN(X) attack boat, still in the analysis-of-alternatives phase, is meant to be swift and lethal while carrying a constellation of undersea drones into future fights, according to USNI News. The design work is part of the Navy's effort to settle competing tradeoffs for the submarine fleet that will eventually succeed today's Virginia-class boats.

The federal government now pays almost $3 billion a day in interest on its debt, Heritage Foundation argues, and Congress should match the spending discipline the Federal Reserve showed when it held interest rates steady last month

Heritage's commentary notes Washington spends nearly $3 billion daily — about $120 million an hour — just servicing past borrowing, and argues that when the Fed held rates steady on Oct. 28 despite hopes for a cut, it modeled the kind of restraint lawmakers should apply to spending rather than looking to monetary policy alone to fix the debt trajectory.

Moses's instructions on judges, kings, property and the vulnerable get a fresh look at American Reformer, which asks whether Old Testament civil law still binds lawmakers now that Israel's particular covenant, land and priesthood are gone

The piece argues that earlier Protestants resisted choosing between re-establishing Israel's exact political order and discarding its teaching altogether, instead asking which obligations of justice in the Mosaic law endure once Israel's distinct national institutions no longer apply, according to American Reformer.

American Reformer

Secretary of State Marco Rubio's recent speech in Athens invoked 'the West' nineteen times, but a critique at Providence argues he should be defending the universal ideals inside that legacy rather than the culture label itself

Rubio's address last month called for the free world to rearm and compete against rising authoritarianism, but repeatedly praised 'the Western mind' and 'the empire of Christendom' as if those labels carried their own virtue, according to the critique published at Providence. The author argues ideals such as human dignity, natural law and political liberty are worth defending precisely because they are universal truths recognized well beyond Europe and America, not because any one culture owns them, and that treating 'the West' itself as virtuous risks excusing the civilization's worst chapters alongside its best.

Japan agreed to send the Philippines unmanned aerial vehicles and a coastal defense radar system this week, the latest in a string of defense packages meant to strengthen Manila's forces

Japanese Ambassador to the Philippines Katsuro Kitagawa and Philippine Secretary of Foreign Affairs Theresa Lazaro signed the agreement, according to USNI News, continuing Tokyo's pattern of transferring military hardware to bolster the Philippines amid regional tensions.

Italy more than tripled its planned 2026 spending on the next-generation GCAP fighter jet it is building with Britain and Japan, even as funding for its new battle tank program with Rheinmetall came in under forecast

Rome spent €875 million ($970 million) on the GCAP program in 2026, up from €625 million last year and far above the €275 million planners had predicted a year ago, according to newly released Italian defense budget documents reviewed by Defense News. Spending on Italy's new battle tank and fighting-vehicle program with Rheinmetall came in at €177 million, lower than forecast, after Defense Minister Guido Crosetto said in July the program would be cut 18 percent; the budget predicts a sharp rebound in that spending next year.

Culture

Binary thinking in politics — the idea that you're either fully for a cause or against it — is theologically, logically and humanly foolish, a Law & Liberty essay argues, pointing to Mussolini's own 'with us or against us' line as a warning

The essay contends that treating political fights as all-or-nothing, the way Jesus divided mankind into for-Him-or-against-Him, mistakes earthly politics for ultimate reality; it traces the same mistake to Mussolini's 1925 'o con noi o contro di noi' speech in Rome, and argues that issues like immigration involve competing real goods — charity and security both — rather than a single right side, according to Law & Liberty.

Law & Liberty

Staff writer Kim Henderson, known for her profiles of people like Elisabeth Elliot's daughter Valerie and Mississippi's Dobbs-case attorney general Lynn Fitch, says she still hopes to land an interview with House Speaker Mike Johnson or In-N-Out owner Lynsi Snyder

In a staff Q&A at WORLD, Henderson described interviewing subjects in their own settings — workplaces, events, dining room tables — and watching how they react to tough questions while trying to paint as honest a portrait as she can within a finite word count. She named a 2022 interview with Valerie Elliot Shepard, daughter of missionary Elisabeth Elliot, and a dual profile of Mississippi Attorney General Lynn Fitch and former abortionist-turned-pro-life-apologist Beverly McMillan tied to the Dobbs case as two of her most memorable assignments.
