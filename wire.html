Political

Italy's new school rule banning full-face coverings like the burqa and niqab, while still permitting the ordinary hijab, is reviving an old liberal-democratic question of what a state is really doing when it treats a visible religious difference as a barrier to belonging

Providence traces Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government argument that a shared civic life and the practical need to identify students require uniform classroom rules, set against the risk that cultural-uniformity mandates can push unassimilated minorities toward isolated enclaves rather than integration. The piece frames the dispute as a live version of a question every liberal democracy in the West faces: whether a political community can maintain shared commitments without requiring minorities to conform fully to the majority culture's way of life, and where religious liberty draws that line.

Providence

Australia's High Court refused to hear a further appeal in Giggle v. Tickle on Sept. 10, letting stand a ruling that removed women's recognition as a distinct, biologically defined protected category under federal discrimination law

Law & Liberty argues the case exposes a structural weakness unique to Australia's constitutional order: unlike the U.S. or Britain, Australia's framers deliberately left rights out of the founding document, reasoning that rights come from parliamentary bargaining rather than being inherent, which leaves judges 'few interpretive bones on which to gnaw' when a Burkean or textual defense of sex-based categories is needed. With the Full Federal Court's May 15 ruling against women's-platform founder Sall Grover now final, the essay contends that if women's legal recognition as a sex is to be restored in Australia, it will have to come from Parliament rewriting the law, not from the courts.

Law & Liberty

This month's historic Republican midterm losses are a warning that Donald Trump's two terms could end up being only a prelude rather than the foundation of a lasting political realignment, a new commentary argues

The American Reformer piece, written by an editor at The American Mind, argues conservatives should not assume the populist-nationalist coalition that elected Trump twice will automatically outlast him, calling the midterm rout a signal that Republicans need a new generation of leaders able to carry the realignment forward on its own terms rather than ride on Trump's personal appeal. The essay frames the loss as a test of whether the movement built institutions and leaders durable enough to survive a single figure's exit from the stage.

U.S. and Philippine Marines set up a coastal surveillance radar on a Palawan beach facing the South China Sea during a maritime strike-targeting drill earlier this month, part of the countries' ongoing joint exercises in the region

Marines with Marine Rotational Force-Southeast Asia practiced deploying a Ground Based Maritime Systems-Light radar system alongside their Philippine counterparts, USNI News reported, demonstrating the kind of mobile coastal-surveillance capability the two allied forces have been building out together as they continue a rotating schedule of joint drills across the Philippine archipelago facing contested waters in the South China Sea.

Denmark's parliament unanimously ratified a security agreement with the United States over Greenland this week, expanding the U.S. military's role on the Arctic island while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination

The executive agreement, signed in September after President Trump repeatedly threatened to take control of the mineral-rich, self-governing Danish territory, takes effect once Denmark's foreign ministry formally notifies Washington and does not require U.S. Senate approval. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it a modernization of the U.S.-Denmark-Greenland defense partnership, and the deal grants permission to build a 'Golden Dome' missile-defense system and establish two new U.S. military bases, while the two governments now begin drafting a Greenland law to screen outside investors on security grounds. A Greenlandic member of Denmark's parliament, Naaja Nathanielsen, said Trump's pressure campaign had bred lasting distrust toward the U.S. administration among Greenlanders even as the agreement moves forward.

Culture

Sports betting apps and 'prediction market' platforms like Kalshi have moved gambling from the back-room fringe of American life into openly sponsored MLB broadcasts and smartphone notifications, a new commentary argues, and the shift deserves a moral verdict rather than a shrug

The TruthScript piece traces how betting ads from FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM and the sexually charged 'trading' app Novig now saturate sports broadcasts, while Kalshi lets users wager on weather, elections and even military conflicts under the softer label of a 'prediction market.' The author argues that gambling's rebrand as harmless 'trading on your takes' doesn't change its underlying nature, and that Christians who wave off concern as old-fashioned moralizing are ignoring how the industry profits by cultivating compulsive behavior rather than sober stewardship of money.

Pope Leo XIV's apostolic journey to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru — undertaken shortly after surgery to remove a cancerous growth on his lung — leaned heavily on his predecessor Pope Francis's teaching while reading it through a more explicitly Christ-centered lens

First Things reports Leo repeatedly cited Francis's encyclicals Laudato Si' and Fratelli Tutti during the trip, especially in Argentina, but gave pride of place to Francis's inaugural exhortation Evangelii Gaudium and its call to loving encounter with Christ and loving desire to share the gospel. The piece argues Leo develops those Franciscan themes more consistently than Francis himself did, pointing to an address to youth in Buenos Aires where Leo took Francis's signature phrase 'todos, todos, todos' ('everyone, everyone, everyone') and reframed it through a distinctly Christological lens.
