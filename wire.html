Military Times

The Navy confirmed there is no intention to change the name of the future aircraft carrier USS Doris Miller, settling months of speculation, as Newport News Shipbuilding prepares to lay the ceremonial keel for CVN-81 on Dec. 5 in Newport News, Virginia

A spokesperson for the Secretary of the Navy told Military Times there is 'no intention to change the name,' ending a stretch of uncertainty that began in August when a possible pivot toward naming the carrier after President Trump drew pushback from Miller's descendants; his great-nephew Thomas Bledsoe called the idea 'quite a shock' at the time. CVN-81 will be the first U.S. aircraft carrier named for a Black American and the first named for an enlisted sailor — Doris Miller, a Mess Attendant aboard USS West Virginia who manned an anti-aircraft gun without prior gunnery training during the attack on Pearl Harbor and received the Navy Cross from Admiral Chester Nimitz. The fourth Gerald R. Ford-class carrier, originally slated for 2032, is now projected for a February 2034 commissioning after a 15-year build tied to shipyard constraints at HII.

Political

Politics cannot furnish meaning on its own — what it can do is create better conditions for meaning-making institutions like families and churches to flourish, a new Public Discourse essay argues, warning that campaign messaging which casts every election as 'the most consequential of our lifetime' is corroding the republic it claims to save

The essay traces how government's deepening entanglement with big business — Washington is now the most important customer for some of America's largest companies — skews market signals and feeds the lobbying, regulatory capture and corruption that the author says contributed to both the ouster of Hungary's Viktor Orban and the Republican Party's recent midterm losses. Rather than treating that drift as inevitable, the piece argues Americans can moderate an overheated, existentially-pitched politics by deliberately rebuilding the institutions — chiefly family — that give individuals meaning government never can.

A week of historical Thanksgiving sermons and reflections is being republished to help readers 'reconnect to the spirit of the holiday,' opening with 18th-century minister Henry Cumings of the First Parish Church in Billerica, Massachusetts

The editors behind the series frame it as an effort to reinfuse modern Thanksgiving celebrations with the lessons of the nation's forebears, opening with Cumings — a Congregationalist minister whose own Thanksgiving preaching dates to the founding era — rather than a contemporary commentator's take on the holiday.

American Reformer

"It's the economy, stupid" explained the 1992 election, but a Heritage Foundation commentary argues "it's the culture, stupid" is the more apt refrain for understanding this month's Republican losses, pointing to how culture — not just policy — shapes how younger voters in particular see the parties

The piece argues conservatives need rigorous data analysis of the midterm results alongside leaders who can respond to the public with prudence, but contends that focus on economic messaging alone misses why young voters in particular are drifting from the GOP: cultural signaling, not just policy substance, is doing more of the persuading than Republicans have accounted for.

Tech / AI

Ukraine's security service says it used seven Ukrainian-made naval drones to sink the Russian corvette Merkury at anchor in the Libyan port of Benghazi over the weekend, destroying a Ka-27 helicopter aboard and striking a second Russian-linked cargo vessel in the same strike

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed Kyiv carried out the Nov. 22 attack jointly with Ukraine's Defense Forces, releasing video showing explosive-laden uncrewed boats slamming into the moored corvette; Reuters reported ten injuries among Russians and Libyans, and Al Jazeera reported one death. Defense News reports satellite imagery from commercial provider Planet showed the ship ablaze with a long smoke trail, corroborating open-source analysts' conclusion that the vessel was sunk — one of the starkest examples yet of Russia's war with Ukraine spilling into Libya's unresolved civil conflict.

Defense News

China's fourth aircraft carrier, a still-unnamed vessel known so far as just Type 004, is taking shape at a Dalian shipyard and will be bigger than any of Beijing's existing carriers and nuclear-powered, with a hull already about 322 meters long and a 2027 launch expected, defense analysts tell Defense News

The carrier, which could accommodate 75 to 90 aircraft once deployed according to the Asia program director at think tank Defence Priorities, measured roughly 322 meters bow to stern and 46 meters wide as of October — 'far larger than any other class of surface combatant' in China's navy, per the Center for Strategic and International Studies, and slightly bigger than the Fujian, Beijing's current largest carrier. Nanyang Technological University's Collin Koh said aerial photos already show the hull's basic form, pointing to a 2027 launch and five-to-ten-year path to deployment, while Taipei-based analyst Alexander Huang called the specs those of a 'supercarrier' in electronics, tonnage and aircraft capacity; nuclear propulsion would give it more sustained launch power than China's existing electromagnetic catapult system without onboard generators, Koh said.

Culture

"The Vow," a new Christian reality dating series on Fox Nation hosted by longtime "Bachelor" host Chris Harrison, promises viewers "no roses, no elimination ceremonies, no gimmicks" — but WORLD's review finds the premiere leans on the same lingering-camera tactics as its secular predecessor despite courting a more conservative audience

WORLD's critic notes the show opens with 30-year-old bachelor Jace Cates showering and, minutes later, lifting weights, with the camera treatment echoing The Bachelor, the ABC franchise Harrison hosted for 19 years before launching this explicitly faith-oriented alternative. The review frames the premiere as a test of whether a show can genuinely reorient reality dating around Christian courtship values while still relying on some of the genre's familiar visual habits to hold an audience.

Greta Gerwig and Noah Baumbach's little-seen 2015 film "Mistress America" — built around a chaotic Thanksgiving wedding — was years ahead of its cultural moment, a Law & Liberty essay argues, tracing Gerwig's rise from Brooklyn 'mumblecore' obscurity to directing Barbie and Netflix's coming Narnia adaptation

The essay contrasts Gerwig and Baumbach's earlier, art-house instinct for stories about authenticity curdling into poverty and wasted youth — epitomized by 2013's Frances Ha and 2015's Mistress America — with the polished commercial success that followed once Gerwig moved toward Hollywood with Lady Bird, Little Women and Barbie, suggesting the pair's smaller, stranger Thanksgiving-set work still has something to say about family gatherings that modern viewers may be ready to hear again this week.
