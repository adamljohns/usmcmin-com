The Navy is retiring the wool peacoat from sailors' seabags after nearly a century, along with the acrylic V-neck sweater and the dinner dress white jacket

A new Navy uniform policy released Friday removes the peacoat, which had been authorized as optional wear, effective Oct. 1, 2027, according to USNI News; sailors will still be able to wear the wool V-neck sweater. The same policy officially adopts new all-blue physical training uniforms. The peacoat has been part of Navy seabags for close to a century, and its removal closes out one of the service's longest-running uniform traditions.

A Wichita nonprofit started by one man moving in with a homeless friend with autism now pairs adults with intellectual disabilities with live-in mentors to teach daily independence skills

Ben French began helping Dallas, who has autism and intellectual disabilities, after learning he was living in a homeless shelter; French moved in with him in February 2015 and taught him skills like grocery shopping and daily hygiene, according to WORLD. Within about 18 months French built that approach into Shepherd's Way, which now runs with five full-time staff and six live-in mentors serving 18 participants. WORLD reports roughly 5,400 Kansans with intellectual or developmental disabilities are on waiting lists for Medicaid waivers covering housing and job supports, leaving many families to find their own path.

A Missouri church's drive-through live Nativity has run every December since the COVID pandemic, drawing families who say what they remember most is an unruly camel almost going off-script

Doug and Kim Chatman started the drive-through production at Chandler Baptist Church in Liberty, Mo., during the pandemic as a temporary fix until indoor services could resume, but families kept coming back years later, according to WORLD. One early mishap became local legend: a camel named Gus, cast as a wise man's companion, got agitated partway through and had to be wrangled back into position mid-production; calmer camels have since taken his place. The production has continued every year since, run largely by church volunteers.

A British shipbuilder's frigate model sat on a display table at a French defense expo last week as one of roughly six foreign designs competing to become the Navy's newest warship

Babcock International displayed a model of its Type-31 Arrowhead frigate at its booth at Euronaval 2026 outside Paris, one of about half a dozen contenders in the U.S. Navy's foreign frigate competition, according to USNI News. The competition was set up by the White House to jumpstart a new wave of American shipbuilding investment, with foreign-designed hulls intended to get a new frigate class into U.S. production faster than an all-new domestic design.

The Theodore Roosevelt Carrier Strike Group left Singapore last Sunday, continuing the Navy's rotation of major fleet assets through the Western Pacific

The strike group, built around the aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt (CVN-71) and its embarked carrier air wing, departed Singapore to continue operations in the region, according to USNI News' weekly Western Pacific Pulse summary of major ship movements and exercises. Adam Smith's parable of the poor man's son who grows rich only to regret the ambition that cost him his youth gets a fresh look at Law & Liberty, which asks what the next generation should learn from it

The essay, published at Law & Liberty, walks through Smith's account in The Theory of Moral Sentiments of a man who devotes his life to chasing wealth and the comforts he imagines it will bring, only to curse that ambition in old age once he has it. The author calls Smith's account a riddle and argues it is best answered by extending the story to the son's own child, who can learn from her father's mistake without having to repeat it — resolving in one generation what cost the other its ease and tranquility.

It's a Wonderful Life turns 80 this year, and theaters across the country will mark the anniversary with special screenings beginning in mid-December

The film wasn't a box-office hit when it opened Dec. 20, 1946, and drew mixed reviews, but it became a holiday fixture after a missed copyright filing let it enter the public domain in the 1970s, allowing TV networks to air it royalty-free each Christmas for nearly two decades, according to WORLD. Seneca Falls, N.Y. — believed to have inspired the fictional Bedford Falls — will host a weeklong festival, and Hillsdale College fellow Titus Techera told WORLD the film's staying power comes from director Frank Capra's portrait of a community's quiet sacrifices still resonating in a culture with fewer shared holiday classics.

This year's Russell Kirk Lecture at Heritage takes up a question running from Heraclitus to Tolkien: whether imagination is one of the mind's highest ways of knowing, not just a diversion from it

Historian Bradley Birzer delivered the 2026 Russell Kirk Lecture at the Heritage Foundation on the theme "Tolkien, Lewis, Kirk, and Myth," arguing, per Heritage's own description of the talk, that human beings know partly through reason and partly through passion, but that it is the imagination — what Kirk and his circle called the chest, or the heart and soul — that bridges the two. The lecture situates Tolkien's and Lewis's fiction alongside Kirk's conservatism as a shared claim that stories carry real moral knowledge, not merely entertainment.
