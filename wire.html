USS Colorado arrived at HMAS Stirling for a month of Australian maintenance

A Navy release dated today says the Virginia-class fast-attack submarine USS Colorado (SSN 788) arrived at HMAS Stirling to begin a scheduled, monthlong maintenance period. The release calls it the fourth submarine maintenance period in Western Australia since AUKUS, and the third in the last 12 months. More than 30 certified Australian civilian technicians from ASC Pty Ltd. will do routine work on a Virginia-class boat there for the first time. The photo caption dates the arrival Oct. 27 and calls the visit a step toward Submarine Rotational Force-West.

Political

Britain will not join Canada's defense bank, and will keep a separate fund instead

Defense News reported Tuesday that Britain will not join the Canada-led Defence, Security and Resilience Bank. The two governments said Sunday they would set up two institutions that work together rather than one joint bank. They could not agree on headquarters, how much buying would be joint, lending to members, or who can join. Canadian Finance Minister Francois-Philippe Champagne said members will meet in Luxembourg on Nov. 30 to sign the bank's articles. Albania, Belgium, Greece, Latvia, Luxembourg, Romania, Turkey, and Ukraine have pledged support, along with Canada. Germany is an observer and has not joined.

Law & Liberty asks whether France's center can hold after the October looting

A Tuesday Law & Liberty essay by Theodore Dalrymple opens on the Oct. 17 riots he calls a jacquerie. He writes that young men of Muslim background were disproportionately involved, and that French law bars ethnic statistics, so precise figures will not exist. He rejects a straight line from Islam to the looting, then traces family reunification in 1976, a parallel society on the welfare state, and left leaders who treat political violence as sometimes justified. Marseille police estimate 30,000 people looted that city, a figure he says is contested. He closes by saying a considerable share of young French people already think political violence is justified, with 2027 elections ahead.

Law & Liberty

A Law & Liberty review says separation of powers is built for argument, not gridlock

Johnathan O'Neill's Tuesday review of Beyond Checks and Balances says the essays reject both the view that separation of powers exists only to slow government and the view that it is an 18th-century relic. They treat the branches as institutions with different jobs that argue, bargain, and still act. O'Neill notes that Jeffrey K. Tulis calls Trump v. United States a case of judicial arrogance. The review is an argument about how the Constitution works, not a bill or a vote.

Tech / AI

Keen Sword's first week put 56,000 troops on anti-ship drills, F-35 upkeep, and drones

A U.S. Forces Japan release from Tokyo says Keen Sword 27 is about halfway through, with more than 56,000 allied and partner forces already training on the ground, at sea, in the air, in space, and in cyberspace. Japan and the United States ran a large anti-ship drill that tied sea, air, land, space, and cyber units to one inbound threat. At Iwakuni, a Typhoon unmanned surface vessel — about 11 feet, jet-powered, able to hold above 50 knots — ran a naval interception demonstration. Crews also flew a Honey Badger X8 camera drone for surveillance. At four bases, engineers practiced filling runway craters so aircraft can keep flying.

DVIDS

Kadena engineers measured a fake runway crater and practiced filling it the same morning

An Air Force photo package dated Oct. 27 shows Airmen from the 18th and 718th Civil Engineer Squadrons measuring a simulated runway crater during Exercise Beverly Sword 27-1 at Kadena Air Base, Japan. The caption says the measurement tells the crew what the repair will take. A companion frame shows Airman 1st Class Evan Watson of the 18th Civil Engineer Squadron in the same airfield-recovery drill. The point of the exercise is to get a damaged runway back to a condition where aircraft can launch.

USS Canberra made a second 2026 logistics stop in Colombo

A Navy release says the Independence-variant littoral combat ship USS Canberra (LCS 30) arrived at Colombo, Sri Lanka, on Oct. 26 for a scheduled logistics port call. The release says the visit is meant to strengthen ties with the Sri Lanka Navy and regional partners. It is the ship's second Colombo call this year. The release does not list a departure date.

Culture

Providence reviews Biggar's case that Britain's lasting mark on the slave trade was ending it

A Tuesday Providence review of Nigel Biggar's Reparations: Slavery and the Tyranny of Imaginary Guilt says the book makes four claims: British slaving was not unique or uniquely worse, Britain's abolition drive is ignored by the reparations case, payments centuries later flatten history, and the advocates' history is wrong. The reviewer, writing as a complement to Biggar's Colonialism, says Britain spent on the order of 1.8 percent of national income from 1808 to 1867 suppressing the trade. It is a book argument, not a bill.
