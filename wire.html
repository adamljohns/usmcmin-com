USNI News

Growing the Navy's fleet by 112 ships over the next three decades would cost about $234 billion more than the service's own budget estimates, an 18 percent overrun requiring a 41 percent increase in the Navy's annual shipbuilding budget, a new Congressional Budget Office analysis finds

USNI News reports the CBO's review of the Navy's Fiscal Year 2027 shipbuilding plan, released Nov. 19, projects the gap between what the service says its 30-year plan will cost and what CBO estimates it will actually cost, a recurring pattern in CBO's annual reviews of Navy shipbuilding plans. The Navy's plan aims to grow the fleet toward a larger target amid pressure from China's naval buildup, but the CBO's new numbers underscore the scale of the industrial-base and funding challenge facing Newport News Shipbuilding, General Dynamics' yards, and the rest of the naval-construction base as Congress weighs the service's long-term budget requests.

Political

A new book from Cambridge historian Brendan Simms argues great-power rivalry never actually disappeared after the Cold War — it only receded from view during a historically unusual 'unipolar moment' that is now ending

Law & Liberty's review of 'The Return of the Great Powers' says Simms identifies four traits that define a great power — resources, reach, reputation and resilience — and traces how states have competed for security and influence from early European empires through both world wars and the Cold War, with the balance among them never fixed but constantly tested and remade. The review frames the book as a corrective to three decades of Western assumptions that commerce and institutions had permanently displaced great-power competition, arguing today's rivalries with China and Russia are a return to historical norm rather than an aberration.

Law & Liberty

Vice President J.D. Vance is right that the Iran war hurt Republicans in this month's midterms but wrong that President Trump should not have fought it, a new commentary argues, laying the political damage instead on how the war was waged

The Providence piece contends Trump failed to make the case that the war served America's vital interests, overpromised how quickly it would end, and then showed a lack of strategic patience by halting operations too soon — compounding the damage with a June memorandum of understanding that the author says echoed Iranian talking points and would have left the regime's effective control over the Strait of Hormuz intact. The essay argues the war itself was necessary given Iran's four-decade record of attacks on Americans, but that execution, not the decision to fight, is what cost the GOP politically.

Providence

Most contemporary Christian political thought leans on the New Testament's commands to honor rulers and love neighbors, but earlier Protestants refused to abandon Moses and the prophets' detailed instruction on judges, kings, property and the vulnerable, a new essay argues

American Reformer's piece frames the historic Protestant question not as whether to reestablish Old Testament Israel's covenant-specific civil order, but which of its underlying obligations of justice still bind rulers once Israel's distinctive land, priesthood and national-covenant structure are set aside. The essay argues this is the more serious question modern Christians skip past when they treat the New Testament's general commands to honor authority as the whole of what Scripture says about government, leaving Moses' extensive case law on courts and the poor unexamined.

Tech / AI

Ukraine's security service says it used seven naval drones to sink the Russian-operated corvette Merkury and destroy its onboard helicopter at the Libyan port of Benghazi over the weekend, extending the drone war far beyond the Black Sea

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine's SBU carried out the strike jointly with the country's Defense Forces, releasing video of the uncrewed boats slamming into the moored corvette; a Russian dry-cargo ship the SBU says was ferrying weapons to Syria and Libya was targeted in the same operation. Defense News reports commercial satellite imagery reviewed by the outlet showed the Merkury ablaze and later confirmed sunk, while the U.N.'s Libya mission warned the country 'cannot afford' to become a battleground for the Russia-Ukraine war amid Libya's own east-west political split.

Defense News

Kadena Air Base in Okinawa became the first American installation abroad to receive permanently assigned F-15EX Eagle II fighters, with two of the jets touching down Sunday as the Air Force pushes to field more modern airpower in the Pacific

Defense News reports the arrival, confirmed in an Air Force release, continues Kadena's multiyear transition away from its retired F-15C/D fleet, which began drawing down in November 2022 and was bridged by rotational deployments of fourth- and fifth-generation fighters while maintainers trained up on the new jet. The 18th Wing's commander, Brig. Gen. John Gallemore, said the permanent F-15EX presence reinforces the U.S.-Japan alliance and strengthens deterrence in 'one of the most strategically important regions in the world.'

Culture

America's national holidays have drifted into mere occasions for fireworks, cookouts and a big family meal, a new Thanksgiving-week essay argues, and recovering their serious purpose starts with rereading George Washington's original 1789 Thanksgiving Proclamation

Public Discourse argues Washington's proclamation offers a 'useful corrective' to the modern claim that America is simply a secular nation built around individual rights, presenting instead a vision of the country that holds gratitude, divine providence and ordered liberty together rather than treating them as rivals. The piece contends recovering that vision is 'the duty of the good citizen' — a deliberate act of memory required to pass on what the author calls a national blessing to the next generation, set against humanity's natural tendency to trivialize what holidays were built to commemorate.

Public Discourse

Sports betting apps and 'prediction market' platforms like Kalshi have moved gambling from the back-room fringe of American life into openly sponsored MLB broadcasts and smartphone notifications, a new commentary argues, and the shift deserves a moral verdict rather than a shrug

The TruthScript piece traces how betting ads from FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM and the sexually charged 'trading' app Novig now saturate sports broadcasts, while Kalshi lets users wager on weather, elections and even military conflicts under the softer label of a 'prediction market.' The author argues gambling's rebrand as harmless 'trading on your takes' doesn't change its underlying nature, tracing the shift back to the Puritans' own ban on gambling in the Massachusetts Bay Colony as evidence that American Christians once saw clearly what the industry's modern sponsors now obscure.
