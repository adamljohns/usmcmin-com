USS Cole marked 26 years since the Aden bombing at Naval Station Norfolk

DVIDS posted Monday that the crew of USS Cole held a remembrance at the USS Cole Memorial on Naval Station Norfolk. The ceremony included a wreath-laying, a reading of the names with a ceremonial bell, a three-volley rifle salute, and Taps. On Oct. 12, 2000, while the destroyer was refueling in Aden, Yemen, an Al-Qaida boat detonated against the port side at 11:18 a.m. local time, killing 17 Sailors and injuring nearly 40. Cmdr. Chris Gostel, the commanding officer, said the ship's legacy is part of everything the crew does. The release says Cole remains an active warship. It does not say where the ship is assigned now.

Political

Law & Liberty says the Court never required an unbroken tradition for every religious practice

Tuesday's essay argues that some separation advocates want a long, specific history before a religious display can stand, and that the Supreme Court has never said that. It cites Marsh v. Chambers on legislative prayer, Lynch v. Donnelly on a public Nativity, and Kennedy v. Bremerton, which dropped the Lemon test for historical practices. Justice Gorsuch's Shurtleff concurrence, drawing on Michael McConnell, lists founding-era hallmarks of an establishment: control of doctrine, mandated attendance, punishment of dissenters, and public funding of a preferred church. The piece says a practice that does not resemble those hallmarks is presumably constitutional. It is an argument, not a new opinion.

Law & Liberty

Law & Liberty says private False Claims Act suits still prosecute in the government's name

Tuesday's essay says a qui tam relator, with no injury of her own, may sue in the name of the United States and keep up to 30 percent of the recovery. It cites a Justice Department release that relators filed a record 1,297 such suits last fiscal year, and that nearly half of what they recovered came in cases the department declined to join. The Eleventh Circuit held that Clarissa Zafirov, a physician-relator, is not an officer because she holds no continuing position, and sent the Vesting and Take Care questions back down. The piece treats fraud on the Treasury as a public wrong that belongs to the executive. It is an argument, not a statute.

Tech / AI

The Army inducted its first robotics technicians for drone warfare

Defense News reported Monday that the Army formally introduced 10 warrant officers as 390A robotics technicians at the John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School. They are to program, build, repair, and integrate robotic and autonomous systems, and advise commanders at brigade and group level. Chief Warrant Officer 5 Jerry Brown said the skill cannot be an extra hat on an operational detachment. Candidates take a 19-week course and must build drones that do not need GPS or radio to navigate. Vice Chief of Staff Gen. Christopher LaNeve said the Army is scaling the specialty. This is a new job, not a fielded fleet.

Defense News

Lockheed showed a longer PAC-3 interceptor at the Army's annual meeting

Defense News reported Monday that Lockheed Martin debuted the PAC-3 Edge at the Association of the United States Army meeting in Washington. Jason Reynolds, a Lockheed missiles executive, told the paper the missile can launch from the M903, from containerized launchers, and from a HIMARS offense-and-defense configuration. He said extra attitude-control motors and a third pulse, from a Northrop Grumman motor, are meant to add agility and altitude. Lockheed said in January it has a seven-year framework to raise annual PAC-3 production from about 600 to 2,000. Reynolds said Army timing is 2031, and Lockheed wants the missile sooner. This is a company unveiling, not a test shot.

Defense News

The Army's jungle school in Panama is open again, with Panamanian instructors

Defense News reported Monday that the Jungle Operations Training Course, closed in 1999, has resumed as an 18-day class taught with Panamanian forces. Lt. Col. Andrew Eagen, who heads the U.S. center, said nine Panamanian instructors from three branches lead the survival work, and that more than 10 Panamanian students sat the class the reporter attended. He said the Army is holding at least 20 of 120 seats for partners, including 15 for Panamanians, and that Colombian students are the next likely add. Eagen said the course is about the environment, not Panama's security situation. This is a reopened school, not a deployment order.

Culture

Public Discourse says Catholic schools should stop handing every student a screen

Tuesday's essay argues that one-to-one Chromebook and iPad programs are hard to justify once generative tools can write a student's essay. It quotes Pope Leo XIV's encyclical Magnifica Humanitas that every technology shapes the person who uses it, and that education includes teaching when not to use it. The writer says character and spiritual formation both suffer when ease is the default, and that fluency with a tablet is not the same as command of language. The piece is an argument for school leaders, not a diocesan order.

Public Discourse

WORLD says Sorkin's Facebook film cuts January 6 against a hearing that came months later

Thursday's review covers Aaron Sorkin's The Social Reckoning, a follow-on to The Social Network, set around the Facebook Files. WORLD rates it R and in theaters. The review says the film cuts a congressional hearing against January 6 as if the two sat close together, when they were nine months apart, and that the Files ran in September 2021, later than the film implies. It also says the picture shows people refusing masks and vaccines after the algorithm fed them misinformation, and does not show left-wing radicalization. The reviewer writes from a Washington screening where Frances Haugen said the film does not get everything right.
