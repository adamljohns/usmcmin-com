The Marine Corps has set a new minimum fleet requirement for water survival, requiring every Marine to pass a novice qualification built around a 50-meter self-rescue and a timed float, under a policy released earlier this month

USNI News reports the new Water Survival Novice qualification requires Marines to complete a 50-meter self-rescue, demonstrate at least one identifiable swim stroke, and perform a "stay at surface" float, among other tasks, under the policy released earlier in November. The hero photo above, taken at Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton on Oct. 23, shows Marines with Marine Combat Instructor School conducting the Corps' graduated novice-and-competent swim test: Novice Water Survival covers a 25-meter swim assessment, platform entry, the 50-meter self-rescue, a five-minute float with blouse inflation, and a shallow-water gear shed, while Competent Water Survival steps up to a 150-meter self-rescue, a seven-minute water tread with trouser inflation, and shallow- and deep-water gear sheds.

American Christians who want real influence in politics and culture need to stop accepting ready-made media narratives about which evangelicals count as respectable, one case study argues, pointing to the public rehabilitation of former NIH director Francis Collins

American Reformer's essay argues the media and cultural establishment reliably hold up evangelicals who affirm progressive cultural priorities — citing Francis Collins, the former National Institutes of Health director who presided over the government's COVID-19 response and publicly marked Pride Month — as the acceptable face of public faith, while sidelining Christians who push back on the prevailing regime. The Marine Corps is trialing a portable private 5G network meant to keep deployed units in contact with each other even when they are spread across dispersed locations

USNI News reports the system, called the Tactical High-Bandwidth, Low-Latency Minimum Viable Product, or Tactical HiBaLL MVP, is being explored to give Marine units a private 5G network they can carry with them rather than relying on fixed infrastructure, aimed at keeping dispersed elements of a force connected during operations.

The Air Force has bought its final lot of 15 KC-46A Pegasus tankers under Boeing's original 2011 contract, a $2.82 billion deal that closes out the last of 13 production lots priced into that agreement

Defense News reports the Pentagon's Tuesday award runs about $350 million above the prior 15-tanker lot bought a year ago for $2.47 billion, with the Air Force having received 116 KC-46As so far and 203 on contract or in service worldwide, per Boeing. After this lot, the Air Force plans to buy up to 75 more KC-46As under a follow-on program called Tanker Production Extension — toward a total fleet of 263 meant to fully replace its 1950s- and '60s-built KC-135s — though Air Force Vice Chief of Staff Gen. John Lamontagne has said that buy depends on Boeing first fixing known issues with the tanker's refueling boom and Remote Vision System cameras. The KC-46 program has cost Boeing more than $7 billion in losses under its firm-fixed-price contract, including a $565 million loss reported last year.

Culture

Western elite culture now treats flat, no-God materialism as settled fact, but a long history of failed state-enforced 'disenchantments' — from French Revolutionary terror to Soviet and Nazi political religion — suggests the hunger for transcendence always resurfaces, for better or worse

The essay defines disenchantment as the progressive displacement of a worldview admitting spiritual and supernatural realities by a materialist one, tracing it through four historical phases: the Reformation's desacralizing of the material world (accompanied by witch hunts and religious wars), the Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment (which produced new sects alongside rising elite materialism), industrialization (which birthed the Soviet Union's atheistic terror and Nazi political religion as rival 're-enchantments'), and the current post-WWII phase, in which the author says a hegemonic materialist "Ministry of Disenchantment" dominates academia and elite institutions. Drawing on sociologist Max Weber's original coinage, Flannery O'Connor's resistance to her era's secular literary establishment, and critic Ross Douthat's term "Official Knowledge" for elite consensus, the piece argues every disenchantment generates its own 'ersatz' re-enchantment, some benign (religious revivals) and some monstrous (totalitarian political religions), and closes by noting a growing number of dissident writers now challenging the current materialist consensus.

First Things

Apple TV+'s "Way of the Warrior Kid," adapted from former Navy SEAL Jocko Willink's bestselling novel, preaches boys becoming their best selves but settles for a bland, by-the-numbers execution, a new review says

The review follows Marc, an 11-year-old bullied at school, whose recovering Navy SEAL uncle (Chris Pratt) spends a summer putting him through an "Operation Warrior Kid" program built on real SEAL training principles, teaching the boy courage rather than just fighting. WORLD praises the film's premise and cast — including Linda Cardellini as Marc's mother — and its willingness to tell a story about boys needing father figures at a moment of real cultural anxiety over struggling young men, but faults flat, clichéd characters, predictable jokes, and lazy shot composition that leave the film feeling like it is checking boxes rather than honestly exploring a boy's pain in growing up. It is rated PG-13 for bullying, a bus-crash rescue sequence, mild or implied violence, a couple of rude words, and an uncle awkwardly dancing around "the talk."

Thanksgiving's deepest roots run through worship rather than shopping, a new essay argues, tracing the holiday from George Washington's 1789 proclamation back to an earlier 1565 Spanish Catholic Mass and communal meal with the Timucua people in St. Augustine, Florida — 56 years before the Pilgrims shared a similar meal with the Wampanoag

The essay argues Thanksgiving, like Halloween and All Saints, is one of the 'high holy days of fall' meant to embody the natural virtue of piety: thanking God and honoring those who came before, grounded in a Sabbath structure the piece traces back to Genesis and Exodus. It credits Washington as the first president to proclaim a national day of thanksgiving in 1789, with Lincoln setting the current November date in 1863 and Congress enshrining the holiday into law in 1941, but argues the practice runs deeper still, through the 1621 Pilgrim-Wampanoag meal in New England to an earlier 1565 Mass and shared meal between Spanish settlers and the Timucua in St. Augustine, Florida. It closes by contrasting that worship-centered vision with modern Black Friday consumerism, which the author calls "the exact inversion of what Thanksgiving should be."
