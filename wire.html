USNI: Navy unveiled Malice at Tailhook, a 250-mile air-to-air missile

USNI reported Monday that the Navy revealed Malice at the Tailhook Association symposium over the weekend — a long-range air-to-air missile the service says can reach aerial targets 250 nautical miles out. USNI calls it AIM-242. Navy file photos posted Saturday on DVIDS show four AIM-424 LRAAM rounds on an F/A-18E Super Hornet from an April test. The Navy has not said how many are in inventory, and it has not explained why the two public designations do not match.

SOUTHCOM: two killed on an Eastern Pacific boat

U.S. Southern Command said Sunday that Joint Task Force Western Hemisphere, under Gen. Francis L. Donovan, struck a low-profile vessel on established narco-trafficking routes in the Eastern Pacific. The command says intelligence showed the boat was actively involved and that two people it called narco-terrorists were killed. SOUTHCOM did not name them, and it did not say the route is closed. This item cites the command release.

Law & Liberty: Gorsuch’s Slaughter concurrence after Humphrey’s falls

Law & Liberty on Monday walked through Trump v. Slaughter. The Court held the president may remove the FTC’s head because the commission exercises executive power; Chief Justice Roberts’s majority overruled Humphrey’s Executor. Justice Gorsuch wrote alone that the leftover problem is the other half of the bargain — agencies still hold legislative and judicial power, now under the president. That is his concurrence, not the holding, and Congress has not written a statute to match it.

Defense News: two U.S. tankers left Bulgaria on Friday

Defense News reported Monday that two U.S. Air Force KC-135s left Bulgaria’s Bezmer air base Friday, Defense Minister Dimitar Stoyanov told reporters. Parliament approved the temporary deployment July 22. Iran called it complicity; Sofia dismissed the charge. Stoyanov did not announce a new basing deal, and the Pentagon has not issued its own statement on the exit.

Defense News: Italy is eyeing APKWS kits for drones

Defense News, Monday from Rome: the Italian Air Force is weighing U.S. APKWS II kits that turn cheap unguided rockets into laser-guided drone killers. State approved a possible Foreign Military Sale of 5,031 kits plus launchers, warheads, motors, and support — a $364 million potential package. Italian sources told Defense News no contract is signed and the kit is still being evaluated, likely for Eurofighter. The RAF already fielded it. Italy has not bought.

Defense News: Patria signed a Finland drone letter of intent

Defense News, Monday: Finnish Patria and Ukraine’s General Cherry signed a Sunday letter of intent to jointly develop unmanned systems and start drone production in Finland. Patria did not say which models or when the line opens. Cherry says its FPV and interceptor drones are already with more than 200 Ukrainian units. No factory date, no delivery schedule.
