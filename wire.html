Defense News

The Air Force has replaced its 2024 modernization blueprint with a classified Force Design 2026 that puts sensors, networks and artificial intelligence ahead of simply shooting down individual drones

An Air Force spokesperson told Military Times the new Force Design 2026 is an 'enduring framework' for shaping capability development and modernization priorities, replacing the 'One Force Design' guidance signed in October 2024 by then-Chief of Staff Gen. David Allvin, according to Defense News. The document itself stays classified, but the service's public release says the future force will integrate autonomy, sensors and command and control with aircraft to operate in denied environments, shifting the emphasis from intercepting individual drones toward software and networked detection. Air Force Chief of Staff Gen. Ken Wilsbach called it 'a fundamental shift' in the service's approach, and Lt. Gen. Chris Niemi, the deputy chief of staff for force modernization, said new sensor, network and AI technology now has a 'rapid impact' on air defense even as it changes how airpower has historically been generated.

Political

Republicans lost roughly 30 House seats and at least 7 Senate seats in the Nov. 3 midterms, and TruthScript argues the swing shows how far economic pain over gas and grocery prices can move votes even after a presidential win two years earlier

TruthScript's commentary calls the result one of the most lopsided midterm defeats in recent history and argues voters moved back toward the same economic message Democrats ran on in 2024, largely over the cost of living rather than any single scandal. The piece argues American Christians should read the swing as a warning about how quickly public opinion shifts when prices rise, rather than as proof any one explanation — including disputed claims of fraud, which the piece raises but does not substantiate — fully accounts for the result.

TruthScript

Few voters cited foreign policy as their top midterm issue, but Providence argues Ukraine, Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, tariffs and immigration shaped the economic pain that actually drove the Nov. 3 results

Providence's analysis argues that exit polling pointing to the economy, corruption and presidential performance as the top midterm issues obscures how directly foreign policy fed those domestic concerns, and asks whether the outcome will push President Trump's weakened party, or an emboldened Democratic opposition, toward course corrections. The piece opens by invoking Abraham Lincoln's 1862 message to Congress — 'we shall nobly save, or meanly lose, the last best hope of earth' — as a reminder that America's foreign conduct is watched closely by both freedom's friends and foes abroad.

Providence

Marking the Declaration of Independence's 250th anniversary, a Catholic International University keynote argues the Founders appealed to an eternal standard of right and wrong — not English common law or elite opinion — when they declared self-evident truths about human equality and unalienable rights

The adapted keynote, published at Public Discourse, argues the Declaration's claim that truths are 'self-evident' reflects seventeen centuries of Christian moral reasoning that ancient Greeks and Romans would not have recognized, since it assumes human nature is rational nature meant to know and freely pursue God and the good. The essay frames America's founding theology, not just its politics, as the proper lens for the 250th-anniversary commemorations now underway.

Tech / AI

International Criminal Court judges formally closed the part of their Afghanistan investigation that had looked at alleged crimes by U.S. troops, narrowing the case to Taliban conduct since 2021

The decision also closes a probe into the Islamic State Khorasan militia, and comes after the dormant U.S.-troops line of inquiry had sat untouched since 2021, according to Defense News. The prosecutor's office told Reuters it has appealed the ruling. The split follows a year of legal sparring between ICC prosecutors and judges, after the United States imposed institutional sanctions on the court over arrest warrants for Israeli officials and the original Afghanistan probe into U.S. personnel; arrest warrants against four Afghan Taliban leaders remain in effect.

Justice Samuel Alito's new book 'So Ordered' is the subject of a Law & Liberty symposium examining how one of the Supreme Court's leading originalists reckons with the tensions between constitutional theory and the daily work of judging

The symposium, launched Nov. 16, gathers several reviews of Alito's book: one on the difference between judicial duty and citizenship, one on the gap between originalism as theory and judging as practice, and one praising what it calls his combination of jurist, scholar and patriot, according to Law & Liberty. Together the pieces treat Alito's account of his own jurisprudence as a test case for whether originalism holds up under the pressure of real cases.

The books of Samuel bracket Israel's monarchy with two portraits of kingship — Samuel's warning against a king who 'takes, seizes and confiscates,' and David's dying vision of a just king shining 'like the light of the morning' — and First Things argues the virtues the Lord rewards in David are not the ones readers expect

The essay traces David's long season as a fugitive from Saul, hiding in caves and fleeing to Philistine territory despite having done Saul no wrong, as the proving ground for the kind of loyal, patient character that God later calls 'a man after his own heart' — high praise, the piece notes, coming from the divine King himself, according to First Things.

As other newsrooms retreat from original reporting because it is expensive and rarely goes viral, WORLD's year-end appeal argues public discussion increasingly becomes 'arguments about opinions, without much attention to the facts'

In his annual fundraising column, WORLD's Kevin Martin writes that the outlet aims to produce journalism 'grounded in facts and biblical truth' rather than reinforcing the assumptions of the surrounding culture, framing sound original reporting itself as an act of resisting conformity to 'the patterns of this world,' according to WORLD.
