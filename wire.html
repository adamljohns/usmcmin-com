Political

Greenland welcomes a U.S. military pact; trust is still thin

Defense News, Wednesday, citing Reuters: Greenland's government expressed relief that a military pact with the United States and Denmark may end anxiety over threats to seize the island. The agreement lets the U.S. expand its presence, reopening Cold War-era bases at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig. Signed Tuesday at the U.N. General Assembly. Pact, not a takeover.

Defense News

Germany wants to build American PAC-3 interceptors

Defense News, Wednesday: German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius said in Berlin he has asked U.S. defense leaders to allow PAC-3 MSE interceptor production in Germany, arguing another site would ease a global shortfall. Germany's MBDA already makes PAC-2 rounds. Whether PAC-3 follows depends on the U.S. government and Lockheed Martin. Lobby, not a signed license.

North Korea tested a new hypersonic missile

USNI News, Wednesday: North Korea test-launched a new weapon over the weekend that appears to be a hypersonic missile, according to photos released Tuesday by Korean Central News Agency. The Missile Administration Bureau tested the weapon Sunday. State-media claim, not a U.S. assessment.

Tech / AI

Air Force will cut all civilian jobs at its China aerospace institute

Defense News, Wednesday: on Oct. 1 the Air Force will eliminate all 10 civilian positions at the China Aerospace Studies Institute, the Air University center that publishes unclassified research on the PLA's air, space, and missile forces. The service says the mission continues with military members. Director Brendan Mulvaney said the research has always been done by civilians, and that the cut effectively ends the institute. Personnel cut, not a new China policy.

Defense News

Taiwan transferred a coast-guard patrol vessel to the Philippines

USNI News, Wednesday: a decommissioned Taiwan Coast Guard Administration patrol vessel is now under a Philippine flag. The Kinmen, one of four 500-ton Taipei-class ships commissioned between 2001 and 2008, appeared registered as BLING NO.1. USNI called it the most significant recent maritime-security step between Taipei and Manila. Transfer, not a treaty.

Winslett says culture, not just hearings, decides what gets built

Law & Liberty, Wednesday: Gary Winslett argues that NIMBY politics is not only a prisoner's dilemma of self-interest and public hearings. Culture decides whether building is the default or the deviant move, who has standing to object, and whether a new development looks like a contribution or a threat. Forum essay, not a zoning vote.
