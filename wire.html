Brig. Gen. Brown takes command of the 2nd Infantry Division in Korea

Brig. Gen. Robert “Todd” S. Brown assumed command of the 2nd Infantry Division/ROK-U.S. Combined Division on Wednesday at Robertson Field, Camp Humphreys. He is the division’s 90th commanding general, succeeding Maj. Gen. Charles T. Lombardo, who relinquished command on July 24. The formation is the Army’s only forward-deployed combined division, with Republic of Korea and U.S. soldiers in one headquarters. Lt. Gen. Joseph Hilbert, commanding general of Eighth Army, told the formation that readiness is built on ranges, live fires, and combined maneuvers.

Public Discourse asks whether Germany will lift its ban on surrogacy

Wednesday’s essay says a so-called altruistic surrogacy contract can avoid some commercial extremes, including material exploitation and harsh terms. It says the basic problem remains: a child is conceived in the interest of others, and the biological mother is treated as a means to an end. The piece is an argument about a pending German debate, not a vote count.

Law & Liberty says ordinary business owners hold more wealth than the Forbes 400

Wednesday’s review of The Everywhere Millionaire, by Owen Zidar and Eric Zwick, says the authors count households with at least $5 million as roughly the top 4 percent as of 2022, nearly five million households. They put “Main Street Millionaires,” rich owners of private businesses, at about three million, with average wealth around $25 million. Collectively, the review says, those owners hold more than thirteen times the wealth of the Forbes 400.

Heritage says Canada’s assisted-suicide system has claimed more than 100,000 lives

Tuesday’s commentary says that since 2016, assisted suicide has claimed more than 100,000 Canadian lives. It cites a 26-year-old with depression who shopped for a doctor to sign off on his death, and a disabled man who applied for medical assistance in dying because he feared going homeless. The piece treats involuntary euthanasia as the next step in that descent.

Providence says a Chinese house-church leader fled persecution and landed in Texas

Wednesday’s essay says the Chinese Communist Party is persecuting Christians in Texas as well as at home. It names Zhang Mei, a lay leader in house-church networks in Henan province for more than three decades, who fled to Dallas as pressure on independent worship rose and her daughter was already there for college. The piece is an argument about that reach, not a court filing.

Tech / AI

Navy Super Hornets flew with a pair of Blackbeard missiles in a carrier-aviation test

USNI News reported Wednesday that an F/A-18E/F Super Hornet took off from Naval Air Station Patuxent River, Maryland, carrying a pair of Castelion-made missiles the Navy describes as faster than Mach 5. The flight, on Sept. 18, was meant to show the missile can be carried on the fighter. USNI called it a step toward early operational capability for the Navy’s first carrier-aviation hypersonic missile. It was a carriage test, not a combat shot.

Last Air Force reservists and guardsmen in the space cohort swear into the Space Force

DVIDS reported Wednesday that the final group of Air Force Reserve and Air National Guard members in this transfer swore into the Space Force. Col. Andrew S. Menschner, deputy commander of Space Systems Command, administered the oath at Los Angeles Air Force Base on Oct. 1. Col. Melinda Albiston said 250 personnel in space-focused fields began the move in July, and this was the last cohort in that pipeline. The release says the ceremony ends the direct cross-service transfer for this total-force group.

Culture

WORLD reviews John Mark Comer’s case for silence in a noisy church

Wednesday’s review of Into the Quiet (WaterBrook, 304 pages) quotes Comer: this is the first generation for whom it is possible, even normal, to banish quiet from life. He argues the average Christian in the digital age looks for a path to God without time alone and untethered from devices, and that constant noise leaves people hollow, distracted, and frayed. He calls retreat and return the twin pillars of discipleship. It is a book review, not a study finding.
