Defense News

Pentagon says the B-1 bombers at RAF Fairford are back at U.S. home stations

A Pentagon spokesman said Sunday that every U.S. bomber deployed to RAF Fairford has returned to home stations in the United States. A dozen B-1s had been at the Gloucestershire base, used for strikes on Iranian missile sites firing on ships in the Strait of Hormuz. The acknowledgment came a week after arrests at the base on suspicion of preparing terrorist acts. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Wednesday that Britain has strong indications Iran was involved. Tehran called the claim baseless.

Political

Yemen’s recognized government says it has opened a drive to retake Houthi-held ground

President Rashad al-Alimi said Sunday the armed forces would press until the country is out of Houthi hands, and a spokesman called on the Houthis to lay down weapons. The Houthis have held Sanaa and much of the northwest since 2014. Last month they took the Bab el-Mandeb strait and about 90 miles of Red Sea coast. As the announcement landed, the Houthis said they had fired missiles and drones at Saudi Aramco sites in Riyadh and Khurais. Saudi Arabia had not confirmed that claim.

Defense News

Eisenhower is headed back to Norfolk after a Growler mishap injured four sailors

USNI News reported Saturday that aircraft carrier USS Dwight D. Eisenhower (CVN-69) is returning to Naval Station Norfolk after a Sept. 28 incident that injured four sailors and lost an EA-18G Growler. The return is so the Navy can look at the ship’s aircraft launch and recovery gear.

USNI News

Pentagon restates a Pacific denial line and the munitions it would take

USNI News reported Friday that the Pentagon restated plans for a forward area-denial network along the First Island Chain to deter China. In a Sept. 22 report to the Senate Armed Services Committee, a defense under secretary said U.S. forces are working on hardened command and control, logistics under fire, and magazine depth for that posture.

Law & Liberty says a police-abolition push is back in the platform, not the slogan

Monday’s essay notes that Democratic Socialists of America recently swapped a call for immediate abolition for language that still aims at fully abolishing police and prisons, step by step. It treats equal neighborhood safety as a duty of government, and abolition as a widening of the safety gap rather than a reform.

Tech / AI

Ukraine’s Fire Point says its FP-7 ballistic missile has been used in combat

Fire Point chief executive Iryna Terekh told the Helsinki Security Forum that the company launched the tactical ballistic missile onto Russian territory on Oct. 1, with a stated range of 300 kilometers, and is working on a longer-range version this year. She said the flagship missile is meant for the pan-European Freyja missile-defense program, with a prototype expected in the first half of 2027.

Law & Liberty asks what is left of the doctor if an algorithm is the better diagnostician

Monday’s essay takes a JAMA argument that AI could soon outperform physicians at much of medicine’s cognitive work, and that putting a doctor back into those decisions could make care worse. It grants the coordination case, then asks what the patient loses when the person who treats him is no longer the one who decides.

Public Discourse makes the case for waiting before a new device enters the house

Monday’s essay argues that most households cannot drop off the grid, but they can wait before buying the next device. The writer treats those small delays as a way to use technology instead of being used by it, and to count the loss that comes with the gain.
