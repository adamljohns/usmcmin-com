The carrier USS George Washington left the Middle East after three months on station, with the U.S. Pacific Fleet confirming the ship has moved into the Navy's 7th Fleet area

A U.S. Pacific Fleet spokesperson confirmed to USNI News on Wednesday that USS George Washington (CVN-73) left the Middle East after three months on station there and is now in the 7th Fleet area of operations. The spokesperson did not provide further details, including where the carrier and its strike group head next. George Washington had been operating in the 5th Fleet area supporting maritime security in the Middle East; a DVIDS release from mid-October shows the carrier conducting routine operations in the Arabian Sea during that deployment.

Political

States chasing lower card swipe fees could push banks to simply relocate the regulated part of their business to friendlier states, a Heritage commentary warns, using Illinois' new interchange-fee law as the test case

The commentary says card swipe fees draw complaints from merchants, who resent the cut taken by card-issuing banks and payment networks on every sale, and from consumers, who resent checkout surcharges for paying by credit card. Under that pressure, states have begun tiptoeing toward regulating interchange fees, with Illinois' law cited as the leading example Heritage warns could simply drive banks to open a technical branch in a friendlier state to route around it.

Heritage Foundation

Social Security's funding gap is a moral problem between generations, not just an actuarial one, a Law & Liberty essay argues, proposing a Burke-inspired 'cohort separation' to protect current retirees while moving younger workers toward owned retirement accounts

Drawing on Edmund Burke's idea of society as a partnership between the living, the dead and the unborn, the essay argues Social Security's Old Age and Survivors Insurance program has let politicians promise benefits while passing the costs to younger and future taxpayers, with the trust fund representing claims on Treasury notes rather than real assets. With benefits projected to be depleted by 2032, the author proposes phasing in change by birth year: protecting retirees and near-retirees under the current system while moving younger workers toward funded, privately owned accounts, financed transparently rather than through more borrowing.

Law & Liberty

Italy's new ban on full-face coverings in schools raises a sharper question than one country's integration policy, a Providence essay argues — whether a state can demand minorities erase religious difference as the price of belonging

The essay examines Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government decree barring full-face coverings, including the burqa and niqab, from school activities, noting even a Milan math teacher told Al Jazeera she had never encountered a student wearing one. Drawing on Reinhold Niebuhr's warning that groups mistake their own interests for universal principles, and on B.R. Ambedkar's writing on fraternity and citizenship, the piece argues governments may set legitimate institutional rules, such as requiring faces visible for identification, but cross a line when cultural conformity itself becomes the test of equal citizenship. It closes by invoking the First Amendment's balance of free exercise and non-establishment as a model for sustaining shared civic life without requiring religious minorities to erase their differences.

Senate appropriators cut nearly $20 billion from the Navy's shipbuilding request, pushing back on several high-profile programs in their draft of the Fiscal Year 2027 defense spending bill

USNI News reports Senate appropriators allotted $41.6 billion for the Navy's shipbuilding account in their FY2027 draft, a reduction of roughly $20 billion from what the service requested, with cuts touching several high-profile shipbuilding programs.

USNI News

The Air Force is stepping up training on America's westernmost islands, from a rare alert exercise on Alaska's Shemya Island to a study of expanded flight operations in the Northern Mariana Islands, as it looks to spread out across the Pacific

Defense News reports F-16s, tankers and cargo planes deployed to Eareckson Air Station on Shemya Island from Oct. 18-26, where aircraft sat alert for the first time in decades, closing with a U.S.-Canada homeland-defense exercise. Separately, the Air Force released a proposal to study up to 1,500-1,800 annual aircraft operations at airfields on Rota, Saipan and Tinian in the Northern Mariana Islands, including refurbishing Tinian's WWII-era North Field, as part of its Agile Combat Employment concept meant to disperse forces and complicate Chinese targeting. A Stimson Center analyst said the Shemya work is mainly about homeland defense and intercepting Russian aircraft, while the Marianas investment is about reducing reliance on Guam's Andersen Air Base.

Culture

A new Museum of the Bible gallery traces how artists from the 1493 Nuremberg Chronicle to Andy Warhol and Salvador Dali have depicted the Annunciation, the moment the angel Gabriel told Mary she would conceive the Son of God

WORLD reports the Bowden Gallery of Biblical Art's first exhibit, 'The Annunciation: Picturing the Incarnation of Christ,' displays 22 artifacts ranging from a postcard-sized 1493 illustration to Bruce Herman's 2006 gold-leafed panel 'Overshadowed.' None of the pieces are icons made for venerating Mary; curator Amy Van Dyke says they are illustrations of one of the Bible's most mysterious moments, including a Delft tile that once adorned a Calvinist Dutch fireplace and a life-sized installation by artist Theodore Prescott. The exhibit opened Nov. 16 and runs six months, through the Feast of the Annunciation on April 5, 2027.

WORLD

The pro-life movement is grievously wounded but not dead after the midterm losses, a Public Discourse essay argues, saying the path back runs through showing a better way to live rather than through politics alone

The essay argues the pro-life movement's setbacks predate the midterms and have been compounded by a White House it says has been an unreliable ally on abortion policy. It warns newly elected state legislators will move to repeal unborn-child protections and that federal backstops — the filibuster, the presidential veto, an originalist Supreme Court — are all precarious. Its conclusion is that durable pro-life recovery will come less from relying on any one politician and more from the movement proclaiming and modeling a better way of life rooted in self-giving love.
