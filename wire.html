Alaska Guard Black Hawk crew pulls two stranded boaters from north of the Arctic Circle

Alaska Army National Guard aviators with the 1-168th General Support Aviation Battalion rescued two overdue boaters on Oct. 6 whose vessel was stuck in icy water about 7 miles north of the Arctic Circle. The Alaska Rescue Coordination Center opened the mission after a request from the Alaska State Troopers. A Nome-based UH-60L Black Hawk, with two Nome Volunteer Fire Department emergency medical technicians aboard, found the pair and their dog at a cabin about 75 miles east of Kotzebue, landed on the tundra, and flew them to Kotzebue, where they were released to the Troopers. Capt. Jared Scheler, one of the pilots, said the crew brought the medics because the ages and condition of the two people were unknown.

Public Discourse proposes forcing sportsbooks to take bets from skilled gamblers

Thursday’s essay says online sports gambling is draining young men, and that politicians are reluctant to crack down because millions of voters place bets. The proposed fix is a congressional rule that sportsbooks treat every customer the same. The piece says books already cap bets from people who can spot a bad line, and that equal treatment would cut into the margin that keeps the business open. It is an argument, not a bill.

Law & Liberty reviews Frederick Douglass’s turn toward the Constitution

Thursday’s review of Dennis Rasmussen’s Frederick Douglass’s American Founding says Douglass moved from William Lloyd Garrison’s view that the Constitution was a slaver’s prison to the claim that it was an abolitionist charter. John O. McGinnis writes that both poles miss the document: it was a compromise that built national institutions able to isolate slavery. The piece is a book review, not a court opinion.

Tech / AI

Army’s lighter M8 rifle is set to reach Fort Bragg units in December

Defense News reported Tuesday that Sig Sauer executives said fielding of the M8 carbine begins in December with some units at Fort Bragg, North Carolina, less than three years after the M7 started reaching soldiers. Dave Buehler, the company’s vice president of manufacturing, said the M250 machine gun, meant to replace the M249, is expected in the first half of next year. Both weapons use the 6.8×51mm Common Cartridge. Josh Shoemaker, Sig’s defense product manager, said the M8 is more than a pound lighter than the M7 and has a barrel 3.5 inches shorter, with a fixed stock in place of the folding one. The figures come from the company, not an Army field report.

Pentagon names four directed-energy weapons for a base counter-drone pilot

Defense News reported Tuesday that the Defense Department named four systems for a homeland counter-drone pilot ordered by Congress: AeroVironment’s Palletized High-Energy Laser, Epirus’s Leonidas high-powered microwave, Kord’s Firefly high-energy laser, and Boeing’s Compact Laser Weapon System. Joint Interagency Task Force 401, the Army-led office stood up last year, will run the pilot. Brig. Gen. Matt Ross, the task force director, said the test is meant to show how the weapons fit a layered defense and what it costs to keep them running. It is a selection for a pilot, not a fielding order.

First Things says a machine that copies a person is still not a person

Thursday’s essay says the usual defense of machine consciousness is functionalist: if a system can imitate the products of human thought, there is no reason to treat it as different. It cites Pope Leo XIV’s recent line that there is an “ontological difference” between the work of human artists and generative systems, and treats that line as a challenge to the imitation test. The piece is an argument about what a person is, not a lab result.
