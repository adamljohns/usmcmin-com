State Department approves selling Ukraine 500 Patriot interceptors for $2.1 billion

The Oct. 30 notice to Congress covers PAC-3 MSE missiles, spare parts and support. It says no U.S. funds are involved: Canada, Germany and Norway are donating through the JUMPSTART program, and the rest comes from a European Union loan. Two people briefed on the decision told Defense News the missiles have yet to be built and are not expected until a couple of years into the 2030s. One of them said Ukraine's defense ministry is pressing the Pentagon to move that up, possibly by giving Kyiv missiles already in the U.S. arsenal.

Virginia's polls open at 6 a.m. Tuesday on three constitutional amendments, a U.S. Senate seat and 11 House seats

The Department of Elections lists Sen. Mark Warner, a Democrat, against Republican Bert Mizusawa, and Rep. Rob Wittman, a Republican, against Democrat Shannon Taylor in the 1st District. Question 1 would protect decisions on abortion, birth control and fertility care while allowing third-trimester restrictions with exceptions. Question 2 would remove the state ban on same-sex marriage. Question 3 would automatically restore voting rights on release from incarceration. Early in-person voting ended Oct. 31. Polls close at 7 p.m., and anyone in line at 7 may vote.

New password-stealing malware hands its loot to AI models to rank what each victim is worth

Cisco Talos researchers say the Windows malware, which names itself VOLTA-AUTODOXER, takes passwords, cookies, crypto wallet seed phrases and cloud keys, then builds a file on the victim with home address and employer. It sends that file to an AI model and asks for a target assessment that ranks the valuables and grades the victim's security habits. If a model refuses, the malware passes the job down a chain of up to three models.

Russia is fitting drones with bigger antennas that are harder for Ukraine to jam

Ukraine's military intelligence agency says Kometa navigation modules with 16-element arrays are in use at scale and 20-element arrays are headed for serial production. Serhii Beskrestnov, an adviser to President Zelenskyy, says Shaheds with 32 elements have attacked Kyiv, and that adding elements is easier than adding jamming sites. Drones that get through have to be shot down with costlier weapons. Defense leaders say jamming more bands at once or feeding false coordinates can still defeat them.

First Things: Two massacres of Christians in one week went almost unnoticed

The essay opens on nine Christians killed at a funeral procession at St. George's Orthodox Church in Jordan and sixty killed by gunmen in Ethiopia, and faults major papers for barely noting the religious motive. It leans on a new Aid to the Church in Need report, which puts about 220 million Christians directly exposed to persecution and counts harassment in 165 countries in 2023, up from 160 in 2021.

American Reformer: Will anyone sing our hymns in 2126?

The essay says cultural Christianity is making a comeback and asks what hymns, churches, art, furniture or books future Christians will inherit. It argues little from the current era is good enough to hand down to great-great-grandchildren, and that much has to be built from the ground up.
